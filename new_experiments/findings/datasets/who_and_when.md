## 1. Data as loaded

- 184 records (58 Hand-Crafted Magentic-One, 126 Algorithm-Generated CaptainAgent), 4,092 steps; one labelled step per record (`mistake_step`, 0-based into `history`), `error_agent` = `mistake_agent`, `reason` = `mistake_reason`; `mistake_type` on 4 records only. All outcomes `fail` by construction.
- Hand-Crafted logs are the raw Magentic-One console: each orchestrator turn is 2–3 steps (`Updated Ledger` JSON thought → `Orchestrator (-> X)` instruction (same text) → `Next speaker X` line); `Stalled.... Replanning...` / `New plan:` steps; the final step carries `FINAL ANSWER:` and the scenario footer. **Specialist turns are missing** in 22/58 logs (37 events): an `Orchestrator (-> WebSurfer)` instruction is followed by another orchestrator step (the next ledger typically says "the web results were interrupted by a timeout error") — the erroring WebSurfer turn was never written to the log (e.g. HC/2 step 7→8, HC/9 7→8 and 45→46). The attributor therefore cannot see some tool failures at all.
- Algorithm-Generated logs start with the CaptainAgent *nested-chat prompt* ("You are given: (1) a task and advises from your manager with a specific plan…") attributed to the first Expert as step 0, although it is authored by the (unlogged) manager; the manager/captain never appears as an agent.
- Final answers: HC 56/58 contain `FINAL ANSWER:`; AG logs end with `TERMINATE` after a verifier message (6 contain `FINAL ANSWER`).
- Overlaps resolved: 44/44 AgentRx Magentic-One logs → HC files (40 by exact question+length, 4 by whitespace-tolerant match: HC/24, 45, 49, 51); 177/289 MP-Bench logs → WW files via `log_source` URL (step lists identical).

## 3. Targeted statistics (`results/who_and_when/stats.txt`, `overlap.txt`)

**3.1 Right answer, failed label.** 3/184 runs end with an answer equivalent to the ground truth (HC/2 `CSI Cyber`; AG/82 `17,000 hours` vs `17`; AG/79 `shrimp and grits` vs `shrimp`). Small (1.6%) but each carries a confident, wrong `mistake_reason`.

**3.2 How HC runs end vs where the label is.** 13/58 HC runs end by budget exhaustion (`Max time` 4, `Max rounds (30)` 6, `Replan counter exceeded` 3), 18 by `Request satisfied`, 27 by a final answer after a crash/other footer. In the 13 exhausted runs the label is a WebSurfer search step (7: rank 0–8 among 25–27 WebSurfer steps) or a Stalled/replan step (HC/19, /9) — never the last agent action. Label type over all 58: WebSurfer 32, Orchestrator ledger 5, Orchestrator instruction 6, Orchestrator stall 3, plan 2, Assistant 5, FileSurfer 4.

**3.3 Label vs the blamed agent's own history.** Label is the blamed agent's *first* step in 15/58 HC and 67/126 AG; its *last* step in 13/58 HC and 42/126 AG; the first WebSurfer step in 10/31 WebSurfer-blamed HC logs. So HC labels cluster on "first action of the blamed agent" (earliest-detectable) rather than on the last action before the wrong answer.

**3.4 Orchestrator stall signals are late symptoms.** 36/58 HC logs contain a ledger `is_in_loop=true` or `is_progress_being_made=false`; the first such flag is *after* the label in 22 and before in 14. `Stalled.... Replanning...` occurs in 25 logs and precedes the label in only 2. Repeated WebSurfer actions (same first line) occur in 29 logs; the first repeat is after the label in 19, before in 10. Error-string density is 0.047/step before the label vs 0.066/step after (HC); 0.198 vs 0.220 (AG). Conclusion: the system's own loop/stall flags and repeats follow the label by a median of several steps (P1 confirmed), but they are weak (error strings are only 1.4× denser after the label).

**3.5 Missing turns.** 22 HC logs / 37 events where the agent named by `Orchestrator (-> X)` never speaks (§1); the next ledger usually mentions a timeout. In HC/9 the missing turn is the one before the labelled stall.

**3.6 Ground-truth visibility.** The GT string appears in an agent step before the label in 5 HC logs (HC/9, 16, 34, 49, 57) and 3 AG logs; never in 41 HC / 84 AG. "Answer seen but not extracted" is ~10% of HC.

**3.7 Label reliability on the overlaps.**
- *vs AgentRx (44 HC logs)*: root == WW step in 16 (36%); within ±1 in 21, ±3 in 25; AgentRx later in 16, earlier in 12 (median signed 0); WW step is inside AgentRx's full failure set in 27/44; the two "first failure" definitions coincide in 15; blamed agent agrees in 27/44. Disagreements of 20+ steps: HC/3 (32 vs 55), HC/8 (8 vs 40), HC/20 (3 vs 32), HC/21 (4 vs 24), HC/30 (82 vs 12), HC/46 (32 vs 4), HC/44 (39 vs 31).
- *vs MP-Bench (57 HC logs)*: WW step gets **0 of 3 votes in 26 logs**, 1 vote in 12, 2 in 6, 3 in 13; is the earliest-majority step in 9; any vote within ±1 in 42, majority within ±1 in 32; no vote within ±3 in 11 logs. Agent agreement 23/57. WW `Orchestrator (-> X)` instruction labels get 0 votes in 6/6 because MP-Bench flags the preceding ledger with the same text.
- *vs MP-Bench (120 AG logs)*: WW step gets ≥2 votes in 116, 3 votes in 74, equals the earliest-majority step in 91; a strictly earlier majority step exists in 28 (omission-type steps: "asked for manual lookup", "did not run code").
- So: **AG labels are reproducible (≈75% unanimous), HC labels are not (≈23% unanimous, 46% unflagged)** — the benchmark's step accuracy on HC (≤7% for every method) is measured against labels that other experts do not reproduce.

**3.8 Prefix probe (local detectability).** `python3 -I inspect/prefix_probe.py who_and_when --n 20 --max-steps 40 --workers 4` (20 records ≤ 40 steps, 216 agent-step probes) — see §3.8 addendum.

## 4. Baseline error analysis (`results/who_and_when/all_at_once_std.jsonl`, 184 records, Qwen3.8-27B thinking, GT shown)

| Hand-Crafted | 58 | 63.8 | **31.0** | 44.8 | 15 / 20 / 18 (53) | 0 |
| Algorithm-Generated | 126 | 62.7 | 48.4 | 78.6 | 26 / 33 / 61 (120) | 0 |
| all | 184 | 63.0 | 42.9 | 67.9 | — | — |

Categories: exact 79, earlier −1..3 32, earlier <−3 21, later +1..3 14, later >3 27, truncated (thinking overran 16k) 8, parse fail 3 (one is HC/9, where the thinking degenerates into a repeating loop "born May 1975? maybe …"). Step accuracy is far above the paper's GPT-4o All-at-Once (12.5 / 5.3) — partly the model, partly because the prompt frames the target as "decisive, not symptom".

**No late bias on this dataset with this prompt.** HC: 20 earlier vs 15 later, median signed offset 0; predicted relative position (median 0.29) is *earlier* than the gold (0.33). The "later(>3)" cases (14 in HC) are not final-answer picks (1/14 in the last three steps); 5/14 are after the first `Stalled` step. P1's "judges localise late" is **not confirmed** for a prompted all-at-once judge that is told to find the earliest decisive step; the errors are split both ways.

**Which trajectories the model handles.** Short, single-commission failures: HC/6 (accepts the office-building price → step 5, exact), HC/24 (translation in the plan → 1, exact), HC/12 (Assistant fabricates the domestic list → 16, exact; the model re-derives the overlap count 6 itself), AG/3 (fabricated numbers → 4, exact). Non-exhaustion HC runs: 15/40 exact; exhaustion runs: 3/13 exact, 5/13 within ±3. By length: ≤10 steps 50% exact, 11–30 32%, >30 21%.

**Which it cannot.** (i) *Stop-rule labels on exhaustion runs*: HC/2 → pred 25 ("the Orchestrator … omitted the required Rotten Tomatoes and Amazon Prime checks", a defensible omission step; gold 4 is the first search); HC/46 → pred 20 (the "first dedicated search" with a narrow query; gold 32 is the 7th search); HC/19 → 17 (an instruction to try LinkedIn; gold 50 is the replan). The model picks an omission at the point where the plan went wrong; the annotator picks an early action or the replan. Both are guesses on runs that have no decisive step. (ii) *Answer-origin tracing*: HC/30 → 8, "the $445,000 unit … introduced the irrelevant value that later became the final answer" — the model traces the wrong final answer backward to the step where the number first appeared (step 8), 74 steps before the gold (82) and 4 before AgentRx's 12. Backward answer tracing is a strong, explicit heuristic in the reasoning, and it is *earlier*-biased. (iii) *Convention gaming*: on HC/19 the thinking says "They might have a human annotator … they'd likely point to step 18 … or 17 because it's the ledger. The difference is one step. Which is safer?" — the model spends reasoning on the ledger/instruction step-identity ambiguity (§5.2). (iv) *Format-only failures*: AG/82 → 1 (CelestialPhysics_Expert computed 17000 instead of 17) — the model finds the real (format) issue and blames its first occurrence, the gold blames a later verifier with a wrong reason. (v) *Long trajectories*: 8 truncations are all HC logs with ≥ 80 steps (16k thinking tokens exhausted).

**Agreement with the other annotation teams.** On the 44 AgentRx logs the prediction equals the WW step in 15 and AgentRx's root in 11, lies inside AgentRx's full failure set in 22 and within ±1 of the AgentRx root in 21. On the 57 MP-Bench HC logs the prediction carries ≥ 2 votes in 20 and 3 votes in 15 — **more often than the WW gold step itself (≥ 2 votes in 17)**. On the 120 AG logs the prediction has ≥ 2 votes in 91 (76%) although it equals WW in only 58; 14 of the "earlier" misses are majority-flagged omission steps. So roughly half of the HC "errors" and most of the AG "errors" are steps that other expert annotators endorse; the single-label step accuracy understates the judge by ≈ 20 pp on AG and is close to noise on HC.

**Step-0 AG labels**: 17/19 parsed exactly (the model recognises the wrong task/plan in the nested-chat prompt) — these are easy, not hard, for an all-at-once judge; they would be impossible for a prefix monitor that only scores agent decisions.

## 5. Annotation issues (prevalence)

1. **Wrong reason / wrong failure (1.6%)**: AG/82 (reason contradicts the log), HC/2 (correct answer), AG/79 (over-specified correct answer).
2. **Content-free label steps (HC ~16%)**: 3 `Stalled.... Replanning...` lines, 6 `Orchestrator (-> X)` duplicates of the ledger — the labelled step has no text of its own or duplicates the previous step. ±1 tolerance is required merely to make the ledger/instruction/next-speaker triple a single decision.
3. **Budget-exhaustion runs (HC 22%)**: 13 runs end at max time/rounds/replans. Their labels are a first search (7) or a replan (2): the annotator applies a stop rule ("earliest step that could have been better"), not a decisive-step criterion. These are the logs where WW, AgentRx and MP-Bench disagree most (HC/2, 8, 19, 46, 9, 30).
4. **Step-0 labels in AG (20/126 = 16%)**: the error is in the manager's task/plan (AG/19) or the first message merges prompt and code (AG/52); the "agent" at step 0 is the nested-chat prompt author, not the named expert. The blamed agent is thus partly arbitrary.
5. **Casing / naming**: `Websurfer` vs `WebSurfer` (HC/11, 13, 20) breaks naive agent matching (loader keeps the raw string; the baseline's `norm_agent` handles it).
6. **Missing specialist turns (HC 38% of logs)**: tool errors are invisible; the ledger's "timeout" mention is the only trace.

## 6. Candidate hypotheses

**H-W1 "Stop-rule labels": on long, budget-exhausted Magentic-One runs the label is the earliest step at which an expert *could* have done better, which is typically the first or second action of the blamed agent; there is no decisive step and labels from different annotation teams do not coincide.** Evidence: §3.2–3.3, §3.7 (0 MP-Bench votes on 26/57 WW-HC steps; AgentRx exact 36%); HC/2, /19, /46. Test: on the 13 exhausted runs, ask three fresh annotators (or three models) for "the step whose correction would have made the run succeed" vs "the earliest mistake" and measure agreement separately; prediction: agreement on the latter ≈ 40%, on the former ≈ 15%, and both are below AG agreement (≈ 75%). Shared with MP-Bench manual, AgentRx Magentic-One, SymTrace C2. A targeted method should first classify the run (decisive-mistake run vs exhaustion run) and, for exhaustion runs, output the *first repeated-action episode* rather than a single step — i.e. switch the target from a step to an episode.

**H-W2 "Ledger lag": Magentic-One's own loop/stall detector fires a median of several steps after the labelled step (first flag after label in 22/36 logs; Stalled after label in 23/25), so a scorer that keys on the orchestrator's self-diagnosis localises late; but the *first instruction that repeats an earlier instruction* (the ledger text, not the flag) is closer to the label.** Test: compute, per log, the first step whose `instruction_or_question` has cosine similarity > 0.9 to an earlier instruction and compare its distance to the label vs the distance of the first `is_in_loop=true` flag. Shared with AgentRx (orchestrator "recovers" silently: 18/24 recovered WebSurfer failures are followed by a ledger that still says progress is being made). A targeted method would use *instruction repetition* as the early signal and ledger flags as confirmation.

**H-W3 "Hidden-turn failures": in 38% of HC logs the step that contains the error signal (a WebSurfer timeout) is absent from the log, and the label lands on the nearest visible step (the preceding instruction or the following stall).** Test: in the 22 logs with missing turns, measure the distance between the label and the nearest missing-turn position vs a random position. If close, the label marks a hole in the log rather than a decision; a method should treat `-> X` followed by non-X as an implicit tool-error step and allow it as an answer.

**H-W4 "AG step-0 is a task-framing failure outside the log"**: 20/126 AG labels at step 0 blame the expert for the manager's plan. Test: compare the step-0 nested-chat task text with the original question (AG/19 is `exitcode: 1` instead of the grocery question); count how many step-0 labels have a task/plan mismatch. A method should model the (unlogged) captain as an agent and allow "before step 0" as a prediction.

**H-W5 "Evidence gap → fabrication" (shared with MP-Bench H-M2, AgentRx)**: the decisive commission step (Assistant lists a top-10 from memory; Verification_Expert assumes numbers) is preceded by an omission step (the WebSurfer never loaded the page; pip timed out) and annotators split. Test: label flagged steps as omission/commission across WW+MP+ARx and measure cross-dataset agreement conditional on the pair. Method: predict the pair and score with either member.
