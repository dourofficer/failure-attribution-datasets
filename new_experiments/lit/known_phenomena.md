## 1. New papers (not summarised in the existing notes)

**SOAP — "Can Failed Trajectories Reveal Where Agents Go Wrong? Agentic Failure Attribution Without Step-Level Labels"** (Nguyen-Thanh, Fang, Li, Fang, Du; arXiv:2610.13105, Oct 2026, preprint). Label-free attribution from *failed* runs only: mean-pooled hidden states of a frozen LLM are stacked over an unlabelled failure corpus, SVD'd, and each step's score is the reciprocal of its energy in a selected band of singular directions. The paper names **downstream contamination**: "later steps may exhibit stronger failure signals because they inherit an earlier mistake", so independent per-step scorers "systematically localize failures too late". The fix is attention-guided back-propagation of downstream scores to the predecessors they attend to, S̃(s_i) = S(s_i) + γ·B_i. Evidence: rescoring lifts WW-AG step accuracy 39.15 → 47.62 (Qwen3.5-9B) but CE only 61.38 → 61.78; a pure position-prior rescoring gives 40.74 and "earliest of top-5" 21.16, so the gain is not a position shift; in all four qualitative traces (App. C.7) the base score peaks at a late symptom and rescoring moves the prediction back (TE-Cap: four steps earlier). Caveat: the contamination claim rests on Figure 3 and four examples; **no signed predicted-minus-gold offset is reported**. Two further facts: a reference corpus of *failures* beats one of *successes* on WW-AG (42.86 vs 38.10), and removing steps at or after t* from the reference lowers accuracy (39.68 → 36.51), i.e. the spectral band is defined partly by symptom steps. Test sets are 58–85 runs (one trajectory = 1.6–3.4 points).

**EMFA** (Liao et al., arXiv:2610.11600, Oct 2026) — cited in the report but not summarised. Two-level producer–consumer graph, traced *backward* from the final failure step; an LLM then labels each upstream step chronologically as propagation source / carrier / recovered deviation / unrecovered deviation / symptom; a second branch extracts persistent loops; counterfactual verification is model-based (no replay). Who&When step accuracy 32.76 (HC) / 50.00 (AG) vs CHIEF 29.31 / 45.60. **Without the propagation branch step accuracy collapses to 1.72 / 13.49**: the backward walk is essentially the whole method. No offset analysis.

**ErrorProbe — "Towards Self-Improving Error Diagnosis in Multi-Agent Systems"** (Li, Yilmaz, Chen, Le; arXiv:2604.17658, Apr 2026). Taxonomy-based local anomaly detection → *symptom-driven backward tracing* to prune context → Strategist/Investigator/Arbiter team with tool-grounded verification and a verified episodic memory. Search snippet claims TracerTraj step accuracy 8.7% (LLM-judge) → 39.4% (ErrorProbe+Memory, Claude 3.7) **[numbers unverified; abstract has none]**. SOAP uses it as the strongest prompt baseline (WW-AG 39.68 with Qwen, 49.21 with GPT-5, which beats SOAP's 47.62).

**AgentDebugX / DeepDebug** (Zhu et al., arXiv:2607.18754, Jul 2026). Toolkit; states "the step where a failure becomes visible is often not the step that caused it" but measures no gap. Who&When (n = 184, qwen3.5-9b): exact step 28.8 vs All-at-Once 22.3; the margin concentrates on traces longer than 40 events (26 traces, "descriptive"). Rerun repair on GAIA: 13 of 73 failures fixed vs Reflexion 6.

**AgentScope / AgentErrata** (Bi et al., arXiv:2609.02371, Sep 2026) — the AgentErrata paper (303 injected trajectories; nothing released). Appendix C: "The annotated 'mistake step' typically marks the earliest detectable issue … but does not necessarily correspond to the most causally critical failure." One Who&When HC case: annotated step 4 (a plausible search-results step) vs a stagnation loop at steps 11 and 15 (7–11 steps later). §5.1 adds that many GPT-5.1 predictions pick a later step on the same chain; **no frequency is given**. On AgentErrata, LLM-as-judge step accuracy is 2.80–3.63 vs AgentScope 30–31; on Who&When with GPT-5.1 AgentScope only ties step-by-step (25.40 / 22.41 vs 25.40 / 24.14).

**DRIFT / TELBench — "Where Do Deep-Research Agents Go Wrong? Span-Level Error Localization"** (Wang et al., arXiv:2606.02060, Jun 2026). 2,790 trajectories (MiroFlow, OAgent; GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-Sonnet-4.5; GAIA, XBench, BrowseComp), LLM-proposed spans validated by experts (no IAA); 1,000-instance benchmark, 11.95 spans per trajectory. **97.3% of failed and 36.9% of successful trajectories contain an error span; 17.4% vs 6.3% of spans are errors.** Recovered mistakes are *not* labelled as errors. Evidence gaps are the most frequent error type but least associated with failure; missed checks, candidate-scope, anchoring and constraint-semantic errors are rarer but more often fatal. Failed runs put first errors in the earliest and latest position bins. Bare first-error accuracy of frontier judges 10.3–15.7 (hard split 1.75–8.5); DRIFT 19.9–24.1; both degrade with span count.

**"From Confident Closing to Silent Failure: Characterizing False Success in LLM Agents"** (Advani, arXiv:2606.09863, Jun 2026, FAGEN@ICML). 9,876 τ²-bench and 1,879 AppWorld runs. False success (completion claim with reward 0): 35.6% of τ²-bench failures (airline 45%, retail 47–48%, dual-control telecom 3%), 75.8% of AppWorld failures that write a status; per model 13% (GPT-5.2) to 79% (Qwen3-Max-Thinking). Five judges × five prompts never exceed AUROC 0.65 (τ²) / 0.54 (AppWorld); judges score assertive closings +0.27–0.36 higher regardless of outcome and rank honest failures as *more* failure-like than false successes (AUROC 0.18–0.30). A TF-IDF/XGBoost detector reaches 0.83 / 0.95 task-disjoint.

**OverclaimBench — "Quantifying Overclaiming Propensity in Frontier LLM Agents"** (Smyth et al., arXiv:2609.20812, Sep 2026). Five file-review scenarios with transcript-measured coverage. Agents fail to read every requested file in 67.9% of runs; 80.4% of those runs are misleading (59–96% per model); agents that falsely claim a complete review miss planted defects at 1.8× the rate.

**"The Unreliable Progress Bar"** (Wang, Wang, Wu; arXiv:2609.08589, Sep 2026). τ²-bench + StageIF: self-reported progress is stage-dependent; most models lose accuracy mid-task and recover at the end (no numbers in the abstract).

**trajectory-judge — "What Outcome-Only LLM Judges Miss on Agent Trajectories"** (Mohammadi, arXiv:2609.00038, Aug 2026, NeurIPS-W). Deterministic support-desk testbed, 400 exactly labelled runs with one injected step fault. Outcome-only 14B judge: 34–76% "recall" on reply-unchanged faults but **zero paired discrimination** (it flags 33% of clean runs); loud faults 84% recall (+0.393 paired), silent faults 45% (+0.048). Told to check each step: all faults, 0/100 clean. It reliably checks only 1 of 4 invented promises.

**"How Coding Agents Fail Their Users"** (Tang et al., arXiv:2605.29442, May 2026). 20,574 real sessions: 91.49% of resolutions need explicit user correction; inaccurate self-reporting grows in share over time.

**WebStep** (Chung et al., arXiv:2606.15673, COLM 2026). 1,800 web tasks with a deterministic semantic MDP beside the GUI. Three agents at 34–37% success differ in exploration reach and execution accuracy; the decisive error found by bifurcation is agent-specific.

**WebArena-Lite audit** (Gan et al., arXiv:2610.01491, Oct 2026). Human review of all 165 tasks recovers 5.45–8.49 pp of successes the automatic evaluator missed (false negatives only).

**ProcessBench** (Zheng et al., arXiv:2412.06559) — only its agreement figure is in the notes. 3/3 agreement on erroneous solutions: GSM8K 66.7%, MATH 59.4%, OlympiadBench 52.8%, Omni-MATH 47.8% (correct solutions 80–96%); ≈30% of solutions discarded for no agreement. Among *correct-answer* solutions, the share with a process error is 3.5 / 18.8 / 32.2 / 51.8%. PRMs trained with MC labels (Math-Shepherd avg F1 31.5) fall far below prompted critics (QwQ 71.5, o1-mini 87.9) and collapse on the hard sets (Skywork 70.8 → 21.0).

**Judge-bias primaries.** *Lost in the Middle* (Liu et al., TACL 2024, arXiv:2307.03172): 20-document QA, GPT-3.5-Turbo 75.8% (answer first) → 53.8% (10th) → 63.2% (last), below its closed-book 56.1%. *LLMs are not Fair Evaluators* (Wang et al., arXiv:2305.17926): swapping order lets Vicuna-13B "beat" ChatGPT on 66/80 queries. *Judging the Judges* (Shi et al., arXiv:2406.07791, AACL 2025): position consistency 0.57–0.82 across 15 judges; direction is family-specific (Claude-3 / Gemini recency PF +0.18…+0.32, GPT-4o primacy −0.12, GPT-4 ≈ 0) and *flips between benchmarks*; the quality gap between candidates, not length, drives it. *BabelJudge* (arXiv:2606.22329): trajectory-level perturbations and a trajectory-length-bias metric for agent judges (7B judge only).

**Graph / backward-tracing methods, abstracts only.** CHIEF (arXiv:2602.23701): hierarchical causal graph, oracle-guided backtracking, "progressive causal screening to separate true root causes from propagated symptoms"; EDGE (arXiv:2609.01360, EMNLP 2026): error-dependency graph pruned by counterfactual rollouts, TRAIL/MAST multi-error attribution; StepFinder (arXiv:2606.03467) **[unverified]**; ECHO (arXiv:2510.04886) **[unverified]**. None reports a signed offset.

**Human-factors primaries.** Caplan, Posner & Cheney, *JAMA* 1991;265:1957–60: 112 anaesthesiologists, 21 matched case pairs identical except outcome severity; "appropriate care" ratings fall 31 pp when the outcome is made permanent, rise 28 pp when made temporary (15/21 pairs). Fischhoff 1975 (*JEP:HPP* 1, 288–299; reprinted *Qual Saf Health Care* 2003;12:304): outcome knowledge raises the judged prior probability of the reported outcome; secondary sources give +10 to +25 pp **[magnitude unverified]**. Rasmussen 1990 (*Phil Trans R Soc B* 327:449–462): the cause found depends on the *stop rule* that ends backward search. Henriksen & Kaplan 2003 (*Qual Saf Health Care* 12 Suppl 2: ii46): hindsight bias in incident investigation. LLM versions: Malberg et al. (arXiv:2410.15413) find hindsight bias among 30 biases present in some of 20 LLMs; Matsuura et al. (arXiv:2609.13454, ML4H 2026) show outcome-consistent "hindsight-trap" shifts in GPT-5.6 / Opus 5 / GLM-5.2 / Gemma 4 when the full timeline is visible, removed by temporal masking.

**τ-bench** (Yao et al., arXiv:2406.12045): pass^8 < 25% for GPT-4o on retail: successes of the same system on the same task are not reproducible.

## 2. Known phenomena

Dataset abbreviations: WW (Who&When), WWP (Who&When Pro), TE (TraceElephant), MPB, LRCA (LongRCA), ARx (AgentRx), SymF (SymFail/SymTrace), TT (TracerTraj), AEG (Aegis), CE (CORRECT-Error), AErr (AgentErrata), MAST, TRAIL, AEB (AgentErrorBench), AH (AgentHallu), FaP, HOR (HORIZON), MRCA (MegaRCA-Mix), MemT (MemTrace), SAB (SearchAuditBench), TEB (TrajErrBench).

### P1. Downstream contamination: symptoms score higher than causes, so label-free and per-step scorers localise late

- **Statement.** A step that *inherits* an error (empty tool return, loop, verbalised confusion, retry) carries a larger anomaly / judge signal than the step that introduced it; any scorer that treats steps independently or trains on outcomes places its mass on the symptom.
- **Strongest evidence.** SOAP: the base spectral score peaks on a late symptom in every qualitative case, and the only rescoring that helps is dependency-weighted back-propagation (+8.5 pp on WW-AG; position-only rescoring +1.6, "earliest-of-top-5" −18). EMFA: removing the backward propagation branch drops step accuracy 32.76 → 1.72 (HC) and 50.00 → 13.49 (AG). Who&When Pro: judges "detect symptoms but mis-locate root causes" (Step@±1 tolerance adds +13.9 / +16.9 / +28.1 pp text / image / video). FaP: median t_err = 7, t_lock = 12, t_obs = 16, so the first observable signal is ~9 steps after the cause, and 28% of failures have none. Magentic-One ledger stall flags fire after k* in 61% of WW-HC logs, median 4.5 steps later (measured). Timeseries README §3.1 reports the same for transition surprise, RUDDER and hazard posteriors.
- **Conditions.** Strongest on long traces with loops (AgentDebugX's margin is confined to >40-event traces; EMFA's loop branch matters most on AG); weakest on short templated runs (CE: rescoring +0.4). SOAP's reference corpus *needs* the symptom steps (removing steps ≥ t* lowers accuracy 39.68 → 36.51): symptoms define the failure subspace, dependencies move the credit back.
- **Applies to.** WW-HC, LRCA, TE, FaP, SAB (median critical position 58.3% of the trajectory, 45.9% in the final third), ARx (root ≠ first failure 27%), TRAIL, MemT, SymF. Less to AEG/TT/CE (late or fixed injection positions coincide with symptoms).
- **Gap.** **No paper reports the signed offset (predicted − gold) or the fraction of predictions after the gold step**; SOAP, EMFA, CHIEF, ErrorProbe, AgentDebugX all claim late bias and none measures it.

### P2. The labelled step is a convention, and the conventions differ by 7–30 steps

- **Statement.** "Earliest detectable issue" (Who&When, TEB, LRCA practice), "decisive / earliest unrecovered" (ARx, EMFA, FaP t_err), "lock-in" (FaP t_lock, ELPO's first irrecoverable step) and "largest repair effect" (Repairs for Whom?) are different steps in a sizeable fraction of runs.
- **Evidence.** AgentErrata App. C: Who&When step 4 vs a stagnation loop at 11/15 in the one case shown. ARx: root cause ≠ first annotated failure in 27% of public and 41% of Magentic-One runs (WW step 3 vs ARx step 33). FaP: fix window median 1, but 239/1,184 runs > 10 steps. EPV: 66% of judge "first mistake" labels show no success-probability drop (training-signal note §4). ELPO Fig. 1: editing the first irrecoverable step repairs far more runs than editing the "first error". Repairs for Whom?: best repair position differs by 0.51 across thirds; labels are executor-specific (62% self-agreement, 29–32% cross-model). ProcessBench adopts "earliest error" because later steps are "ambiguous to judge": the convention is chosen for annotatability, not causality.
- **Agreement falls with difficulty and length.** ProcessBench 3/3 agreement 66.7 → 47.8% from GSM8K to Omni-MATH (~30% discarded); TEB κ 0.91 (τ², 29 messages) vs 0.67 (SWE-Pro, 120–300 steps); LRCA 40.3% exact (median 145 steps); MPB 16.2% unanimous, pairwise disagreement up to 60% on ~33-step logs vs nDCG@5 0.68–0.79 on 8-step logs. MPB frames disagreement as *perspective* (web-first vs code-first), never as earlier-vs-later on one chain, and never analyses position.
- **Applies to.** All single-step datasets (WW, TE, LRCA, TEB, SAB, AEB, AH, CE, TT, AErr, HOR); set-valued ones (MPB, ARx, TRAIL, MRCA) expose it rather than hide it.

### P3. Annotators anchor on LLM pre-annotations and on the known outcome

- **Statement.** Most 2026 step labels are human *edits* of an LLM draft produced with the outcome (and often the verifier diagnostics) visible; agreement statistics then measure agreement with the draft, and labels inherit the judge's late bias (P1) and hindsight.
- **Evidence (agent datasets).** TEB: three pre-annotators saw the trajectory *plus* the failure diagnostics; their exact match with the final label is 53–77% (τ²) and 31–56% (SWE-Pro), whereas the best trajectory-only detector reaches 52.5 / 52.8 (τ²) and 17.4 / 24.4 (SWE-Pro): the labels are closer to an outcome-informed LLM than any auditor can be. FaP: Claude Opus 4.6 drafts every timestamp (reading only steps 1–40, every 5th, last 30 when m > 120); two reviewers then agree at κ 0.94–0.96 *on the same draft*. SAB: Claude Opus 4.6 pre-screens, one annotator per case, no IAA. TELBench: LLM-proposed, expert-validated, no IAA. MAST: o1 labels, κ 0.77 on 19 traces. HOR: judge-only. Only WW, TE, MPB, LRCA, ARx were labelled without a draft, and their agreement is the lowest (α 0.64, 40.3%, 16.2%).
- **Evidence (humans, transferable).** Outcome severity alone moves expert "appropriate care" ratings by 28–31 pp on otherwise identical cases (Caplan 1991); outcome knowledge raises judged prior probabilities by ~10–25 pp and judges are unaware of it (Fischhoff 1975); the cause found depends on the stop rule (Rasmussen 1990), and Who&When's "first mistake" policy is a stop rule by name.
- **Evidence (LLM judges).** Hindsight-trap answers rise when the full timeline is visible and fall under temporal masking (Matsuura 2026). Giving Who&When judges the ground-truth answer changes step accuracy by only −1 to +1.8 pp (All-at-Once AG 13.53 → 12.50, HC 3.51 → 5.26), and SOAP with-GT is *lower* (43.39 vs 47.62): outcome knowledge changes *which* steps are blamed more than *how many* are right.
- **Applies to.** TEB, FaP, SAB, HOR, MAST (drafted); WWP/AEG/CE/TT/AErr are label-by-construction and immune; WW/TE/MPB/LRCA/ARx carry human-only noise instead.

### P4. Outcome-only judges detect loud faults, miss silent ones, and are fooled by assertive closings

- **Statement.** A judge that sees the final reply or an outcome flag has near-zero discrimination for faults that leave the reply unchanged, and treats confident completion language as evidence of completion.
- **Evidence.** trajectory-judge: paired discrimination +0.393 for loud vs +0.048 for silent faults; 33% false alarms on clean runs; step-by-step prompting restores 100% / 0%. False Success: judges ≤ 0.65 AUROC, honest failures ranked as more failure-like than false successes (0.18–0.30), +0.27–0.36 completion score for assertion vocabulary. EPV: four frontier judges flag "mistakes" in 8/10 and 10/10 *successful* runs. OverclaimBench: 80.4% of incomplete reviews are reported as complete.
- **Applies to.** Any dataset whose failure set was defined by a judge (HOR 12.9% verifier–judge disagreement; MAST; WW outcome labels from GAIA answer match, with the AgentErrata note that a WW case ends with the correct final answer yet is labelled a failure).

### P5. Long-trajectory judging: position effects, context limits and the all-at-once / step-by-step / binary-search trade-off

- **Statement.** Step localisation by a prompted judge degrades with length faster than agent/mode classification, through (i) serial-position effects on attention, (ii) context exhaustion, and (iii) a prompting-strategy trade-off in which no strategy is good on long traces.
- **Evidence.** Lost-in-the-middle: 22-pp drop for mid-context evidence (GPT-3.5), below closed-book. Position bias: consistency 0.57–0.82 and the *direction* flips by family and benchmark, so a judge's step bias is not predictable without calibration. Who&When: All-at-Once best on agent (54–55%) but 3.5–5.3% on HC steps; Step-by-Step best on AG steps (25.5%) but 7–9% on HC; on HC (median 32 steps) every strategy is ≤ 7% exact. LRCA: the same six methods score 30–42% step on WW but 3–24% on LRCA. TRAIL: o1/o3/Claude exceed context on SWE traces (median 1.6M chars); joint accuracy ≤ ~0.1. WWP: worst on long and multimodal traces. DRIFT: bare FEA 10–16 overall, 1.75–8.5 on the hard split, falling with span count. ProcessBench: all critics fall GSM8K → Omni-MATH (QwQ 88.0 → 61.3; PRMs to ~21). TEB: direct prompting 10–17% on SWE-Pro vs 41–52% on τ².
- **Why binary search fails.** It assumes a monotone prefix state (correct … correct, error … error); recovered errors (ARx 27%, FaP PASS runs 71% with ≥1 error event, measured) violate monotonicity, and ELPO shows the quantity binary search converges to is the first *irrecoverable* step, not the first error.
- **Applies to.** LRCA, TE, TRAIL, MemT, FaP, TEB-SWE, SAB (73 messages, 65k tokens), WW-HC; not to WW-AG, AEG, TT, CE, AH (≤ 10 steps).

### P6. The "normal flow" premise: successes share dynamics only in narrow, templated systems

- **Statement (OAT's premise).** Successful trajectories follow a common dynamics from which failures deviate measurably. The evidence says successes are heterogeneous, error-laden and outcome-noisy outside short fixed-protocol systems.
- **Against.** Process errors inside correct-answer solutions rise from 3.5% (GSM8K) to 51.8% (Omni-MATH) (ProcessBench); 36.9% of successful deep-research runs carry an error span (DRIFT); FaP PASS runs carry 2.29 error events on 22.8 steps, 71% ≥ 1 (measured); 39/54 SymTrace successes fail at least once in three reruns; τ-bench pass^8 < 25%; the WebArena-Lite evaluator misses 5.45–8.49 pp of successes, so the "failure" set contains successes; WebStep: the decisive error is agent-specific and three agents at the same success rate differ in process; Repairs for Whom?: 29–32% cross-executor label agreement. MP-Bench's "web-first vs code-first" disagreement is the same heterogeneity seen by annotators.
- **For.** OAT's in-domain gains come from MCP-Atlas 7-step runs; the Aegis frameworks have fixed turn counts (LLM Debate 12, MacNet 10); SOAP's *failure* reference beats a success reference on WW-AG (42.86 vs 38.10), evidence that the informative subspace is "what failures share"; the mark-structural priors (README §3.2: first write 41.9%, first source edit 45.5%) show that *task-phase* structure is shared even when content is not.
- **Applies to.** AH and HOR (the only released sets with successes), FaP PASS, SymF reruns; the premise is untestable on WW/TE/MPB/LRCA (no successes).

### P7. Absorption vs cascade: which errors die and which propagate

- **Statement.** Most injected or natural errors are absorbed; what cascades is determined by error *type*, *count of faulty messages*, *topology* and the presence of a checker, not by position alone.
- **Evidence.** Huang et al.: semantic errors cut code-generation from 64.73 to 39.15, syntactic only to 47.78, and systems with a compiler or judge absorb syntactic errors entirely (Self-collab 75.6 vs 76.2 clean; MAD 67.1 vs 62.2, i.e. *improves*); raising the share of faulty messages 0.2 → 1.0 costs 12 pp, raising errors-per-message 0.2 → 0.6 costs 7 pp; hierarchical −5.5 vs linear −23.7; Challenger + Inspector restore 43.3 → 75.0 of 76.8. Jamshidi et al. (claim-level refinement chains, no tools): per transition 35.2% corrected, 19.4% weakened, 21.3% preserved, 11.4% deleted, 7.3% amplified, 3.8% over-corrected; net attenuation 0.644 over 3 agents, but factual accuracy also decays (0.789 → 0.769) and the *last* model dominates. Aegis: 23–66% of injections leave the outcome unchanged (report §4). DRIFT: evidence gaps are frequent but weakly fatal; missed checks, candidate-scope, anchoring and constraint-semantic errors are rare but fatal. FaP: after lock-in 58.7% never notice, 34.9% notice and apply the wrong fix; 2.52 recovery attempts per failed run. EMFA labels "recovered deviation" but reports no count.
- **Conditions.** Absorption needs an *independent* checker (compiler; DB-state user simulator: telecom false success 3% vs 45–48% single-control); refinement chains attenuate hallucinated *claims* by deletion, not correction, which does not transfer to tool-state errors.
- **Applies to.** AEG (absorbed branches discarded), ARx (recovered events labelled), FaP (PASS error events, recovery attempts), TRAIL (error spans in correct-ending traces), MPB (single-annotator flags), SymF, WWP (injections late because the prefix must be clean).

### P8. Verification theatre and false success are the dominant late symptom

- **Statement.** The final "done" claim is both the most frequent symptom and the least reliable step; it is where outcome-only judges and last-step priors concentrate.
- **Evidence.** FaP: verification theatre 27.6%, fabricated success 15% of failures. False Success: 35.6% of τ²-bench failures and 75.8% of status-writing AppWorld failures claim completion. OverclaimBench: 67.9% incomplete, 80.4% of those misleading, 1.8× more missed defects. 20,574 sessions: inaccurate self-reporting is a growing share. SAB: the final answer *is* the critical step in 17%. C-without-V rule separates PASS 16.3% vs FAIL 28.7% (measured). MAST FM-3.x verification modes.
- **Applies to.** FaP, SAB, TEB VERIFY.*, MAST/AEG FM-3.x, WW-HC (orchestrator "FINAL ANSWER" after stalls), ARx, TRAIL.

## 3. Hypotheses the literature suggests but has not tested

1. **Late bias is measurable and method-dependent: on WW-HC, LRCA and TEB-SWE, ≥ 60% of exact misses by All-at-Once / OAT / SOAP-base land *after* the gold step, with median offset ≥ 3 steps, and backward-tracing methods (EMFA, SOAP-rescored, CHIEF) halve the median offset without changing the miss rate.** Compute the signed offset distribution per method; no paper has.
2. **The late-bias magnitude equals the dataset's t_obs − t_err gap: a judge's median offset on FaP ≈ 9 (median t_obs − t_err), on TEB-τ² ≈ 1–2, and it correlates across datasets with the fix-window length.** Testable on FaP (both timestamps) and ARx (first vs root events).
3. **Pre-annotated labels are closer to the drafting model than to a blind annotator: re-labelling 100 TEB-τ² and 100 SAB cases without the LLM draft or the diagnostics will reproduce the released step in < 70% (TEB) and < 55% (SAB), against κ 0.91 and no-IAA respectively, and the disagreements will skew *earlier* than the released step.** The Caplan design (same case, outcome masked) transfers directly.
4. **Masking the outcome and the final k steps from a judge moves its predictions earlier and raises exact accuracy on long traces: on WW-HC / LRCA, hiding the last 20% of steps improves step accuracy of All-at-Once by ≥ 3 pp while agent accuracy falls.** Follows from P1 + P4 + Matsuura's temporal masking.
5. **The spectral "failure subspace" of SOAP is a symptom subspace: scoring FaP PASS runs with a SOAP reference built from FAIL runs will flag the recovered error events (t of the 2.29 PASS events) at ≥ 2× the position prior, i.e. SOAP detects "something went wrong here", and the attention transport is what turns it into attribution.** Testable once FaP step text is rejoined.
6. **Success-reference (one-class) scorers and failure-reference scorers rank steps nearly identically on symptom steps and differ only on cause steps; the Spearman correlation between OAT-style and SOAP-base scores is > 0.7 on post-t_obs steps and < 0.3 on pre-t_lock steps.** Distinguishes "normal-flow deviation" from "failure-subspace membership" on AH / HOR.
7. **Error-type fatality in natural traces mirrors the injection results: missing-verification and constraint-semantic errors cascade (FaP: present in > 60% of FAIL t_err triggers), while retrieval/evidence-gap and syntactic/tool-format errors are absorbed (present in PASS error events at ≥ the FAIL rate).** Uses FaP codes, ARx recovered-vs-root categories, TRAIL error-spans in correct-ending traces, DRIFT's fatality ordering.
8. **Binary-search attribution converges to lock-in, not to the decisive step: on FaP-rejoined and ARx, a judge-driven binary search lands within ±1 of t_lock more often than of t_err whenever the fix window > 2, and its advantage over Step-by-Step vanishes on runs with ≥ 1 recovered event.** Direct test of the monotonicity assumption.
9. **Judge position bias transfers to steps: the sign of a judge family's preference-fairness score (recency vs primacy) predicts the sign of its mean step offset on WW-AG.** Cheap: re-run All-at-Once with reversed step numbering.
10. **Claim-style features alone attribute late failures: a TF-IDF detector on the last assistant message predicts whether the gold step lies in the final third of SAB / FaP with AUROC ≥ 0.75, i.e. the "false success" signal is a position prior in disguise.**

## 4. Contradictions and gaps between papers

- **Earliest vs decisive is unresolved in the benchmarks themselves.** Who&When annotates "the earliest step whose correction would succeed"; AgentErrata says that in practice it is the earliest *detectable* issue; AgentRx chooses the first *unrecovered* failure and shows the two differ in 27–41% of runs; FaP keeps all three. Methods are then scored as if one convention held, and EMFA's "earliest unrecovered deviation" rule is tuned to Who&When yet matches AgentRx's definition.
- **Late bias is asserted by five papers and measured by none.** SOAP, EMFA, CHIEF, ErrorProbe, AgentDebugX, Who&When Pro all claim symptom-vs-cause confusion; the only numbers are accuracy differences. The FaP t_err / t_lock / t_obs gaps and the ledger-flag measurement are the only quantified distances, and they are properties of trajectories, not of attributors.
- **Which reference corpus?** OAT trains on successes only and gains +0.172 AUROC OOD from the gate; SOAP trains on failures only and finds a success reference *worse* (38.10 vs 42.86). Both cannot be the right "normal"; neither compares against the other on the same data with the same embeddings.
- **Position-bias direction is not stable** across judge families or benchmarks (Judging the Judges), so "LLM judges are recency-biased" (The Silent Judge, search-only) and "primacy-biased" are both true for some judge; agent-attribution papers never report which judge they used for which split, and MP-Bench's judge-scored reasoning metric uses GPT-5.1 without a position check.
- **Agreement figures are not comparable.** κ 0.91–0.96 (TEB-τ², FaP) are measured after an LLM draft and, in FaP, on the same draft; α 0.64 (TE) and 40.3% (LRCA) are blind. The high numbers are closer to "editor agreement" than to label reliability, and the datasets with the highest κ are the ones most exposed to P3.
- **Attenuation vs amplification.** Jamshidi finds net attenuation (0.644) in refinement chains of *claims*; Huang finds up to −23.7 pp in linear topologies when the error is *injected into the work*; Aegis finds 23–66% absorption. The three use incompatible units (claim score, task accuracy, outcome flip) and none separates deletion from correction (Jamshidi's "deleted" 11.4% counts as attenuation).
- **Self-verification: helps in some benchmarks, theatre in others.** BackendForge's self-verify tool recovers 5 tasks but 49/56 still fail **[unverified]**; FaP finds 27.6% verification theatre; False Success says reasoning traces "rationalize completion rather than verify it" (unquantified). No study measures verification *efficacy* conditional on the error type being verified.
- **Replay stochasticity undermines single-rollout verifiers.** EMFA's counterfactual verification is model-based; CHIEF's oracles are synthesised; SymTrace shows 39/54 successes fail on one of three reruns. Every "counterfactually verified" label here except EPV, ELPO, CSO and ProVer is a single draw.
- **Data gap unchanged.** The two phenomena most in need of measurement (signed offset of attributors; error fate inside successes) need successes with step text (AH, HOR only) or FaP's step text (not released). Data-engine rows 1–3 of the report remain the prerequisite.
