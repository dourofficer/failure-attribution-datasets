## OAT — Tracing Agentic Failure from the Flow of Success (Yeh, Zhu, Deep, Li; arXiv 2607.12747, Jul 2026)
Mechanism: step representation h_t = mean-pooled last-layer hidden state of the generating (or a proxy) LLM over the tokens of action a_t, PCA to 64-d; a Neural CDE (3-layer MLP vector field, cubic-spline control path, gated control path that damps atypical spline derivatives) is fit by MSE to reconstruct h_t along the normalized time u=t/T on ~80 SUCCESSFUL trajectories; anomaly score e_t = ||h_t − ĥ(u_t)||²; error set = top-k or steps above a conformal threshold calibrated on held-out successful steps (α=0.2). Evaluated on self-collected MCP-Atlas (103 succ / 88 fail, authors' own set-valued "contributing step" labels: hallucination, wrong tool args, unhandled tool error, repeated identical queries) and OOD on Who&When (184). F1 0.435 in-domain vs 0.21 GPT-5; 0.21–0.23 on Who&When vs 0.15 GPT-5; AUROC 0.76 on Who&When. Later layers > earlier; mean pooling >> last token; proxy LLMs (Llama-4-Scout, Gemma-4-31B, GPT-oss-120B) work almost as well; CORAL second-order alignment used for OOD. Case studies: scores DECAY along propagation chains (origin > inherited); misses early latent reasoning errors ("no tool available" at step 1) and reliably fires where failure is verbalized; fixed CP threshold drops subtler causal steps.

Ideas:
- **Propagation-decay decoding of anomaly scores** — OAT's case studies show origin steps score higher than steps that inherit the error, with monotone decay. Decode the decisive step as the start of the first "peak-then-decay" run rather than argmax / threshold; evaluate exact and ±k on Who&When, TraceElephant. L1 (successes only) or L0 with any step scorer. Strengthens P2 (decoding of the hazard posterior). Risk: decay observed on 3 cases. Plausibility 4, Effort S.
- **OAT is a t_obs estimator, not a t_err estimator** — it fires when failure becomes manifest, misses latent causes. Use Failure-as-a-Process's three timestamps to evaluate *which timestamp* each estimator targets (OAT → t_obs, hazard model → t_lock, human → t_err) and learn the lag t_obs − t_err as a separate regression. New; strengthens P2's Assumption (absorption). Risk: FaP has no trajectory text, needs CodeTracer re-join. Plausibility 4, Effort M.
- **Reconstruction error as a covariate of the hazard head** — feed e_t (one-class, from successes) together with the raw embedding into h_θ; the survival likelihood then supervises how to turn "anomalous" into "fatal" (absorbed anomalies get low hazard). Strengthens P2 U1 directly. Plausibility 5, Effort S.
- **Continuous normalizing flow / Neural SDE version of q_0** — OAT's own future work: exact per-step log-likelihood under the success flow; this is exactly the pre-change density q_0 needed by P2's L0 change-point mixture. Strengthens P2 U2. Plausibility 3, Effort M.
Quotable: "+20% F1 in-domain, +7% OOD"; "0 tokens, 7–16 ms per trajectory vs 4–40 s"; "later-layer representations consistently outperform"; "mean pooling vs last token: significant degradation"; "OAT tends to miss early, latent errors ... reliably detects steps where the failure becomes explicitly manifest" (App. H.2). Code/data: anonymous.4open.science/r/OAT-183C (MCP-Atlas labels + code).

## Progress Advantage — Neglected Free Lunch from Post-training (Oh, Li, Park, Yeh, Mallick, Li; arXiv 2606.26080)
Mechanism: for a KL-regularized (or clip-constrained, Prop. 2) RL-trained policy π* and its reference π_ref, the token-level log-ratio β·log π*(a|s)/π_ref(a|s) EQUALS the optimal advantage A*(s,a)=Q*−V* even under stochastic transitions (Prop. 1); the reward itself is not recoverable (residual δ_t = V(s_t) − E[V(s_{t+1})], Remark 1). Aggregations: sum / mean / position-weighted / extreme token (min, max) per step, then min/mean over steps. Public pairs: Qwen3.5-9B vs -Base, Gemma4-4B, Qwen3-14B, Olmo3-7B. Results: best-of-8 selection beats trained PRMs (WildReward-8B, ThinkPRM) and confidence baselines; trajectory UQ AUROC up to 0.865 on τ²-Airline; cross-policy scoring works (Gemma4 scoring Qwen trajectories, AUROC 0.75); failure attribution on Who&When via argmin of per-step reward "rivals AgenTracer on Hand-Crafted" (figure only). Reference choice matters: TIES-merged intermediate references beat the raw base.

Ideas:
- **PA as a zero-cost estimate of the interventional effect** — A*(s_t,a_t) = Q*−V* is by definition "how much better this action is than the policy's average action at this state", i.e. the same object as P1's φ(τ,k,δ) with δ = resample. Validate PA against rollout-measured p_0 / φ in replayable environments (τ²-bench, ALFWorld, WebShop) — the first ground-truth check of PA as a PRM — then use it as the surrogate f of T1. New link between P1 and an existing signal. Risk: needs policy/reference checkpoint pair; released datasets were generated by GPT-4o so only cross-policy scoring is possible there. Plausibility 5, Effort M.
- **PA tokens as the input sequence of the hazard model** — replace raw embeddings by the per-token advantage sequence (or both); learn the aggregation that the paper hand-tunes (MEAN/MIN vs MAX/MEAN) with the outcome likelihood (L1). Strengthens P2 U1. Plausibility 4, Effort S.
- **Observation shock from the stochastic residual** — in the stochastic MDP the per-step value change splits into the action advantage (PA, model-side) and δ_t (what the environment returned relative to expectation, tool/user-side). With V estimated by the survival function of P2, the sign and size of δ_t at the labelled step gives a *fault side* (model vs observation), which MegaRCA-Mix, AgentRx (User-blamed events) and SearchAuditBench's excluded environment failures need. New; strengthens P1's fault-side output. Plausibility 3, Effort M.
- **Cross-policy PA as a universal text-only baseline** — Table 4 shows a Gemma4 pair scores Qwen3.5 trajectories; run one open pair over all 21 datasets' logs (argmin step, min-step aggregation) as the standard annotation-free baseline in the report. Plausibility 5, Effort S.
Quotable: Prop. 1 (exact advantage recovery); "rivaling AgenTracer in the Hand-Crafted split"; "policy log probability assigns low scores to tool-calling strings even though they are correct ... progress advantage assigns positive scores" (Fig. 3) — this answers the open \note in futureworks.tex about why the agent's own likelihood is not an error signal. Code: URL in paper; policy pairs listed in App. A.1.

## Hide-and-Seek — VLA runtime monitoring from trajectory labels (Park, Li, Oh, Yeh, Kira, Hagenow, Li; arXiv 2605.30834)
Mechanism: per-step action embeddings h_t (OpenVLA: layer- and DoF-averaged action-token embeddings; π0/π0.5: hidden state before the velocity head), window-averaged (w=4–8); a 1-layer LSTM f_φ(τ_≤t) → s_t ∈ [0,1]. Losses from trajectory labels only: L_inter = hinge(m_r − max_t s_t^fail + max_t s_t^succ) over fail/success pairs; L_intra = hinge(m_o − mean_{t≥t_onset} s_t + mean_{t<t_onset} s_t) with proxy onset t_onset = argmax_t (s_t − s_{t−1}). Functional conformal band (μ_t + b_t) from successful calibration rollouts gives a time-varying alarm threshold. Results: bACC 0.85–0.89 vs SAFE-MLP 0.82–0.87 (LIBERO), +19 bACC over uniform label propagation, t_onset within 0.016/−0.008 of GPT-5.2 oracle onset without temporal supervision, 2000× faster than a VLM monitor.

Ideas:
- **MIL contrastive objective as the L1 alternative to the survival likelihood** — same inputs as P2 U1, different estimator: inter-trajectory max-contrast + intra-trajectory onset-contrast. Run on AgentHallu (443/250), HORIZON (2498/1284), TRAIL, and self-collected runs; score onset vs t_err / t_lock / t_obs (FaP) and vs Who&When exact step; compare directly with the hazard MLE and OAT on the same frozen encoder. Strengthens P2 (as alternative/ensemble); the onset proxy argmax Δs is itself a cheap decoding rule for any step scorer. Plausibility 5, Effort S.
- **Functional conformal monitor for U3** — time-varying threshold calibrated on successful prefixes gives the prefix monitor of P2 U3 a false-alarm guarantee per trajectory; report TWA (time-weighted accuracy) and detection lag ∆t to t_lock. Plausibility 5, Effort S.
- **Anti-pattern evidence**: uniform propagation of the trajectory label to every step costs ~19 bACC — a quantitative argument against training step scorers on "every step after t* is wrong" (also PQM's finding). Use as justification in the proposals. Effort none.
Quotable: "+19.4% bACC on OpenVLA and +18.6% on π0 over uniform labeling"; "t_onset closely approximates the oracle (+0.016 / −0.008)"; "0.001 s vs 2.343 s per step". Code/videos: project page.

## UQ in LLM Agents — position paper (Oh, Park, Kim, Li, Li, Yeh, Du, Hassani, Bogdan, Song, Li; arXiv 2602.05073)
Mechanism: agent trajectory as a dynamic Bayesian net (A_i | E_{i−1},O_{i−1}; O_i | A_i,E_i; E deterministic memory); total uncertainty U(F_≤T) = U(E0,O0) + Σ[U(A_i|·) + U(O_i|·)] (chain rule). Four challenges with τ²-bench pilots: estimators (NLL/entropy/verbalized AUROC 0.47–0.69, near random); heterogeneous entities (agent's NLL of user messages deviates from the simulator's true NLL; an auxiliary "world model" LLM narrows the gap); dynamics (naive averaging cannot separate success/failure curves; proposes a signed gating g(F_i): interactive+evidential actions subtract information gain, others add uncertainty); benchmarks (only 4/44 agent benchmarks are turn-level).

Ideas:
- **Observation-side surprise as the environment-fault feature** — estimate U(O_i | A_i, E_i) with a separate "world-model" LLM prompted to predict the next tool/user output; a step whose observation is highly surprising under the world model but whose action was ordinary is an environment/tool-side fault (SymFail's 377 failed tool results, AgentRx "User" blame, MegaRCA environment side). Gives the fault-side output of P1 from logs alone. New; Plausibility 3, Effort M.
- **Action-type-conditional hazard** — g(F_i) says information-gathering/clarifying actions reduce uncertainty, commit actions (writes, final answers) propagate it. Make the hazard h_t in P2 depend on an action-class covariate (read / ask / think / write / answer; FaP's action codes E/P/W/X/V/C/R/O already exist). Predicts human labels concentrate on commit steps (lock-in) and gives a structured, interpretable hazard. Strengthens P2 U1/U3. Plausibility 4, Effort S.
- **Reframe the 21 attribution datasets as the missing turn-level agent-UQ benchmark** — the position paper's gap (4/44 turn-level) is exactly what Who&When-style t* labels provide: evaluate calibration of P(fail | prefix) *at t** and lead time, not only exact step. Framing; Plausibility 4, Effort S.
Quotable: "only 4 out of 44 benchmarks provide turn-level annotation"; Table 2 AUROC 0.47–0.69 for NLL/entropy/verbalized on τ²; "remarkable deviations" between agent-estimated and true observation NLL (Fig. 3). Artifacts: HF changdae/tau2-uq-artifacts.

## PQM — Process Reward Model with Q-value Rankings (W. Li, Y. Li; ICLR 2025)
Mechanism: Q*(s_t,a_t) := σ⁻¹ P(correct final answer | a_{1:t}) under the optimal policy; Assumption 3.1 (from a correct state the next step is far more likely correct than from a wrong one); Theorem 3.5: Q descends along wrong steps, ascends along correct steps, with a large gap between the two groups: Q_w|W| < … < Q_w1 ≪ V(x) < Q_c1 < … < Q_c|C|. Trained with a Plackett-Luce-style comparative loss with a margin ζ on wrong steps (Eq. 9) and a practical variant (Eq. 10) that ignores the ordering among post-error steps because "all steps after the first incorrect one are marked negative" is noisy pseudo-labelling. Beats BCE PRMs by up to +11.6 BoN accuracy on MATH500; Q drops sharply at the first error (Table 4) while BCE PRMs decay smoothly; validated Assumption 3.1 with 32 Monte-Carlo continuations per step.

Ideas:
- **Ranking-with-margin loss for the attributor** — train a step scorer on L2 data so that all steps before t* outrank steps at/after t* by margin ζ while internal order after t* is unconstrained (Eq. 10); decode t* as the largest drop. Directly handles the "later steps are also wrong but we don't know which" ambiguity; also fits MP-Bench vote counts (graded ranking) and AgentRx multi-event labels. Compare with BCE/AgenTracer-style training on Who&When Pro, TracerTraj, CORRECT-Error → test on natural sets. Plausibility 5, Effort S/M.
- **Q* = survival function: one theory for both proposals** — PQM's Q_σ(a_{1:t}) = P(success | prefix) is P2's S̄_t and P1's p_0(τ,t+1); Theorem 3.5 gives the monotone shape the hazard posterior should have, and the 32-rollout Monte-Carlo estimate (Fig. 3–4) is exactly P1's rollout screen. Use the comparative loss to fit h_θ from rollout-estimated Q (L1+) instead of MSE. Strengthens both. Plausibility 4, Effort S.
Quotable: "Q-values ascend with the continuation of correct steps and descend as wrong steps proceed, while having a prominent gap"; "existing data corpora typically categorize all subsequent steps a_{l:H} as incorrect ... not necessarily accurately annotated"; "sharp decrease at Step 4 ... accurately locates the error" (Table 4). Code: github.com/WindyLee0822/Process_Q_Model.

## SpikeScore — cross-domain hallucination detection (Deng, Fang, Li, Chen; ICLR 2026)
Mechanism: after an answer A1, induce K=20 follow-up turns with a fixed prompt library; score each turn with a trained probe (SAPLMA MLP on hidden states, or SEP, or training-free scores); SpikeScore = max second-order difference of the score sequence (curvature). Hallucinated answers produce self-contradicting continuations with sharp spikes; factual ones stay stable. Leave-one-domain-out AUROC 0.75–0.79 vs 0.52–0.58 for SAPLMA/SEP and 0.70–0.75 for PRISM/ICR Probe; Theorem 1 gives a separability bound via coefficient of variation; saturates at 15–20 turns; works on RAG (RAGTruth).

Ideas:
- **Instability-under-continuation as a replay-free perturbation screen** — for a candidate step k, branch K short LLM-only continuations from the prefix (no tool execution: ask the agent to re-derive/justify/continue) and score the curvature of a probe along them; erroneous steps yield unstable branches. Sits between P1 (true rollouts, needs replayable env) and P2 (log-only): a cheap proxy for p_0 that is domain-invariant by construction. Restrict to the hazard model's top candidates to control cost. New; Plausibility 3, Effort M. Risk: agent steps are tool-grounded, continuations without tools may not reproduce instability; K=20 per step is expensive on 150-step traces.
- **Leave-one-dataset-out evaluation protocol** — SpikeScore's GHD protocol (train on one domain, test on the pooled rest) is the right template for Tier B of P2 across the 21 datasets; report Δ = in-domain − cross-domain. Plausibility 5, Effort S.
Quotable: "hallucination-initiated multi-turn dialogues universally exhibit larger uncertainty fluctuations"; mean SpikeScore of hallucinations > 2× factual; "performance saturates around 15–20 turns". Code: not stated in main text.

## Cyclical Entropy Eruption / SEAL (W. Li, Im, Li; arXiv 2605.27954)
Mechanism: agent RL (GRPO/GiGPO on ALFWorld, WebShop, search-QA) shows recurring entropy eruptions. Phase 2 cause: correct and wrong trajectories overlap heavily in the policy's last-layer mean-pooled representation (Fig. 3, more than in other LLM tasks); Lemma 3.2 ⟨g_i,g_j⟩ ∝ Σ⟨h_i,k,h_j,k'⟩ so suppressing a wrong trajectory also suppresses similar correct ones (likelihood of correct trajectories DECREASES during training, Fig. 5), flattening the policy and amplifying sentence duplication / hallucination / format errors, which then persist. SEAL: auxiliary token-level BCE head predicting the trajectory outcome, pushing correct/incorrect token representations apart; stabilizes training (Llama3.2-1B WebShop 0 → 79.7%).

Ideas:
- **Raw policy representations do not separate success from failure** — Fig. 3 is a direct caution for OAT- and hazard-style models that read the policy's own hidden states: the useful signal must be *learned* (SEAL head, PA contrast with a reference) rather than read off. Two concrete uses: (a) SEAL-trained policies as the generating agents of the data engine, whose hidden states are already outcome-separated → better features for attribution; (b) a SEAL-style outcome head as a pre-training stage for the hazard encoder, then the MIL/survival loss fixes the temporal structure (SEAL's uniform token labels are exactly what Hide-and-Seek shows to be noisy). Strengthens P2. Plausibility 4, Effort M.
- **Answers the open \note in futureworks.tex** — "why would the agent's own policy assign high probability to its mistakes?": because wrong and correct trajectories are near-identical in representation and likelihood (Fig. 3, Fig. 5), and PA's Fig. 3 shows raw likelihood penalizes correct tool strings; likelihood under the agent's policy is not a correctness signal, the *contrast* to a reference or to successful dynamics is. Effort none.
- **Eruption-phase checkpoints as a natural-failure generator** — during eruption the policy samples degenerate trajectories (repetition = MAST FM-1.3, hallucination, premature/format errors) at elevated but realistic rates; sampling from these checkpoints yields failed trajectories that are on-policy and not prompt-injected, with the pre/post-eruption checkpoint pair as a built-in reference for PA. Cheap source of training failures with a known mode bias. New; Plausibility 3, Effort M.
Quotable: "correct and wrong trajectories exhibit substantial overlap rather than forming clearly separated clusters" (§3.3); "the likelihood of correct trajectories and valid trajectories decreases during agent RL" (Fig. 5); SEAL recovers Llama3.2-1B WebShop from 0% to 79.69%. Code: link in paper.
