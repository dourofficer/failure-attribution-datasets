## Scope and verdict

1. Proposal 2's object is a textbook model: a **discrete-time logistic-hazard model** (person-period logistic regression, Allison 1982; called Nnet-survival or `LogisticHazard` in pycox) with **time-varying, internal (history-generated) covariates**. Successes are **administratively right-censored at T**. Estimators, losses and metrics already exist off the shelf.
2. **At L1 it is not ordinary survival data.** On a failed run the event time is never observed; we only know that the event happened by step T. That is **current-status data** (interval censoring with a single inspection), and its likelihood `1 - prod_t(1-h_t)` is exactly **noisy-OR multiple-instance learning**. Survival and MIL are therefore the same problem here, and their known failure modes carry over together.
3. Three identifiability problems are already known:
   - Current-status data under **case-only or case-control sampling** identify the event-time law only up to a one-parameter family (Jewell & van der Laan 2004). The benchmarks release failures only.
   - **Internal covariates** (steps after absorption are generated by the failed process) pull hazard mass onto symptoms, i.e. toward `t_obs`. This is WTAL's "localisation by classification" bias, and it is the bias OAT reports in its App. H.
   - **Unmodelled task frailty** makes the population hazard fall with step index and removes the causal reading of hazard contrasts (Vaupel et al. 1979; Aalen et al. 2015).
4. The fixes are also known:
   - more inspection times: L1+ restarts turn current-status data into interval-censored data;
   - exact event times on a small subset (L2 `k_lock`);
   - task stratification or frailty terms;
   - hiding the most discriminative steps during training (Hide-and-Seek);
   - instance-level rather than embedding-level pooling (Jang & Kwon 2024);
   - MIL "algorithmic unit tests" (Raff & Holt 2023).
5. The extensions add capability:
   - **Competing risks** give a joint posterior over (when, mode, who) and can use trajectory-level mode labels with no step (Aegis, MAST).
   - **Multi-state models** separate `t_err`, `t_lock` and `t_obs` and can represent recovered errors in successes.
   - A **cure / zero-time atom** absorbs task-level failures that no step should be blamed for.
6. Evaluation:
   - Survival metrics (time-dependent concordance, IPCW/administrative Brier and IBS, incident AUC) and PHM metrics (prognostic horizon, alpha-lambda) score the **prefix monitor**.
   - The ranked probability score and randomised-PIT (D-) calibration score the **attribution posterior**.
   - SED/WTAL tolerance metrics (collar, PSDS) handle low inter-annotator agreement.
7. **LLM-side precedents exist, but none does step attribution.**
   - Time-to-Inconsistency (ICLR'26) fits Cox and AFT models with turn-level drift covariates and builds a monitor with a 2-turn median lead.
   - An attention-MIL early-alert paper (arXiv:2606.05414) trains on trajectory labels only.
   - Absorbing-Markov-chain papers model agent reliability and safety.
   - Executable prefix values (arXiv:2610.17633) supply exactly the L1+ data the hazard needs.
8. **The cheapest decisive experiment uses data already on disk.** Failure-as-a-Process ships per-step action codes (`E/P/W/X/V/C/R/O`) for **all 1,794 PASS + FAIL runs**. A logistic hazard on codes plus position can be fitted at L1 today and scored separately against `t_err`, `t_lock` and `t_obs`. That tests which timestamp an L1 hazard posterior actually recovers.
9. **Verdict.** Proposal 2 is sound. At L1, however, it is a noisy-OR MIL model whose posterior will localise the most failure-predictive symptom unless the design adds order information (L1+, a `k_lock` subset, masking). Report it against the life-table (Kaplan–Meier-type), position-only hazard: that is the survival literature's own null model, and it equals the brief's position prior.
10. I verified 30+ core papers and about 25 supporting ones. **Gap:** no paper fits a hazard to agent steps for attribution, and none treats a *latent* absorbing event with language covariates.

## The hazard model of Proposal 2, placed in the survival literature

### 2.1 Which model class it is

| Element | Proposal 2 | Survival-literature name |
|---|---|---|
| Clock | step index `t = 1..T` | intrinsically discrete time. No discretisation choice is needed, which removes the main design issue Kvamme & Borgan (2021) study. |
| Event | absorption into failure = first *unrecovered* error | single absorbing event. In the datasets this is AgentRx's "first unrecoverable failure" and Failure-as-a-Process `t_lock` (`t_commit`), **not** the counterfactual `k*` / `t_err`. |
| Hazard | `h_t = P(A_t=1 \| A_{t-1}=0, tau_{<=t}) = sigma(f_theta(tau_{<t}, s_t))` | discrete-time hazard with logit link ("logistic hazard", "partial logistic regression", "continuation-ratio" model). The cloglog link would give the grouped proportional-hazards model [U as a cited item; standard]. |
| Covariates | step embedding / step type / observation features of the prefix | time-varying, history-dependent, **internal** covariates in the sense of Kalbfleisch & Prentice (2002, ch. 6) [V-sec]. They are generated by the subject and are not external to the failure process. |
| Successes | no absorption by `T` | administrative right-censoring at `T` (follow-up ends with the run) |
| Failures at L1 | `A_T = 1`, time unknown | **current-status observation** at inspection time `T` (Huang & Wellner 1997 [V-sec]; Jewell & van der Laan 2004 [V]) |
| Attribution posterior | `pi_tau(t)` | the conditional distribution of a current-status event time given the covariate path: the E-step of an EM for interval-censored data *(derivation)* |
| Online monitor | cumulative hazard / `1 - prod_{s<=t}(1-h_s)` | dynamic prediction / landmarking; cumulative incidence `F(t \| tau_{<=t})` |

**Log-likelihood contribution of one trajectory, by supervision level** *(derivation; the L2 row is the standard Allison / Kvamme–Borgan likelihood)*:

| Level | What is observed on the run | log L |
|---|---|---|
| success (any level) | `A_T = 0` | `sum_{s<=T} log(1-h_s)` |
| L2 (`k_lock = k`) | exact event at `k` | `sum_{s<k} log(1-h_s) + log h_k` |
| L1 | `A_T = 1` | `log(1 - prod_{s<=T}(1-h_s))` = **noisy-OR bag likelihood** |
| L1 + symptom (if `t_lock <= t_obs`) | event by `t_obs` | `log(1 - prod_{s<=t_obs}(1-h_s))`; later steps carry no information about the event time |
| L1+ (restart from `t_a` can still succeed, restart from `t_b` cannot) | `t_a < event <= t_b` | `sum_{s<=t_a} log(1-h_s) + log(1 - prod_{t_a<s<=t_b}(1-h_s))` = **interval censoring** |

Restarts are noisy inspections, because a continuation success rate `p_hat` from `n` rollouts only estimates whether `A_t = 0`. The exact version is a misclassified-current-status likelihood with sensitivity set by `n` and by the success probability of a non-absorbed continuation *(derivation)*. ELPO's and Shen et al.'s binary-search restarts are a sequential design of inspection times for exactly this likelihood.

### 2.2 Known estimators

- **Person-period logistic regression** (Allison 1982, *Sociological Methodology 1982*, ed. Leinhardt, chapter on discrete-time methods [V-sec]). Each run contributes one row per step up to the event or censoring time; the model is fitted with binary cross-entropy. This is the L2 estimator.
- **Neural logistic hazard** (Nnet-survival, Gensheimer & Narasimhan 2019; Kvamme & Borgan 2021; pycox `LogisticHazard`) and **PMF parametrisations** (DeepHit, MTLR). Kvamme & Borgan find the hazard parametrisation slightly better than PMF [V].
- **Recurrent and attention hazards over sequences:** RNN-SURV, DRSA, Dynamic-DeepHit (longitudinal input, competing risks, attention over the history).
- **Controlled-differential-equation intensities:** Bleistein et al. ICML 2024 fit a neural CDE, and its linearised signature version CoxSig, as the intensity of a counting process. This is the direct survival counterpart of OAT's NCDE.
- **For L1 (current status):** either direct SGD on the noisy-OR likelihood, or EM. *(derivation)* The E-step weight of step `t` in failed run `tau` is the posterior `pi_tau(t)`, and the M-step is a weighted person-period logistic fit. So **the attribution posterior is the E-step of the fit itself**, as in classic MIL-EM.

### 2.3 Known identifiability issues and how they show up on the benchmarks

- **I1. Current status = noisy-OR MIL.** `P(S=0|tau) = 1 - prod_t(1-h_t)` is symmetric in the steps. Order enters only through the posterior's `prod_{s<t}` factor, and only if `h` is computed causally.
  - Without event times, *where* the hazard sits is identified only through the function class (an instance-level `h`) and cross-trajectory variation of step features. Whatever step type best predicts failure across runs receives the mass: final answers, failure verbalisations, reactions to empty tool output.
  - The SED pooling study (§3.I1) and the WTAL critiques (§3.H2) document exactly this.
- **I2. Case-only / case-control sampling.** Every core benchmark releases failures only, so successes must be generated separately at another sampling rate. For case-control current-status data, Jewell & van der Laan (2004, *Biometrika* 91:529) show the event-time distribution is identified **only up to a one-parameter family**; knowing the population case/control frequency pins it down [V].
  - *Practical:* record the pass rate of the system that generated the successes and use it as a weight. Otherwise the absolute hazard level, and therefore the shape of `pi_tau`, is partly arbitrary.
- **I3. Internal covariates after absorption.** For internal covariates, the time-dependent Cox model can be considerably biased (Rizopoulos, via R-help 2010) [V-sec]. Cause-specific hazards stay estimable, but predicted cumulative incidences are not valid (Cortese & Andersen 2010) [V-sec].
  - In agents, the post-lock-in tail is strongly outcome-predictive. Failure-as-a-Process reports: repairs the wrong problem 24% of runs, keeps repeating 15%, verification theatre 28%, fabricated success 15%, gives up 18%.
  - An L1 hazard therefore rises on symptoms. The prefix-only input `h_t(tau_{<t}, s_t)` does **not** prevent this, because the content of `s_t` itself reveals that absorption has already happened.
  - *Remedies:* make absorption a latent state and model post-absorption steps as **emissions** of that state rather than as hazard inputs (§2.5); truncate at `t_obs`; calibrate on a small `k_lock` subset.
- **I4. Outcome-revealing last step.** The final answer, or the `C` completion-claim step in Failure-as-a-Process, is close to a deterministic function of `S` given the verifier. It lets the model explain `S` without using any earlier step.
  - *Remedies:* mask the last `k` steps, or treat the final step as a noisy *measurement* of the state.
  - This is the WTAL "most discriminative snippet" problem in its purest form.
- **I5. Frailty / task difficulty.** With unobserved heterogeneity, the population hazard falls over time even when every individual hazard is constant (Vaupel, Manton & Stallard 1979, *Demography* 16:439, DOI 10.2307/2061224) [V-sec]. Even under randomisation, hazard ratios are not causal because survivors are a selected group (Aalen, Cook & Røysland 2015, DOI 10.1007/s10985-015-9335-y [V-sec]; Martinussen, Vansteelandt & Andersen 2020, DOI 10.1007/s10985-020-09501-5 [V-sec]).
  - In agents: hard tasks fail early and late steps are populated by easy tasks. Ord's Weibull reanalysis of METR horizons (shape `k ≈ 0.6 < 1`, decreasing hazard) fits a frailty explanation. That reading is my *inference*; Ord gives no mechanism.
  - *Remedies:* query embedding `h_Q` as a covariate; a shared per-task frailty, which Failure-as-a-Process's 21 runs per task make estimable; matched designs.
- **I6. Event definition.** The L1 hazard targets the first *fatal* step (`k_lock`), not the counterfactual `k*`. The gap is large on the datasets:
  - Failure-as-a-Process fix window `t_lock - t_err`: median 1, mean 6.6, 44% at least 3 steps.
  - AgentRx: the root cause is not the first failure event in 27% of public trajectories (41% of Magentic-One).
  
  Scoring an L1 hazard against Who&When `k*` therefore builds in an irreducible error, which a multi-state model can reduce.
- **I7. Self-conditioning.** Errors in context raise later error rates (Sinha et al., ICLR 2026). The hazard of a fatal error depends on the run's own earlier non-fatal errors. A single-absorbing-event model conflates the first error with the error cascade.
- **I8. Competing risks.** From (first time, cause), the dependence between latent cause-specific failure times is not identifiable (Tsiatis 1975, *PNAS* 72:20, DOI 10.1073/pnas.72.1.20) [V-sec]. Report cause-specific hazards and cumulative incidences, never "the time mode m would have struck without mode m'".
- **I9. Cure fraction.** The cure fraction is identified only with **sufficient follow-up**: the support of event times among susceptibles must lie inside the follow-up window, which shows up as a plateau (Patilea & Van Keilegom, AoS 2020; Weng & Engelhard 2026) [V].
  - In a time-to-success framing, step caps violate this (AgentErrorBench caps runs at 30 steps).

### 2.4 What competing risks add

Cause-specific discrete hazards `h_t^{(m)}`, `m = 1..M`, come from one softmax over {no event, m_1, ..., m_M} (the DeepHit / SurvTRACE / Dynamic-DeepHit construction):

```
P(S=1 | tau)                   = prod_t (1 - sum_m h_t^{(m)})
P(t*=t, m*=m | S=0, tau)       = h_t^{(m)} prod_{s<t}(1 - sum_m' h_s^{(m')}) / P(S=0 | tau)
P(S=0, m*=m | tau)             = sum_t h_t^{(m)} prod_{s<t}(1 - sum_m' h_s^{(m')})     # mode known, step unknown
```

- **Mode as the cause:** Who&When Pro (17 modes, injected, step labelled), AgentRx (9 categories), TRAIL (21 leaves), TrajErrBench (20 types), MAST (14 modes per trace, no step, no outcome).
- **Agent as the cause** *(derivation)*: set `h_t^{(a)} = 0` unless `a_t = a` (a structural zero). "Who" is then the cumulative incidence by agent. Aegis's `(agent, mode)` labels without a step are the third likelihood line above: competing risks with a known cause and an unknown time.
- **Per Tsiatis:** cause-specific hazards are identified from (time, cause) data; latent "net" mode-specific failure times are not.

### 2.5 What multi-state models add

Take latent states `Z_t ∈ {H healthy, E erred-recoverable, L locked-in, O locked-and-observed}`. Transitions: `H→E` at an error, `E→H` on recovery, `E→L` at lock-in, `L→O` at the first symptom. The outcome is `S = 0` iff `Z_T ∈ {L, O}`.

- **Fitting.** Transition probabilities `P(Z_t | Z_{t-1}, tau_{<=t})` come from a network. Steps are emissions `P(s_t | Z_t, tau_{<t})`, so post-lock tails are explained by state `L` rather than by hazard (this addresses I3). The model is fitted by the forward algorithm on outcome-only data: an input–output HMM with absorbing states *(derivation)*.
- **Outputs.** Forward–backward gives separate posteriors for `t_err` (last `H→E` entry before `L`), `t_lock` (`E→L`) and `t_obs` (`L→O`, or none for silent failures). These are exactly Failure-as-a-Process's three timestamps.
- **Recovered errors.** These are `H→E→H` paths. Failure-as-a-Process PASS runs carry `num_error_events` (mean 2.29), `first_error_step` and `num_recoveries`, so the recovery intensity is directly calibratable.
- **Ordering.** The constraint `t_err <= t_lock <= t_obs` is what MENSA's "trajectory-based likelihood" encodes for multi-event survival [V]. The Kolmogorov-forward neural version is Groha et al. [V].
- **Agent precedent.** Absorbing Markov chains are already fitted to agent traces, for reliability (arXiv:2604.24579) and safety (SafetyDrift, arXiv:2603.27148) [V], though with states clustered from observed actions rather than latent error states.

### 2.6 What cure models (and a zero-time atom) add

There are two different mixtures, and both matter:

1. **Doomed-at-start atom (failure framing)** *(derivation)*:
   - `P(S=0 | x, tau) = pi_0(x) + (1 - pi_0(x)) (1 - prod_t (1-h_t))`.
   - Here `pi_0` is the probability that the task is infeasible for this system: under-specified query, broken environment, or beyond capability (cf. "futile reasoning", arXiv:2607.29211 [V]).
   - The attribution posterior gains a "no step / task-level" outcome, `P(t*=∅ | S=0) = pi_0 / P(S=0)`. This is MIL's "null instance" and WTAL's background class.
   - It gives the model a legal place for task-difficulty failures, which otherwise get spread over the earliest steps.
2. **Mixture cure (time-to-success framing):**
   - `P(T_succ > t) = pi + (1 - pi) S_u(t)`, with `pi` the never-solvable fraction ("task solvability").
   - It is identifiable only with sufficient follow-up, i.e. a step budget beyond the support of success times (I9).
   - Weng & Engelhard (2026) show the mixture cure model is a PU model whose labelling propensity is parametrised as a time-to-event distribution. PU learning matches it except under selective censoring [V].
   - With repeated runs per task (Failure-as-a-Process: 21 systems per task; tau2 trials), `pi` per task is estimable from pass@k across systems and seeds.

## Methods

Every entry has the same parts: citation; mechanism; the mapping (a)–(f) from brief §7; supervision level; what breaks; code.

In the mappings, "**PP**" means the position-prior baseline the method must beat. PP is the life-table hazard `h^0(t) = #{k = t} / #{k >= t}` estimated from labelled event steps, or `h^0(t/T)` when pooling across datasets. Its posterior gives the brief's first-3 / position-only numbers (Who&When: first-3 hit 0.36, position-only AUROC 0.60).

### A. Discrete-time and sequence survival models

#### A1. Nnet-survival / logistic hazard (Gensheimer & Narasimhan 2019; Kvamme & Borgan 2021) [V]
- **Citation:**
  - Gensheimer & Narasimhan, "A scalable discrete-time survival model for neural networks", arXiv:1805.00917, *PeerJ* 7:e6257 (2019), DOI 10.7717/peerj.6257.
  - Kvamme & Borgan, "Continuous and discrete-time survival prediction with neural networks", arXiv:1910.06724, *Lifetime Data Analysis* 27(4):710–736 (2021), DOI 10.1007/s10985-021-09532-6.
- **Mechanism:**
  - A network outputs one logit per time interval: `h_j(x) = sigma(phi_j(x))`.
  - Loss = negative right-censored log-likelihood: `-sum_i [ sum_{j<kappa_i} log(1-h_j(x_i)) + d_i log h_{kappa_i}(x_i) + (1-d_i) log(1-h_{kappa_i}(x_i)) ]`.
  - The baseline hazard and the covariate effects can both vary with time. Training is minibatch SGD.
  - Kvamme & Borgan compare hazard (LogisticHazard) and PMF parametrisations; hazard is slightly better. For continuous data they discretise at quantiles of the event-time distribution and interpolate; they also propose PC-Hazard (piecewise constant).
  - pycox implements LogisticHazard ("also called partial logistic regression or Nnet-survival"), PMF, DeepHit, MTLR, PCHazard, BCESurv, plus `concordance_td`, IPCW Brier/IBS, NBLL and administrative-censoring variants [V, pycox README].
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) `x_t` = a GRU/causal-transformer summary of `tau_{<t}` plus the step embedding of `s_t` (OAT's `h_t`, or a cheap proxy-LM embedding). Alternatives: step-type tokens (Failure-as-a-Process `E/P/W/X/V/C/R/O`), agent id, observation flags (exception, empty return, repeated call).
  - (b) L2 when `k_lock` is known (AgentRx; Failure-as-a-Process `t_lock`); L1 with the noisy-OR row of §2.1.
  - (c) per-step hazard `h_t`, posterior `pi_tau(t)`, cumulative incidence.
  - (d) `k_lock` / `t_lock` (AgentRx exact/±k; Failure-as-a-Process `t_lock` exact/±k plus RPS); secondarily `k*` on Who&When / TrajErrBench (step acc, ±k).
  - (e) PP life-table hazard, and the step-index-only logistic hazard (identical in the limit).
  - (f) I1–I4 in §2.3; the time axis must be `t`, not `t/T` (`T` is only known at the end, so `t/T` leaks the future, as OAT's spline does).
- **Supervision:** L2 natively; L1 via the current-status likelihood (not in the package, but a 5-line loss).
- **Breaks:** with no event times the package's loss does not apply; I1 symmetry; the last-step shortcut.
- **Code:** pycox (`havakv/pycox`); Keras code with Nnet-survival.

#### A2. DeepHit (Lee, Zame, Yoon, van der Schaar, AAAI 2018) [V record; mechanism V-sec]
- **Citation:** "DeepHit: A deep learning approach to survival analysis with competing risks", AAAI-32, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11842.
- **Mechanism:**
  - A shared sub-network feeds cause-specific sub-networks.
  - One softmax over (time bin × cause) gives the joint PMF `P(T=t, K=k | x)`.
  - Loss = `alpha * L_likelihood + (1-alpha) * L_rank`. The ranking term penalises pairs where the subject with the earlier event does not get the higher cumulative incidence at that time; it uses an `exp(-(F_i - F_j)/sigma)` surrogate (per pycox/autoprognosis docs).
  - No assumption on the stochastic process.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) static trajectory summary, or prefix summary for landmark use.
  - (b) L2 for the PMF, or L1 with the current-status sum over `t`.
  - (c) joint (step, mode) PMF = the competing-risk posterior of §2.4.
  - (d) Who&When Pro (step + 17 modes), AgentRx (step + 9 categories), TrajErrBench (step + 20 types): joint accuracy, step acc, mode macro-F1.
  - (e) PP times the marginal mode prior.
  - (f) the ranking loss compares *across* trajectories, but attribution is *within*-trajectory; the PMF form does not factor into a causal hazard, so it is unusable as an online monitor without re-normalising on the risk set.
- **Supervision:** L2 (or L1 with a modified loss).
- **Breaks:** PMF over relative position is non-causal.
- **Code:** pycox `DeepHit`, `DeepHitSingle`.

#### A3. Dynamic-DeepHit (Lee, Yoon, van der Schaar, IEEE TBME 67(1), 2020) [V record + code]
- **Citation:** "Dynamic-DeepHit: A deep learning approach for dynamic survival analysis with competing risks based on longitudinal data", DOI 10.1109/TBME.2019.2909027 (IEEE doc 8681104).
- **Mechanism** (read from `class_DeepLongitudinal.py`):
  - An RNN over the longitudinal measurements, with missing-indicator inputs.
  - Attention over past hidden states, keyed on the last measurement; the context vector is concatenated with `x_last`.
  - Cause-specific sub-networks feed a softmax over (event, time).
  - Loss = `a * L1 + b * L2 + c * L3`:
    - `L1` is the log-likelihood **conditioned on survival past the last measurement time `t_M`** (the denominator is `1 - sum P(T <= t_M)`): "uncensored: log P(T=t, K=k | x, Y, t > t_M); censored: log sum P(T > t | x, Y, t > t_M)".
    - `L2` is a DeepHit-style ranking loss.
    - `L3` is a **next-measurement prediction** loss on observed features. There is a burn-in phase on `L3` alone.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) per-step embeddings and observation features.
  - (b) L2 (`k_lock`), or L1 with modification.
  - (c) cause-specific CIF from any landmark `t`.
  - (d) monitor: lead time vs `t_lock`, incident AUC per step (Failure-as-a-Process timestamps; AgentRx `k_lock`). Attribution via the PMF on AgentRx / Who&When Pro.
  - (e) PP and a landmark-only (`x_last`) model.
  - (f) **conditioning on "alive at `t_M`" assumes the event is observable**. For agents the absorption is latent (silent failures: 28% in Failure-as-a-Process), so the risk set at a landmark is unknown and must be replaced by the filtered `P(A_t = 0 | tau_{<=t})` *(derivation)*.
- **Note:** OAT's objective is essentially `L3` alone, trained on successes. Proposal 2 at L1 = `L1` with current-status censoring. A **semi-supervised OAT** = `L3` on all runs plus `L1` on outcomes (+ `L2` ranking) is the natural ablation ladder.
- **Supervision:** L2 / L1.
- **Breaks:** latent risk set; non-causal attention if keys look ahead (they do not here: only the history plus the last observation are used).
- **Code:** github.com/chl8856/Dynamic-DeepHit (TF 1.13); also the `tempor` plugin.

#### A4. RNN-SURV (Giunchiglia, Nemchenko, van der Schaar, ICANN 2018) and DRSA (Ren et al., AAAI 2019)
- **Citations:**
  - RNN-SURV, "A deep recurrent model for survival analysis", ICANN 2018, LNCS [V-sec via the Wiegrebe et al. review, arXiv:2305.14961, *AI Review* 57:65 (2024)].
  - DRSA, "Deep Recurrent Survival Analysis", arXiv:1809.02403 [V].
- **Mechanism:**
  - Both unroll a recurrent network over the **time axis** and emit a conditional hazard (DRSA) or a survival probability plus risk score (RNN-SURV) at every step.
  - The likelihood multiplies `(1-h)` up to the event (or censoring) and `h` at the event; DRSA adds a censored-data term. Neither assumes a distribution form.
  - In both, the *covariate* is mostly static and repeated over the time index. They model the shape of time, not a time-varying covariate stream (DRSA's abstract: "models sequential patterns in the feature space").
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) static task/trajectory features; useful as a *time-only* baseline with a task embedding.
  - (b)–(c) L2 hazard per step.
  - (d) Failure-as-a-Process `t_lock` distribution per (scaffold, model, task category).
  - (e) PP.
  - (f) no real step content, so it is a richer position prior, not an attributor.
- **Supervision:** L2.
- **Breaks:** cannot localise within a trajectory beyond position.
- **Code:** DRSA released by its authors [U link]; RNN-SURV [U].

#### A5. SurvTRACE (Wang & Sun, ACM-BCB 2022) [V]
- **Citation:** "SurvTRACE: Transformers for survival analysis with competing events", arXiv:2110.00855, DOI 10.1145/3535508.3545521.
- **Mechanism:**
  - A transformer over **covariate tokens** (tabular, not time series).
  - Competing-event heads with no distributional assumption.
  - Multi-task auxiliary objectives so the transformer can be trained from scratch on survival data.
  - Handling of selection bias from implicit confounders.
  - Attention-based covariate importance.
  - The hazard parametrisation is not shown on the abstract page [U on details].
- **Mapping:** relevant only as the *competing-events head* design and as evidence that transformers need auxiliary tasks at survival-data scale (184–2,226 failed runs per dataset). (a)–(e) as in A2. (f) not a sequence model over steps.
- **Supervision:** L2.
- **Breaks:** "survival as sequence modelling" here means tokens = covariates, not steps.
- **Code:** [U] (only aggregator links on the abs page).

#### A6. Dynamical survival analysis with controlled latent states: neural CDE / CoxSig (Bleistein, Nguyen, Fermanian, Guilloux, ICML 2024) [V]
- **Citation:** arXiv:2401.17077.
- **Mechanism:**
  - The intensity of a counting process is the solution of a **controlled differential equation driven by the observed irregular time series**: `dz = f(z) dX_t`, `lambda(t) = g(z_t)`.
  - The authors give a neural-CDE estimator and, under regularity conditions, a linearisation in **signature** space (CoxSig). Theoretical learning guarantees for both.
  - Experiments: simulations, finance, **predictive maintenance**, food supply chain.
- **Mapping:**
  - This is OAT's NCDE with the reconstruction head replaced by an **intensity head trained on outcomes**.
  - (a) OAT's `h_t` and the time channel.
  - (b) L2 (event times), or L1 with the current-status likelihood.
  - (c) intensity path → hazard → posterior.
  - (d) as A1.
  - (e) PP; OAT (same encoder).
  - (f) OAT's critique applies verbatim: the natural cubic spline is non-causal, so use a causal (rectilinear/linear-in-the-past) control path for monitoring. Steps are not an irregular continuous process.
- **Supervision:** L2 / L1.
- **Breaks:** the spline and smoothness bias; tiny-data regime.
- **Code:** [U] (not linked from the abs page).
- **Why it matters:** it is the cleanest single ablation that separates "CDE encoder" from "one-class objective" in OAT.

#### A7. Landmarking (van Houwelingen 2007) [V-sec]
- **Citation:** "Dynamic prediction by landmarking in event history analysis", *Scand. J. Statist.* 34:70–85, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9469.2006.00529.x.
- **Mechanism:**
  - At landmark time `s`, fit the model only on subjects still at risk at `s`, with covariates frozen at their value at `s`.
  - Predict `P(T <= s + w | T > s, x(s))` for a fixed window `w`.
  - Stack landmarks into a "super-model" with coefficients smooth in `s`.
  - It avoids joint modelling of the covariate process.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) features of `tau_{<=s}`.
  - (b) L2 (needs to know who is still at risk at `s`, i.e. `k_lock`).
  - (c) windowed risk `1 - S(s+w)/S(s)`, exactly Time-to-Inconsistency's monitor rule (K1).
  - (d) Failure-as-a-Process monitor targets (lead time to `t_lock`, recall, false-alarm on PASS), but only with re-collected step text. AgentRx `k_lock`.
  - (e) PP and Failure-as-a-Process's prompted monitor (82% precision, 18% recall, median lead 0).
  - (f) the at-risk set is latent for agents (silent failures). Using "not yet observed failing" (`t_obs`) instead of `t_lock` defines a different, later event.
- **Supervision:** L2.
- **Breaks:** latent risk set.
- **Code:** R `dynpred`, `mstate` [U links].

#### A8. DeepSurv (Katzman et al. 2018) [V]
- **Citation:** arXiv:1606.00931, *BMC Med. Res. Methodol.* (2018), DOI 10.1186/s12874-018-0482-1.
- **Mechanism:** a neural Cox model; the partial likelihood over risk sets gives a proportional-hazards risk score.
- **Mapping:**
  - Continuous-time and proportional hazards; the time-varying-covariate extension is not native.
  - At most usable as a *trajectory-level* risk score (which tasks/systems fail sooner): (d) AUROC of `P(S=0)` and C-index of `t_lock` across Failure-as-a-Process runs.
  - (f) proportional hazards is violated when a covariate's effect changes over steps (Time-to-Inconsistency found PH violations for drift covariates).
- **Supervision:** L2.
- **Breaks:** PH; no step attribution.
- **Code:** pycox `CoxPH`.

### B. Competing risks and multi-state models

#### B1. Cause-specific hazards, Fine–Gray, and the Putter–Fiocco–Geskus tutorial [V-sec]
- **Citations:**
  - Putter, Fiocco & Geskus, "Tutorial in biostatistics: competing risks and multi-state models", *Stat. Med.* 26:2389–2430 (2007), DOI 10.1002/sim.2712.
  - Fine & Gray, "A proportional hazards model for the subdistribution of a competing risk", *JASA* 94:496–509 (1999), DOI 10.2307/2670170.
  - Tsiatis 1975 (I8).
- **Mechanism:**
  - Cause-specific hazards `h^{(m)}(t)` act on the risk set of subjects without any event yet. The cumulative incidence `F_m(t) = sum_{s<=t} h^{(m)}(s) S(s-1)` depends on *all* cause-specific hazards.
  - Fine–Gray models the subdistribution hazard so that covariates act directly on `F_m`.
  - Multi-state models generalise this to transition intensities between states (illness–death etc.), with the `mstate` software.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) as A1.
  - (b) L2 for (step, mode); L1 with **mode-only** labels (Aegis `(agent, mode)`, MAST modes) via the third line of §2.4.
  - (c) `F_m(t)` per trajectory: a step × mode heat map.
  - (d) Who&When Pro (step acc + mode acc), Aegis (agent + mode F1, no step), AgentRx (category + step), TRAIL (category per span).
  - (e) PP times the mode marginal; an LLM-judge mode classifier.
  - (f) I8 nonidentifiability. With internal covariates, cumulative-incidence prediction from cause-specific hazards is invalid (Cortese & Andersen 2010 [V-sec]). Modes in MAST are multi-label per trace, i.e. not mutually exclusive first events, so competing risks are the wrong model there and MENSA-style multi-event is the right one.
- **Supervision:** L2 / mode-only L1.
- **Code:** R `cmprsk`, `mstate`; pycox DeepHit.

#### B2. Neural multi-state and multi-event survival: survNODE (Groha, Schmon, Gusev 2020/21) and MENSA (Lillelund et al., ML4H 2025) [V]
- **Citations:**
  - Groha et al., "A general framework for survival analysis and multi-state modelling" (v1 title "Neural ODEs for multi-state survival analysis"), arXiv:2006.04893.
  - Lillelund et al., "MENSA: A multi-event network for survival analysis with trajectory-based likelihood estimation", arXiv:2409.06525.
- **Mechanism:**
  - **survNODE** parametrises the **Kolmogorov forward equations** `dP/dt = P Q(t, x)`, with a neural transition-rate matrix whose rows sum to zero. History enters through auxiliary memory states that follow their own ODE.
  - **MENSA** jointly learns time-to-event distributions for several non-exclusive or semi-competing events, with a **trajectory-based likelihood term that encodes the temporal ordering of events**.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) per-step features.
  - (b) L2 with Failure-as-a-Process's three timestamps (FAIL) plus PASS error and recovery counts. L1 on re-collected Terminal-Bench runs.
  - (c) the occupation probabilities of H/E/L/O per step, and separate posteriors for `t_err`, `t_lock`, `t_obs`.
  - (d) Failure-as-a-Process: exact/±k accuracy on each timestamp, and silent-failure detection (`t_obs = null`) AUROC.
  - (e) PP per timestamp (Failure-as-a-Process medians 7 / 12 / 16).
  - (f) the discrete-step analogue is an absorbing HMM, not an ODE. MENSA's ordering likelihood needs `t_err <= t_lock <= t_obs`, which Failure-as-a-Process violates for some runs (negative observability lag).
- **Supervision:** L2 (timestamps) → L1.
- **Code:** MENSA at github.com/thecml/mensa; survNODE [U].

#### B3. Absorbing Markov chains fitted to agent traces (2026) [V]
- **Citations:**
  - Tran-Truong & Le, "Measuring the unmeasurable: Markov chain reliability for LLM agents", arXiv:2604.24579 (cs.SE).
  - Dhodapkar & Pishori, "SafetyDrift", arXiv:2603.27148 (cs.CR).
- **Mechanism:**
  - (i) An absorbing DTMC over clustered agent states with success and failure absorbing states. Laplace-smoothed MLE transitions; AIC and KS goodness-of-fit; Dirichlet/bootstrap intervals. The first-passage distribution subsumes pass@k, pass^k and the reliability decay curve. Held-out first-passage CDFs are accepted on all 7 MAST-style frameworks.
  - (ii) Safety risk as an absorbing chain with monotone risk states. Reported: 85% violation within 5 steps from a mild-risk state in communication tasks vs <5% in technical tasks. The monitor detects 94.7% of violations with **3.7 steps of advance warning** on 357 traces.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) a discrete state token per step (clustered action type), i.e. the HMM/transition baseline in `notes/alternatives.md` item 3.
  - (b) L1 (outcomes as absorbing states).
  - (c) first-passage distribution, point-of-no-return states.
  - (d) monitor lead time vs `t_lock`; `k_lock` as the first entry into a state from which the failure-absorption probability exceeds 1−ε.
  - (e) PP.
  - (f) states are observed clusters, not latent error states. "Point of no return" is a property of the state, not of a step's content.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** [U].

### C. Cure models

#### C1. Mixture-cure identifiability (Patilea & Van Keilegom, AoS 2020) and the cure-appropriateness tutorial (2026) [V]
- **Citations:**
  - Patilea & Van Keilegom, "A general approach for cure models in survival analysis", arXiv:1701.03769, *Ann. Statist.* 48(4) (2020), DOI 10.1214/19-AOS1889. Parametric (e.g. logistic) cure proportion, unspecified survival of the uncured, identifiability shown.
  - Mudunkotuwa, Ghosh & Selukar, "A tutorial for evaluating cure model appropriateness", arXiv:2605.04999 (abs [V]; the sufficient-follow-up condition `tau_{F0} <= tau_G` comes from the PDF via a search snippet [V-sec]).
  - Amico & Van Keilegom's 2018 *Annual Review* "Cure models in survival analysis" [U DOI; the ISBA discussion-paper version is V-sec].
- **Mechanism:** `S_pop(t | x) = pi(x) + (1-pi(x)) S_u(t | x)`. It is identified only if follow-up covers the support of the uncured event times (a plateau in KM).
- **Mapping** (time-to-success framing):
  - (a) task features `h_Q`, plus system id.
  - (b) L1 (success step, or censoring at the cap).
  - (c) per-task solvability `1 - pi`.
  - (d) no attribution label. Use it to *remove* doomed tasks before attribution, and validate on Failure-as-a-Process environment-layer failures and on capability-overreach tasks.
  - (e) per-task pass rate across systems.
  - (f) step caps (AgentErrorBench at 30) break sufficient follow-up.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** R `smcure`, `npcure` [U].

#### C2. Mixture cure ≡ PU learning with a time-to-event propensity (Weng & Engelhard 2026) [V]
- **Citation:** "When are time-to-event models a waste of time? Bridging mixture cure models and positive-unlabeled learning for binary classification under right-censoring", arXiv:2609.19370.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Shared likelihood `sum_i [s_i log(e(x_i) p(x_i)) + (1-s_i) log(1 - e(x_i) p(x_i))]`, with `p(x)` the outcome probability and `e(x)` the labelling propensity.
  - SCAR PU sets `e = c`. The mixture cure model sets `e(x) = P(T < C | Z=1, x)` from the event-time and censoring distributions.
  - Time-to-event constraints mitigate but do not remove the scaling/swapping ambiguity. Guaranteed identifiability needs sufficient follow-up.
  - The paper uses a **discrete-time NN** with a final "beyond horizon" bin as the cure probability (10 bins).
  - Finding: the mixture cure model wins only under selective censoring with unidentifiable censoring features; otherwise PU matches it.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a)–(c) the doomed-at-start atom of §2.6 is the PU class prior of "failed for task-level reasons".
  - (d) none direct.
  - (f) the message for us: **if timestamps are unreliable** (low agreement on `k*`), a PU/MIL formulation without time structure may do as well as the hazard. That is a falsifiable prediction for Proposal 2.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** [U].

### D. Heterogeneity, selection and current-status identifiability

#### D1. Frailty and the causal reading of hazards [V-sec]
- **Citations:**
  - Vaupel, Manton & Stallard 1979, *Demography* 16(3):439–454, DOI 10.2307/2061224.
  - Aalen, Cook & Røysland 2015, *Lifetime Data Anal.* 21(4):579–593, DOI 10.1007/s10985-015-9335-y.
  - Martinussen, Vansteelandt & Andersen 2020, *Lifetime Data Anal.* 26(4):833–855, DOI 10.1007/s10985-020-09501-5 (preprint arXiv:1810.09192).
- **Mechanism:**
  - Unobserved heterogeneity makes the population hazard fall over time.
  - Survivors at later times are a selected sub-population, so randomisation balance is lost and hazard ratios lose their causal interpretation. The Cox model remains a valid test of no effect.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) task id / `h_Q` as frailty.
  - (b) any level.
  - (c) per-task random effect.
  - (d) check: does the per-step hazard estimated on Failure-as-a-Process (21 runs per task) still decline with `t` after conditioning on task? If not, Ord's decreasing hazard (K3) is selection, not dynamics.
  - (e) PP.
  - (f) on failure-only datasets the frailty is confounded with everything.
- **Code:** R `frailtypack`, `coxme` [U].

#### D2. Case-control current-status data (Jewell & van der Laan 2004) [V]
- **Citation:** "Case-control current status data", *Biometrika* 91(3):529–541 (UC Berkeley WP 116, 2002). Reviews: Jewell & van der Laan WP 113 (2002); Huang & Wellner 1997 [V-sec].
- **Mechanism:**
  - Current-status data: one observes only whether `T <= C` at a single monitoring time `C`.
  - Under case-control sampling, `F` is identified only up to a one-parameter family. Given the population case/control frequency, a weighted NPMLE is the NPMLE.
- **Mapping:**
  - This is the formal statement of I1 + I2.
  - (b) L1 with failures from a benchmark and successes from the data engine.
  - (d)–(e) n/a.
  - (f) **Record the pass rate of the generating system** (e.g. per scaffold × model in Failure-as-a-Process: 19–45%) and weight accordingly.

### E. Remaining-useful-life prediction and prognostics

#### E1. C-MAPSS run-to-failure simulation and the PHM'08 score (Saxena, Goebel, Simon, Eklund, PHM 2008) [V-sec]
- **Citation:** "Damage propagation modeling for aircraft engine run-to-failure simulation", IEEE PHM 2008 (IEEE Xplore doc 4711414; NASA NTRS 20090029214).
- **Mechanism:**
  - Simulated multivariate sensor trajectories from a thermodynamic engine model, with damage propagating until a health index reaches zero. The health index is the minimum of several operational margins.
  - Training series run to failure. Test series are truncated, and the target is the RUL at truncation.
  - PHM'08 asymmetric score `s = sum_i (exp(-d_i/13) - 1)` if `d_i < 0`, `(exp(d_i/10) - 1)` if `d_i >= 0`, with `d` = predicted − true RUL. Late predictions are penalised more [V-sec via later papers].
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) per-step features.
  - (b) L2 when the failure time is known. Failed runs are run-to-failure series **only if failure means `t_lock` or `t_obs`**.
  - (c) remaining steps to lock-in at each prefix.
  - (d) Failure-as-a-Process `t_lock − t` at each step (no step text, so features are action codes); AgentRx `k_lock`.
  - (e) PP: the life-table mean residual life `E[t_lock − t | t_lock > t]`.
  - (f) agent runs do not degrade smoothly. Failure is often a single jump (Failure-as-a-Process fix window median 1), and the "end of life" is latent.
- **Code:** NASA dataset (dashlink resource 740).

#### E2. Unsupervised health index from an LSTM encoder-decoder trained on healthy behaviour (Malhotra et al., KDD PHM workshop 2016) [V]
- **Citation:** "Multi-sensor prognostics using an unsupervised health index based on LSTM encoder-decoder", arXiv:1608.06154.
- **Mechanism:**
  - The LSTM-ED is trained to reconstruct sensor windows from **healthy operation**.
  - Reconstruction error becomes a health index (HI), and HI curves become RUL estimates (matched against training HI curves).
  - Evaluated on turbofan and milling datasets; on an industrial pulverizer the HI correlates with maintenance cost.
- **Mapping:**
  - **This is OAT, eight years earlier and in PHM:** a one-class reconstruction model of "success" whose per-step error is a degradation signal.
  - What PHM adds is the **second stage**, turning HI into a time-to-threshold with a first-predicting-time (FPT) trigger. For agents, the FPT is OAT's conformal threshold and the threshold crossing is `t_obs`-like.
  - (a) OAT `h_t`.
  - (b) L0/L1 (successes only).
  - (c) HI path → predicted remaining steps.
  - (d) lead time vs `t_lock`; prognostic horizon (E3) on AgentRx / Failure-as-a-Process.
  - (e) PP and OAT.
  - (f) symptom bias, exactly as in OAT App. H: reconstruction error rises when the run *looks* broken, not when it *becomes* broken.
- **Code:** [U].

#### E3. Prognostics metrics (Saxena, Celaya, Saha, Saha, Goebel, IJPHM 2010) and the Lei et al. review (MSSP 2018) [V-sec]
- **Citations:**
  - "Metrics for offline evaluation of prognostic performance", *IJPHM* 1(1), DOI 10.36001/ijphm.2010.v1i1.1336.
  - Lei, Li, Guo, Li, Yan & Lin, "Machinery health prognostics: a systematic review from data acquisition to RUL prediction", *MSSP* 104 (2018). Its four stages are acquisition, **HI construction**, **health stage division** and RUL prediction; the FPT wording is [U] for this review.
- **Mechanism** (definitions per NASA's `prognosticsmetricslibrary` and a tutorial; the PDF itself returned 403):
  - **Prognostic horizon:** `PH = t_EoL − t_{i*}`, where `i*` is the first prediction time after which the predicted RUL stays inside `±alpha` bounds around the true RUL (probability mass > beta for probabilistic predictions).
  - **alpha–lambda accuracy:** at `t_lambda = t_P + lambda (t_EoL − t_P)`, the indicator that the prediction is inside the `(1 ± alpha)` cone.
  - **Relative accuracy:** `RA_lambda = 1 − |r*(t_lambda) − r(t_lambda)| / r*(t_lambda)`.
  - **Convergence:** distance from the origin to the centroid of the area under the metric curve.
- **Mapping:**
  - (c) any monitor that outputs predicted steps-to-lock-in.
  - (d) `t_EoL = t_lock` (Failure-as-a-Process; AgentRx `k_lock`); `t_P` = first step (or the FPT).
  - (e) PP mean residual life.
  - (f) needs a regression output (remaining steps), not just an alarm. Agents' short horizons (median 7–33 steps) make `alpha` cones coarse.
- **Code:** github.com/nasa/prognosticsmetricslibrary (MATLAB).

### G. Multiple-instance learning on sequences

#### G1. Attention-based deep MIL (Ilse, Tomczak, Welling, ICML 2018) [V; formula from the paper, not re-read]
- **Citation:** arXiv:1802.04712.
- **Mechanism:**
  - The bag label is Bernoulli, with probability `g(z)` where `z = sum_k a_k h_k`.
  - Attention weights: `a_k = exp(w^T tanh(V h_k)) / sum_j exp(w^T tanh(V h_j))`. The gated variant multiplies `tanh(V h_k)` elementwise by `sigm(U h_k)`.
  - The claim: the weights give "insight into the contribution of each instance to the bag label".
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) step embeddings.
  - (b) L1 (bag = trajectory, label = S).
  - (c) attention weights `a_t` as step scores.
  - (d) Who&When step acc / top-3, MP-Bench nDCG@5, AgentRx set-F1.
  - (e) PP, OAT.
  - (f) **attention is embedding-level pooling.** It is not PAC-learnable for instances (G5) and fails the MIL "presence" test (G4); weights are class-agnostic (G2); in SED it localises worse than linear-softmax (I1). It also ignores order unless positional encodings are added.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** github.com/AMLab-Amsterdam/AttentionDeepMIL [U link; the abs page says code is available].

#### G2. MILLET: inherently interpretable time-series classification via MIL (Early et al., ICLR 2024) [V incl. HTML]
- **Citation:** arXiv:2311.10049.
- **Mechanism:** timepoints are instances, the series is the bag, and five poolings are compared on backbones (FCN, ResNet, InceptionTime):
  - Embedding (GAP): `Y = psi_CLF(mean_j z_j)`.
  - Attention: `Y = psi_CLF(mean_j a_j z_j)`.
  - Instance: `Y = mean_j psi_CLF(z_j)`.
  - Additive: `Y = mean_j psi_CLF(a_j z_j)`.
  - **Conjunctive** (proposed): `Y = mean_j a_j psi_CLF(z_j)`, i.e. attention times *class-specific* instance predictions.

  Results:
  - Conjunctive is best for both accuracy (UCR 85: 0.841→0.846 over GAP) and interpretability.
  - **Attention pooling is poor for interpretability because it is class-agnostic.**
  - Metrics: **AOPCR** (label-free: remove timepoints in predicted-importance order and integrate the drop in class probability) and **NDCG@n** against known discriminative timepoints (synthetic WebTraffic: 10 classes, signatures injected in windows of 36–288 points).
  - Stated limits: sparse explanations miss the middle of long regions (CAM beat MILLET on NDCG@n for InceptionTime); zero padding biases models toward the boundaries; univariate equal-length series only.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) step embeddings plus a positional encoding.
  - (b) L1 (S), or mode labels (Who&When Pro / Aegis) for class-specific scores.
  - (c) a per-step, per-class score: a "failure-mode activation map".
  - (d) MP-Bench **nDCG@5** (graded consensus as relevance; the same metric family MILLET uses), AgentRx / TRAIL set-F1, Who&When step acc.
  - (e) PP, OAT.
  - (f) the **boundary bias from padding** is the MIL analogue of the last-step shortcut. Sparse explanations under-cover multi-step error sets.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** github.com/JAEarly/MILTimeSeriesClassification.

#### G3. TimeMIL (Chen et al., ICML 2024) [V]
- **Citation:** arXiv:2405.03140.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Multivariate time-series classification is recast as weakly supervised MIL.
  - Time-aware MIL pooling on a tokenised transformer with a **learnable wavelet positional token**, so that temporal ordering and correlation enter the pooling.
  - Claims better localisation of patterns of interest.
- **Mapping:** as G2. (a) needs a positional token, and here the position prior *is* the token, so (e) PP must be compared with and without it. (f) a learned positional token can implement the position prior on its own.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** github.com/xiwenc1/TimeMIL (stated as forthcoming on the abs page).

#### G4. Algorithmic unit tests for MIL (Raff & Holt, NeurIPS 2023) [V incl. HTML]
- **Citation:** "Reproducibility in multiple instance learning: a case for algorithmic unit tests", arXiv:2310.17867.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Three synthetic tests. **Presence:** a "poison" signal appears only in negative training bags. **Poisoned threshold:** two concepts are needed. **False-frequency reliance:** test frequencies change.
  - A correct MIL model must not learn "positive unless a negative-only signal is present".
  - All five deep MIL models tested fail at least one: MI-Net, Ilse's MIL-pooling, TransMIL, GNN-MIL, Hopfield. Ilse's pooling fails **Presence** (test AUC 0.000) and **False-Frequency** (0.477), because the final layer can use the *absence* of an instance as positive evidence.
- **Mapping:**
  - The direct agent analogue of "poison" is a **success-only marker**: a verification step before the completion claim (`verification_before_claim` is true in 96.1% of Failure-as-a-Process PASS runs), "tests passed", or a well-formed final-answer template. A model can predict failure from its *absence* and then attribute to no specific step or to the last step.
  - (d) a synthetic test: plant or remove the marker in held-out successes and failures and check that attribution does not move.
  - (f) any L1 model, including Proposal 2's noisy-OR hazard if implemented with trajectory-level features.
- **Supervision:** test harness.
- **Code:** released with the paper [U link].

#### G5. Instance-level PAC learnability of deep MIL (Jang & Kwon, NeurIPS 2024) [V record; results V-sec from the NeurIPS slides]
- **Citation:** "Are multiple instance learning algorithms learnable for instances?", NeurIPS 2024 (proceedings hash 1468ecc3…; no arXiv id found).
- **Mechanism:**
  - A PAC framework for instance-level learnability; bag-level learnability is a prerequisite.
  - **Instance-pooling and conjunctive-pooling MIL are learnable for instances. Embedding-pooling, attention-pooling and additive-pooling are not.**
- **Mapping:**
  - This is a formal reason to prefer Proposal 2's *instance-level* hazard `h_t` (instance pooling through noisy-OR) over attention-MIL on trajectory embeddings.
  - (d)–(e) as G1.
  - (f) learnability is an asymptotic statement; sample sizes here are 10^2–10^3 bags.
- **Supervision:** theory.
- **Code:** n/a.

#### G6. Noisy-OR MIL (MILBoost; Viola, Platt, Zhang, NIPS 2005) [V-sec]
- **Citation:** "Multiple instance boosting for object detection", NIPS 18:1417–1424.
- **Mechanism:**
  - The bag positive probability is noisy-OR, `p_bag = 1 − prod_i (1 − p_i)` (§3.1 "Noisy-OR Boost"; formula per Ali & Saenko, CVPR 2014).
  - Instance weights from the gradient concentrate on the most likely positive instances.
- **Mapping:** identical to Proposal 2's L1 likelihood (§2.1). The survival posterior adds only the earliest-first factor `prod_{s<t}(1-h_s)` and causal evaluation of `h_t`.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** n/a.

### H. Weakly supervised temporal action localisation and video anomaly detection

#### H1. The localisation-by-classification family: UntrimmedNet (CVPR 2017), STPN (CVPR 2018), W-TALC (ECCV 2018) [V]
- **Citations:**
  - Wang, Xiong, Lin & Van Gool, arXiv:1703.03329.
  - Nguyen, Liu, Prasad & Han, arXiv:1712.05080.
  - Paul, Roy & Roy-Chowdhury, arXiv:1807.10418.
- **Mechanism:** each is trained only on video-level labels.
  - **UntrimmedNet:** a classification module plus a selection module (both feed-forward).
  - **STPN:** class-agnostic attention over segments with an L1 **sparsity** loss; T-CAMs; proposals scored by activation × attention.
  - **W-TALC:** **k-max MIL** (class score = mean of the top-`k_i` activations, `k_i = max(1, floor(l_i / 8))`; softmax cross-entropy against the normalised multi-hot label) plus a **co-activity similarity loss** that pulls high-attention features of two videos sharing a class together and away from low-attention features (cosine distance, margin 0.5). Localisation thresholds the per-class activation sequence.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) step embeddings.
  - (b) L1 for outcome; **mode-level labels** for per-mode localisation: train on MAST-Data (14 modes, 1,642 traces) or Aegis (`(agent, mode)`), test step localisation on TRAIL / AgentRx / Who&When Pro.
  - (c) a per-step, per-mode activation sequence and spans.
  - (d) TRAIL span-F1 and category; SearchAuditBench tolerance span `[k_s, k_e]` (hit if inside); Who&When Pro step + mode.
  - (e) PP times the mode prior.
  - (f) W-TALC's `k = T/8` assumes the action covers ≥1/8 of the video, but errors are 0.6–18% of steps, so `k` must be about 1–2. Co-activity needs ≥2 failures of the same mode (fine for Who&When Pro).
- **Supervision:** L1 / mode-L1.
- **Code:** W-TALC and STPN public [U links]; CoLA see H2.

#### H2. The critique: most-discriminative-part and context confusion (Hide-and-Seek ICCV 2017; CMCS CVPR 2019; CoLA CVPR 2021) [V; CMCS V-sec]
- **Citations:**
  - Singh & Lee, arXiv:1704.04232.
  - Liu, Jiang & Wang, "Completeness modeling and context separation for weakly supervised temporal action localization", CVPR 2019, pp. 1298–1307.
  - Zhang et al., "CoLA", arXiv:2103.16392.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Weakly supervised localisers "focus on the most discriminative parts … rather than all relevant parts". **Hide-and-Seek** randomly hides patches/frames during training so the network must find other evidence (nothing hidden at test).
  - **CMCS:** a multi-branch network whose branches discover distinct parts (completeness), plus **hard negatives** mined from static clips to separate action from co-occurring **context** (ActivityNet 1.2 mAP 18.0 → 22.4).
  - **CoLA:** "localisation by classification" scores snippets in isolation and suffers "single-snippet cheating". It fixes this with hard-snippet mining and a snippet contrastive loss.
- **Mapping:** this is the literature's own account of the brief's two failure modes.
  - **Last-step shortcut = the most discriminative part.** Fix: Hide-and-Seek on steps (random step dropout, and *always* hiding the final-answer/claim step for a fraction of batches).
  - **Symptom shift = action–context confusion.** Failure verbalisations and empty-return reactions co-occur with failure but are not the action. Fix: CMCS-style hard negatives = symptom-like steps from *successful* runs (Failure-as-a-Process PASS: 2.29 error events and recoveries per run; `R` recover segments).
  - (d) Who&When / TrajErrBench step acc; Failure-as-a-Process `t_err` vs `t_obs` separation (does the fix move mass earlier?).
  - (e) PP.
  - (f) hiding can delete the true error step in short runs (median 4–10 steps), so hide with probability < 1 per step.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** CoLA github.com/zhang-can/CoLA; CMCS github.com/Finspire13/CMCS-Temporal-Action-Localization.

#### H3. MIL ranking for video anomaly detection (Sultani, Chen, Shah 2018) [V; loss from the paper as commonly reproduced]
- **Citation:** "Real-world anomaly detection in surveillance videos", arXiv:1801.04264 (CVPR 2018 [V-sec]).
- **Mechanism:**
  - Anomalous videos are positive bags and normal videos negative bags of segments.
  - Loss: `max(0, 1 − max_i f(V_a^i) + max_i f(V_n^i)) + lambda_1 sum_i (f(V_a^i) − f(V_a^{i+1}))^2 + lambda_2 sum_i f(V_a^i)` (ranking + temporal smoothness + sparsity).
  - Dataset: 1,900 untrimmed videos, 128 h, 13 anomaly types (UCF-Crime).
- **Mapping:**
  - The cleanest template for "failed vs successful run" with sparse error steps: the top-scored step in a failure must outrank the top-scored step in a **matched success of the same task** (Failure-as-a-Process 89 tasks × 21 systems; AgentHallu 443 failed / 250 clean).
  - (c) per-step anomaly score.
  - (d) Who&When / TraceElephant step acc, MP-Bench nDCG.
  - (e) PP, OAT (OAT is the one-class version of the same idea).
  - (f) the max picks the most discriminative step (H2). The smoothness term spreads mass to neighbours: harmless for ±1 metrics, wrong for exact.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** public [U link].

### I. Weakly supervised sound event detection

#### I1. MIL pooling functions for clip-level labels (Wang, Li, Metze, ICASSP 2019; McFee, Salamon, Bello 2018) [V incl. HTML for Wang et al.]
- **Citations:**
  - "A comparison of five multiple instance learning pooling functions for sound event detection with weak labeling", arXiv:1810.09050.
  - "Adaptive pooling operators for weakly labeled sound event detection", arXiv:1804.10070 (auto-pool interpolates min/mean/max).
- **Mechanism:** frame probabilities `y_i`, pooled to the clip probability `y`.
  - Max: `max y_i` (gradient to one frame only, so frame false negatives).
  - Average: `mean y_i` (equal gradient to all frames, so false positives).
  - **Linear softmax:** `sum y_i^2 / sum y_i`. The gradient is positive only where `y_i > y/2`, which pushes frames to 0/1.
  - Exponential softmax.
  - Attention: `sum w_i y_i / sum w_i`. In negative clips it concentrates weight on low-probability frames, so high-probability frames keep small weights and **remain frame-level false positives**.
  
  DCASE 2017 Task 4 localisation (1-s segments): linear softmax ER 84.3 / F1 43.7, best; max 84.7 / 35.4; attention 102.5 / 40.1.
- **Mapping:**
  - (a) step embeddings.
  - (b) L1.
  - (c) per-step failure probability.
  - (d) step-set metrics: AgentRx / TRAIL set-F1, MP-Bench.
  - (e) PP.
  - (f) **noisy-OR (Proposal 2) behaves like soft max pooling.** Few steps get gradient, so expect under-detection on multi-step error sets, as OAT's hard threshold does. Linear softmax is the empirically better default for *sets*; noisy-OR / hazard is the principled one for the *first* fatal step. Report both.
- **Supervision:** L1.
- **Code:** TALNet / auto-pool public [U links].

#### I2. SED evaluation: collar-based event metrics and PSDS (Mesaros, Heittola, Virtanen 2016; Bilen et al., ICASSP 2020) [V]
- **Citations:**
  - "Metrics for polyphonic sound event detection", *Appl. Sci.* 6(6):162, DOI 10.3390/app6060162 (`sed_eval`).
  - "A framework for the robust evaluation of sound event detection", arXiv:1910.08440.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Event-based metrics count a detection as correct if it matches a same-label reference within a time collar. Segment-based metrics score fixed segments.
  - **PSDS** replaces collars with intersection-based tolerance criteria and integrates a polyphonic ROC over operating points. It is designed to be **robust to labelling subjectivity** and threshold-free.
- **Mapping:** (d) collar = **±k step tolerance**. PSDS-style threshold-free, tolerance-based scoring is the right way to score per-step scores on low-agreement labels (LongRCA exact-step agreement 40%; TraceElephant α = 0.64; only 16% of MP-Bench flags are unanimous) and on tolerance spans (SearchAuditBench). See §4.
- **Code:** `sed_eval`, `psds_eval`.

#### K1. Time-To-Inconsistency (Li, Krishnan, Padman, ICLR 2026) [V incl. HTML]

- **Mechanism:**

## Evaluation transfer

**Notation.** For trajectory `i` with per-step hazards `h_{i,t}`:
- survival `S_i(t) = prod_{s<=t}(1 − h_{i,s})`;
- cumulative incidence `F_i(t) = 1 − S_i(t)`;
- attribution posterior `pi_i(t) = h_{i,t} S_i(t−1) / F_i(T_i)`;
- its CDF `Pi_i(t) = F_i(t) / F_i(T_i)`.

`k_i` is whichever labelled target is being scored (`k*`, `k_lock`, `t_err`, `t_lock` or `t_obs`). Monitors must use **prefix-only** predictions `F_i(t | tau_{i,<=t})`.

### 4.1 Scoring the attribution posterior (within each failed trajectory)

### 4.3 Baselines every table must carry

- **PP posterior:** life-table `h^0(t)` fitted on labelled events of the same dataset (in-domain) and pooled over datasets in relative position (cross-domain).
- **Trivial posteriors:** uniform, last-step, first-step.
- **OAT**, where runnable.
- **For monitors:** the PP hazard (alarm when the population hazard peaks) and the published Failure-as-a-Process prompted monitor.

Survival analysis calls the life-table hazard the null model, so the "beat the position prior" requirement in brief §7(e) is the standard null comparison here, not an extra demand.

## Open gaps

1. **Latent absorbing event.**
   - Survival methods assume that the event, or at least the at-risk status, is observable. The agent lock-in event is latent: 28% of Failure-as-a-Process failures are silent, and none has an observed `t_lock` without annotation.
   - I found no survival or MIL paper on *latent-event* landmarking with language covariates. The nearest pieces are hidden multi-state models and misclassified current-status data, neither applied to text.
2. **No dataset has all four ingredients** (step text, PASS+FAIL runs of the same tasks, `k_lock`, and `k*`).
   - Failure-as-a-Process has timestamps and codes but no text.
   - AgentHallu has clean runs, but its label is a hallucination step, not lock-in.
   - AgentRx has `k_lock` but no successes.
   
   Re-collecting the CodeTracer-sourced 71% of Failure-as-a-Process would close most of this.
3. **Case-control weights are unknown.** The benchmark papers rarely report the pass rate of the system that produced the released failures, which Jewell & van der Laan's result makes necessary for an L1 fit.
4. **Restart-certified points of no return exist for one benchmark only** (SWE-bench Verified, two agents; Shen et al. 2026). Their relation to annotated `k*` / `k_lock` on Who&When-style MAS logs is unmeasured. Those logs are mostly not replayable.
5. **No MIL "unit tests" for agent attribution.** It is unknown whether published attributors, including OAT and LLM judges, rely on the absence of success-only markers.
6. **Competing vs multi-event.** Agent failure modes are not mutually exclusive (MAST is multi-label per trace). No agent dataset gives per-mode onset times, so multi-event models (MENSA) cannot yet be validated.
7. **Frailty vs dynamics.** Whether the declining agent hazard (Weibull `k` ≈ 0.6) is per-run dynamics or task selection is unresolved. Failure-as-a-Process's 89 tasks × 21 systems can test it with a shared-frailty model.
8. **Calibration of step posteriors.** No benchmark reports calibration or proper scores for step attribution, and no threshold-free tolerance metric is standard. All published numbers are point accuracies at one threshold.
9. **Long traces and unit mismatch.**
   - LongRCA (median 145 steps, max 728) and MemTrace (497 ops) make per-step hazards tiny and products long. Compute in log space and consider coarser "episode" clocks.
   - Pooling datasets with different step units (message vs LLM call vs span vs op) breaks a shared hazard clock; relative position is not a valid causal clock (§2.1).
10. **Weak-supervision evidence appears late.** Baidya et al. report that high-relevance evidence first appears after 59–84% of the trajectory (as we read the abstract: relative position), while the brief's labelled `k*` sits at 0.23–0.65 relative position on natural-failure sets. If that gap holds on our data, L1 weak supervision will systematically localise symptoms. Measuring it on Failure-as-a-Process (`t_obs` − `t_err`) is part of recommendation #1.
