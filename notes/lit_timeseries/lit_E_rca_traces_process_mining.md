# Slice E: root-cause analysis on metrics, traces and logs (AIOps), and process mining, read as failure attribution

*Written 2026-10-30 for `notes/lit_timeseries/` (brief: `00_context_brief.md`). Every paper below was checked against arXiv metadata (export.arxiv.org API), Crossref / Semantic Scholar DOI records, or the publisher or author page during this session. Claims taken from a secondary source (a survey or blog summary) are marked as such, and items not confirmed are marked **unverified**. Section 4 and the appendix report two small pilots computed in this session on data already in `data/`.*

## Scope and verdict

1. **Scope.** This file covers root-cause analysis (RCA) of microservice failures from metrics, distributed traces and logs, plus process mining (conformance checking, discovery, Declare, deviance mining). It reads all of it against "which agent, which step" (who/when). There are about 65 verified sources: 25 AIOps/RCA papers, benchmarks and critiques (metrics and traces), 8 on log analysis, 12 on process mining, and 22 from 2024-2026 on LLM agents (attribution, process mining of agent logs, observability, LLM-based RCA agents).
2. **The analogy is real but only partial.** A trajectory is a trace, an agent is a service, and a step is a span. TRAIL literally ships OpenTelemetry/OpenInference span trees, SymFail ships event-dependency DAGs, and MemTrace ships data-lineage DAGs. But agent failures are *semantic*: no latency or error-rate metric moves. Each task runs once, and each system has only tens to hundreds of runs.
3. **Most transferable idea: score a step conditional on its causal parents, not on its whole prefix.** CIRCA's regression-based hypothesis test plus descendant adjustment, the Shapley outlier attribution of Budhathoki et al., and Sage's counterfactuals all separate the *root cause* from *propagated symptoms*. That is exactly OAT's documented failure: it "fires on reactions to empty tool returns" and works closer to a `t_obs` detector. These methods need the dependency graph that SymFail, MemTrace and TRAIL provide, and that TraceCompiler's def-use rule can recover from tool arguments.
4. **Second idea: spectrum-based localisation across many runs** (TraceRCA, MicroRank, then FAMAS for multi-agent systems). It needs repeated runs per task, so it fits the replayable "data engine" systems (tau2-bench, ALFWorld, Terminal-Bench). It does not fit Who&When as released.
5. **Third idea: discrete next-event models and conformance checking are the discrete analogue of OAT.** Examples are DeepLog/LogBERT and alignments, prefix-alignments and Declare. Two pilots run here show the pattern. These decoders beat position priors (Failure-as-a-Process `t_err` exact 0.21 vs 0.10; AgentHallu-BFCL exact 0.28 to 0.36 vs 0.18). But a one-line *step-type prior* ("first write/edit step") reaches 0.41 on Failure-as-a-Process.
6. **Strongest cautionary transfer: the RCA evaluation crisis.** Random "Dummy" selection ties most PC/Granger + PageRank methods (Pham et al., ASE'24). Simple rule-based methods match the state of the art on four public benchmarks (Fang et al., FSE'26). AgentTrace (ICLR'26 workshop) weights *position* features at 0.70 on a synthetic benchmark whose bugs sit at steps 2-3 in 60% of cases. Agent attribution needs the same discipline: position, step-type and random baselines, plus AC@k/Avg@k reporting.
7. **Weakest transfer: process discovery from successes** (Inductive Miner). Its guarantees are soundness and fitness, so with loops and few traces it drifts toward permissive "flower" models that flag nothing. Use frequency-filtered directly-follows graphs, n-gram models or Declare constraints, or align against a *task reference* when one exists (tau2 gold actions, BFCL gold calls, ALFWorld expert plans).
8. **No 2025-2026 paper evaluates an AIOps RCA method unchanged on a failure-attribution benchmark.** FAMAS (spectrum), CHIEF (causal graph) and GraphTracer (dependency graph; **withdrawn** for a methodological error) adapt the ideas but use LLMs for graph building. Agent process-mining papers (COMPASS, Agent Behavior Mining, AgentLTL, TraceCompiler) do not score against `k*`.
9. **Supervision.** Most methods here are L1: they need successful/normal runs, like OAT. RCD-style "failure as intervention" uses the outcome `S` as an explicit indicator node, which is the clean L1 formulation. Spectrum methods are L1 over replays. Alignment against a task reference needs no outcome at all: L0 plus a specification.
10. **Recommendation.** Build three cheap baselines first: step-type prior, first deviation from a reference or success model, and parent-conditional anomaly with descendant adjustment on OAT's own embeddings. Then build one medium project: spectrum attribution on tau2-bench replays. Report Dummy and position baselines everywhere (section 5).

**Where the analogy breaks.**
- **No metrics, no SLOs.** RCA localises a fault through *numerical* deviations of latency, error rate and saturation that physically propagate along the call graph. An agent error is a wrong fact, plan or argument, visible only to a text model or to the final verifier. The only metric-like signals are observation features: tool error flags, empty returns, return length, repeated queries, token NLL. Failure-as-a-Process's `forewarning_*` fields list them.
- **The fault is not an exogenous intervention.** RCA benchmarks inject a fault into one service (CPU hog, delay, loss). RCD's whole logic treats the failure as an intervention whose *target* is the root cause. An agent's decisive error is drawn from the same policy that produces its successes. Under that policy, mistakes are typical actions, which is the OAT critique in the brief, so "distribution shift at the root cause" is weaker.
- **One trace per task, few traces per system.** Spectrum methods compare many normal and abnormal traces through the *same* element under the *same* fault. Microservices emit thousands of traces per minute. Agent datasets have one run per task. Failure-as-a-Process has 610 successes over 21 systems (about 29 each), and AgentHallu has 250 clean runs over 7 frameworks. Re-runs must be bought (FAMAS replays each failed task 20 times).
- **Control graph vs causal graph.** A span tree records *who called whom*. RCA wants *what depended on what*. In an LLM agent every earlier token is in the context, so potential dependencies are dense, and the useful sparse graph must be estimated. TraceCompiler's def-use rule recovers producer-consumer edges at 0.928 precision / 0.943 recall, against 0.712 F1 for a directly-follows heuristic.
- **Ground truth is soft.** Injected RCA faults have exact labels. Agent `k*` has 40-78% exact human agreement, so every method's ceiling is lower and the evaluation noise floor is higher.
- **Time is an index, not a clock.** Agent steps have no latency or propagation delay to exploit. On the other hand, most datasets are single-threaded and totally ordered, which is simpler than concurrent span trees. TRAIL's DFS order equals chronological order in all 148 traces.

### 3.1 Metric-based RCA on multivariate time series

#### 3.1.1 MonitorRank (random walk on the call graph)
*Kim, Sumbaly, Shah. "Root cause finding in large distributed systems", SIGMETRICS 2013, DOI 10.1145/2465529.2465753.*
**Mechanism.** Combines each sensor's metric time series with the call graph between sensors. When the frontend is anomalous, it runs a random walk with transition probabilities from metric similarity. Frequently visited instances rank as likely root causes (walk detail per survey arXiv:2407.01710). On LinkedIn outages it reports a 26-51% gain in mean average precision.
**Mapping.** (a) node = agent; edge weight = similarity of per-agent feature series across steps, e.g. per-step OAT error `e_t` restricted to that agent's steps. (b) L0: no training, only the failed trace and its graph. (c) ranking over agents (who). (d) `a*` on TRAIL (manager vs search agent), Who&When, LongRCA role; metric AC@1/AC@3. (e) LongRCA role prior 40.3%; Who&When "most frequent agent". (f) A personalised walk from the symptom favours *nearby* nodes, i.e. late agents; with 1-5 agents per trace the walk is nearly trivial.
**Code.** No official code found (unverified).

#### 3.1.2 MicroRCA (attributed graph + personalised PageRank)
*Wu, Tordsson, Elmroth, Kao. NOMS 2020, DOI 10.1109/NOMS47738.2020.9110353 (HAL hal-02441640).*
**Mechanism.** Builds an attributed graph of services *and hosts* that models anomaly propagation, with no application instrumentation. Edge and node weights come from correlation with the anomalous symptom. Personalised PageRank ranks the candidates (89% precision and 97% MAP on injected faults in Kubernetes, per the abstract). A poster summary says the personalisation = average edge weight x correlation (secondary source).
**Mapping.** (a) bipartite graph of agents and *tools/resources* (each agent node linked to the tools it calls), node attribute = per-agent anomaly series. (b) L0 on the failed trace plus L1 normalisation statistics from successes. (c) ranking over agents/tools. (d) `a*` (Who&When, TRAIL) or tool-level blame (TraceElephant SWE-Agent, where "agent" = tool and 80% of labels blame `str_replace_editor`); AC@k. (e) majority-agent prior. (f) Correlation with the symptom needs a *time series per agent* that co-moves with the failure. Agents act in turns, not concurrently, so there is little co-movement.
**Code.** Not verified.

#### 3.1.3 MicroCause (PCTS + temporal-cause-oriented random walk)
*Meng, Zhang, Sun, Zhang, et al. IWQoS 2020, DOI 10.1109/IWQoS49365.2020.9213058.*
**Mechanism.** Localises root-cause *indicators within one microservice*. A path-condition time-series (PCTS) variant of PC captures time-lagged causal edges, then a temporal-cause-oriented random walk (TCORW) combines causal relations, temporal order and priority. It was evaluated on 86 real failure tickets.
**Mapping.** (a) per-step indicator channels of *one* agent (tool-error flag, return length, repetition count, token NLL). (b) L0. (c) ranked indicators, which give a mode-like explanation, not a step. (d) mode labels (TRAIL categories, AgentRx categories); category-F1. (e) majority-mode prior. (f) Lagged PC needs long series. A median of 10-30 steps per trajectory is too short; pooling across trajectories breaks the single-system assumption.
**Code.** Re-implemented in RCAEval (Apache-2.0, from `PanYicheng/dycause_rca`).

#### 3.1.4 CIRCA (intervention recognition, regression-based hypothesis testing, descendant adjustment)
*Li, Li, Yin, Nie, Zhang, Sui, Pei. KDD 2022 ADS, arXiv:2206.05871, DOI 10.1145/3534678.3539041.*
**Mechanism.** RCA = *intervention recognition*. A variable is a root-cause indicator if its distribution *conditioned on its parents* in the causal Bayesian network changes. The structural graph comes from the call graph plus meta-metric assumptions: Traffic causes Saturation, Latency and Errors; callee latency causes caller latency. For each variable, a regression on its parents is trained on the pre-fault reference window (120 min). The score is `a_t = |((v_t - vhat_t) - mu)/sigma|`, maximised over the test window. **Descendant adjustment:** processing from the leaves upward, a node with score >= 3 adds the maximum score among its anomalous descendants, which favours the upstream node when parent and child are both abnormal. Top-1 recall is 25% above the best baseline on a real dataset.
**Mapping.** This is the most direct upgrade of OAT. (a) `x_t` = OAT's step embedding `h_t` (or scalar observation features). (b) L1: fit `g(h_parents(t)) -> h_t` on successful trajectories only, where parents = the step's in-edges in the dependency DAG, not the full prefix. (c) per-step *conditional* anomaly score plus adjusted ranking; agent of the argmax = who. (d) SymFail failure node (536; node accuracy), MemTrace faulty operation (OIA), TRAIL error spans (location accuracy, set-F1), TraceElephant `k*` once def-use edges are inferred. (e) position prior per dataset (TRAIL errors at mean relative position 0.64; MemTrace 0.69), OAT's prefix-conditioned score, and "last node". (f) OAT App. H says the score fires on reactions to empty tool returns. Under a parent-conditional score, a reaction that is normal *given* an abnormal observation scores low, which is exactly the fix. What breaks: the regression `R^64 -> R^64` from parent embeddings needs many successes; graph errors (a missing edge) turn propagated symptoms back into root causes. Shared parents across steps of the same agent need care.
**Code.** `NetManAIOps/CIRCA` (BSD-3), PyPI `circa-clue`; also in RCAEval.

#### 3.1.5 RCD, Root Cause Discovery (failure as an intervention, F-node, localised Ψ-PC)
*Ikram, Chakraborty, Mitra, Saini, Bagchi, Kocaoglu. NeurIPS 2022 (Proceedings, hash c9fcd02e…); no arXiv version found.*
**Mechanism.** Treats data from the normal period as observational and data from the fault period as interventional. A binary **F-NODE** indicator (normal vs fault) is added as a variable, and the root causes are the F-node's *children*. Instead of learning the whole graph, RCD runs a localised Ψ-PC (a Ψ-FCI variant without latent confounders) on chunks of variables, hierarchically. Metrics are discretised. It matches Ψ-PC's top-k recall at a fraction of the runtime, and was validated on Sock-shop and three real cloud failures.
**Mapping.** The cleanest L1 formulation for *system-level who*. (a) per-*trajectory* variables, one row per run: per-agent and per-tool aggregates (call counts, error-flag counts, repetition, mean OAT score of that agent's steps). (b) L1: the F-node is the outcome `S` across many runs of the same MAS. (c) the set of variables whose distribution shifts *directly* with failure, i.e. which agent/tool behaviour is the proximate cause of failures for this system. (d) per-system aggregates of `a*` and of mode labels: MAST-Data (1,642 traces, but no outcome label), Aegis (agent, mode) pairs, HORIZON domains; evaluate as AC@k against the most frequent `a*` per system. Not per trajectory. (e) frequency of each agent among failures (deviance-mining baseline, 3.4.4). (f) Gives population-level, not per-trajectory, attribution. It needs hundreds of runs per system with outcomes, and the d-separation tests assume one stationary causal system, which tasks of varying difficulty violate (the task-difficulty confound).
**Code.** `azamikram/rcd` (MIT); in RCAEval.

#### 3.1.6 Causal-structure-based attribution of outliers (Shapley over ancestors)
*Budhathoki, Minorics, Blöbaum, Janzing. ICML 2022, PMLR 162:2357-2369; arXiv:1912.02724.*
**Mechanism.** Given a known DAG and its functional causal model, define an information-theoretic outlier score. Define a *conditional* outlier score (how unexpected `x_j` is given its parents). Attribute the target's outlier score to the noise terms of its ancestors via Shapley values.
**Mapping.** (a) node = step on the dependency DAG; target = final answer / `final_result` node. (b) L1: fit the causal mechanisms on successes. (c) a contribution of each ancestor step to the final-answer anomaly, i.e. a soft `E` set with shares. (d) MP-Bench nDCG@5 (step consensus rates), AgentRx all-events set-F1, TRAIL set-F1; `k*` = top contributor. (e) position prior; OAT top-k. (f) Needs a functional causal model per node type (invertible noise). On text embeddings this means a learned conditional density (OAT's own future-work item: flows / NLL). Shapley cost grows with ancestors (use the DAG's sparsity).
**Code.** DoWhy `gcm` module (`attribute_anomalies`).

#### 3.1.7 CausalRCA (gradient-based DAG learning + PageRank)
*Xin, Chen, Zhao. JSS 2023, arXiv:2209.02500, DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2023.111724.*
**Mechanism.** Learns a weighted causal graph over service metrics with a gradient-based (non-linear) structure learner, then ranks root-cause metrics with a random-walk inference step. It does both coarse-grained (service) and fine-grained (metric) localisation.
**Mapping.** (a)-(c) as in 3.1.3 but non-linear. (d) mode / indicator-level labels. (e)-(f) In Pham et al.'s comparison it is among the better methods, but structure learning on one short trajectory is not identifiable. It could pool across many trajectories of one system (L0), which again assumes stationarity.
**Code.** `AXinx/CausalRCA_code` (no licence); in RCAEval.

#### 3.1.8 CausIL (instance-level causal graph with domain knowledge)
*Chakraborty, Garg, Agarwal, Chauhan, et al. WWW 2023, arXiv:2303.00554, DOI 10.1145/3543507.3583274.*
**Mechanism.** Causal structure learning over service metrics where auto-scaled *instances* of the same service are treated as i.i.d. replicas. Architecture knowledge restricts the edges.
**Mapping.** The useful idea is "instances as replicas". Several invocations of the same agent role within a trajectory, or the same role across runs, are replicas of one service, which addresses the few-traces problem. Structural prior = the MAS wiring diagram (who can message whom). (b) L0/L1. (f) Instances of an LLM agent are not i.i.d.: each call's context differs.
**Code.** Not verified.

#### 3.1.9 RUN (neural Granger causality + contrastive pretraining + personalised PageRank)
*Lin, Chang, Wang, Wang, Peng. AAAI 2024, arXiv:2402.01140.*
**Mechanism.** Contrastively pretrains a time-series encoder, uses a forecasting model for neural Granger causal discovery (respecting temporal order, unlike PC), then ranks top-k root causes by PageRank with a personalisation vector.
**Mapping.** (a) multichannel per-step feature series. (b) L0. (c) a ranked list of channels. (d) same as 3.1.3. (f) Pham et al. (ASE'24) find that Granger/PC + PageRank methods, RUN included, "mostly perform similarly to Dummy" (random selection) on their benchmark suite. Do not prioritise.
**Code.** `zmlin1998/RUN` (no licence); in RCAEval.

#### 3.1.10 BARO (multivariate BOCPD + robust median/IQR scorer)
*Pham, Ha, Zhang. FSE 2024, arXiv:2405.09330, DOI 10.1145/3660805.*
**Mechanism.** (i) Anomaly time: multivariate Bayesian online change-point detection (Gaussian, inverse-Wishart prior) on latency and error series; the first change point is `t_A`. (ii) RobustScorer: for each metric, `a_t = |x_t - median| / IQR`, with median and IQR from the pre-`t_A` window; score = max over the post-`t_A` window. Rank metrics, and their services, by score. It needs no causal graph and is robust to errors in `t_A`.
**Mapping.** Two separate transfers. **(i) BOCPD = an online change-point detector for `t_lock`.** (a) scalar per-step features (tool error, empty return, repetition, OAT `e_t`, proxy-LM NLL); (b) L0; (c) run-length posterior, change point; (d) `t_lock` (Failure-as-a-Process `t_commit`, AgentRx `k_lock`), lead time vs `t_lock` and `t_obs`; (e) Failure-as-a-Process's prompted monitor (82% precision, 18% recall, median lead time 0). BOCPD is *causal* (online), unlike OAT's spline. **(ii) RobustScorer = a robust OAT decoder.** Standardise each agent's or step type's score by the median/IQR of the same agent or step type in successes, instead of a global conformal threshold. (f) A change point marks where *behaviour* changes, which is usually after `t_err` (`t_err` is a silent wrong decision). Expect `t_lock` and `t_obs`, not `t_err`.
**Code.** `phamquiluan/baro` (MIT), PyPI `fse-baro`; in RCAEval.

#### 3.1.11 Nezha and Eadro (multi-modal RCA)
*Nezha: Yu, Chen, Li, Chen, et al. ESEC/FSE 2023, DOI 10.1145/3611643.3616249. Eadro: Lee, Yang, Chen, Su, Lyu. ICSE 2023, arXiv:2302.05092, DOI 10.1109/ICSE48619.2023.00150.*
**Mechanism.** Nezha turns metrics, traces and logs into one *event* representation, mines event graphs, and compares event patterns in the fault-free phase with those in the fault-suffering phase. It localises to code region and resource type, interpretably. Eadro jointly learns detection and localisation from traces, logs and KPIs with intra-service and inter-service (dependency) modelling, and is supervised.
**Mapping.** Nezha is a pattern-contrast method: (a) events = (agent, step type, observation status) n-grams; (b) L1, successes vs failures of the same system; (c) patterns over-represented in failures, localised to the first occurrence; (d) `k*` / mode; (f) needs many runs per system, like deviance mining (3.4.4). Eadro is an L2-trained multi-source classifier, the analogue of fine-tuned attributors (TracerTraj, Aegis); it is not covered further here.
**Code.** Not verified for either.

#### 3.1.12 Benchmarks and critiques: what the RCA field learned about its own evaluation
- **Pham, Ha, Zhang. "RCA for microservice systems based on causal inference: how far are we?" ASE 2024, arXiv:2408.13729, DOI 10.1145/3691620.3695065.** 9 causal-discovery and 21 RCA methods on synthetic (CIRCA, RCD, CausIL generators) and real testbeds (Sock Shop, Online Boutique, Train Ticket). They are the first to report a **Dummy (random node) baseline**: PC/FCI/Granger/LiNGAM/fGES-based methods, CausalAI, RUN and MicroCause "mostly perform similarly to Dummy". The best methods are CausalRCA, RCD, CIRCA and two methods with *no graph*: NSigma (z-score) and BARO. NSigma is the fastest. Performance on synthetic data does not predict real systems. Metrics: AC@k and Avg@k.
- **RCAEval. Pham, Zhang, Ha, Salim, Zhang. WWW 2025 Companion, arXiv:2412.17015, DOI 10.1145/3701716.3715290.** 735 failure cases (RE1 metrics only, 375; RE2 metrics + logs + traces, 270; RE3 code-level faults, 90) on three systems, plus a reproducible library. It implements RUN, CausalRCA, CIRCA, RCD, MicroCause, MSCRED, BARO, ε-Diagnosis, TraceRCA, MicroRank, multi-source variants, TORAI and EventADL, so one `pip install` gives most baselines in this section.
- **PetShop. Hardt, Orchard, Blöbaum, Kirschbaum, Kasiviswanathan. CLeaR 2024 (PMLR 236), arXiv:2311.04806.** 41-component app, 68 injected issues with unique ground truth, 5-minute metrics. Methods that do well on Sock-shop struggle here.
- **Fang, Zhang, Yang, Wu, Xu, et al. "Rethinking the evaluation of microservice RCA with a fault propagation-aware benchmark." FSE 2026, arXiv:2510.04711, DOI 10.1145/3797100.** Simple rule-based methods match or beat the state of the art on four widely used benchmarks. A harder benchmark (1,430 validated cases from 9,152 injections, 25 fault types) drops 11 models to an average Top@1 of 0.21.
- **Survey:** Zhang et al. "Failure diagnosis in microservice systems: a comprehensive survey and analysis", arXiv:2407.01710. **Thesis:** Pham, arXiv:2606.09942 (RMIT PhD).
- **EventADL. Pham, Nicolet, Dodds, Guan, Kroening. FSE 2026, DOI 10.1145/3808186.** RCA over CloudTrail-style *API-call event logs*. It builds an actor -> resource -> anomaly graph from write and error events and ranks root-cause *actors* with a temporal-aware random walk (from the RCAEval docs). This is the closest AIOps object to a multi-agent tool log: actor = agent, resource = tool, file or database row. (b) L0. (d) `a*` on SymFail, TRAIL, AgentHallu multi-agent subsets.

**Mapping of the critique.** These are the RCA counterparts of the brief's position-prior warning. The agent-attribution field should adopt the same three baselines: **Dummy** (random step / random agent), **NSigma-style** (z-score of a single scalar feature, e.g. tool error or OAT `e_t`, against successes), and **position / step-type priors** (section 4). It should report AC@1, AC@3, Avg@5 alongside exact-step accuracy.

### 3.2 Trace-based RCA on call graphs and spans

#### 3.2.1 TraceAnomaly (trace-level likelihood; unseen call paths)
*Liu, Xu, Ouyang, Jiao, Pei, et al. ISSRE 2020, DOI 10.1109/ISSRE5003.2020.00014.*
**Mechanism (per surveys arXiv:2105.12378 and 2407.01710).** Encodes each trace as a service-trace vector of response times over call paths. A deep Bayesian network / VAE with posterior flow, trained on normal traces, gives a likelihood of normality. *Unseen call paths* are anomalous unless an operator whitelists them.
**Mapping.** This is OAT at the trace level. (a) trace vector over (agent, tool) call paths with step-level features. (b) L1, successes only. (c) a trajectory-level score plus "new path" flags whose first occurrence is a step guess. (d) trajectory AUROC on datasets with successes (AgentHallu, HORIZON, Failure-as-a-Process); first unseen path vs `k*`. (e) trajectory length (AUROC 0.653 in the Failure-as-a-Process pilot). (f) With few successes almost every agent path is "unseen". Our Failure-as-a-Process pilot uses a smoothed n-gram for this reason.
**Code.** Not verified.

#### 3.2.2 MicroRank (spectrum analysis + PageRank over normal and abnormal traces)
*Yu, Chen, Chen, Guan, et al. WWW 2021, DOI 10.1145/3442381.3449905.*
**Mechanism.** After a latency alarm, traces are split into normal and abnormal. A PageRank scorer weights operations by their coverage in each set, then spectrum-based fault-localisation formulas (from software testing) rank operations by how much more they occur in abnormal traces.
**Mapping.** (a) element = (agent, step type, tool, argument signature). (b) L1 over *many runs*: normal = successful runs, abnormal = failed runs of the same task family. (c) suspiciousness per element, mapped back to its first occurrence in a failed trajectory. (d) `a*` and `k*` on replayable sets (tau2-bench, ALFWorld, Terminal-Bench); AC@k. (e) FAMAS (3.5.1) is the agent-native version. (f) Coverage spectra need the *same* elements across runs, so free-text steps must be abstracted (FAMAS uses an LLM plus clustering). Elements specific to one task never get a spectrum.
**Code.** `IntelligentDDS/MicroRank`; in RCAEval.

#### 3.2.3 TraceRCA (suspicious-set mining on abnormal traces)
*Li, Chen, Jiao, et al. (… Sui, Pei). IWQoS 2021, DOI 10.1109/IWQOS52092.2021.9521340 (IEEE Xplore 9521340).*
**Mechanism.** Trace anomaly detection, then mining of suspicious microservice sets, then ranking. A microservice with more abnormal and fewer normal traces passing through it is more likely the root cause (Jaccard-style scoring per survey 2407.01710). A service with both incoming and outgoing abnormal invocations is treated as a propagation victim. Top-1 accuracy is 44.8% above unsupervised baselines; deployed at a large bank.
**Mapping.** As 3.2.2. The victim rule ("anomalous in and anomalous out = propagated") is directly usable on SymFail and TRAIL. A step whose parent observation was already anomalous and whose output is anomalous is a *carrier*, not a root. (f) Same requirement of many runs.
**Code.** In RCAEval.

#### 3.2.4 MicroHECL (anomaly propagation chains with pruning)
*Liu, He, Peng, Lin, et al. ICSE-SEIP 2021, arXiv:2103.01782, DOI 10.1109/ICSE-SEIP52600.2021.00043.*
**Mechanism.** On a dynamically built service call graph, walk *against* the propagation direction from the initially anomalous service. Prune calls unrelated to the current chain (pruning details per Alibaba's write-up), and rank candidates by Pearson correlation between the entry service's business KPI and the candidates' quality indicators. Alibaba deployment: top-3 hit 68%.
**Mapping.** This is MemTrace's attributor (backward walk over operation sub-graphs from the prediction) in AIOps form. (a) nodes of the backward slice. (b) L0. (c) the earliest anomalous node on the chain. (d) MemTrace OIA (faulty op inside the slice in 140 of 160 cases, so the slice is a valid search space); SymFail failure node. (e) last-node and random-node baselines (SymFail reports both for repair). (f) Needs a per-node anomaly test to decide whether the chain continues. With text nodes, that test is an LLM judge or an OAT-style score.
**Code.** Not released (unverified).

#### 3.2.5 Sage (causal Bayesian network + graphical VAE counterfactuals)
*Gan, Liang, Dev, Lo, Delimitrou. ASPLOS 2021, DOI 10.1145/3445814.3446700; arXiv:2101.00267.*
**Mechanism.** Learns a causal Bayesian network over microservices from RPC-level traces (latency propagates backend to frontend). A graphical VAE then generates *counterfactuals*: set a service's latency or usage to a value that meets QoS and infer whether the end-to-end violation disappears. It is unsupervised (no labelled traces) and identifies the root cause of QoS violations with over 93% accuracy.
**Mapping.** This is the *model-based* version of the report's Proposal P1 (counterfactual perturbation screens) without re-execution. (a) node = step embedding on the DAG. (b) L1: a generative model of successes conditional on parents. (c) for each step `k`, the predicted change in "success-likeness" of the final node when `h_k` is replaced by its typical value given parents, a surrogate for `e_tau(k)`. (d) `k*` (TraceElephant, which has re-execution-checked labels on the SWE-Agent subset), SymFail; also the P1 surrogate task T1. (e) OAT; position prior. (f) The counterfactual is only as good as the generative model of successes. "Replace by typical value" is not an executable intervention, so validate on replayable subsets.
**Code.** Not verified.

#### 3.2.6 CRISP (critical-path analysis of traces)
*Zhang, Ramanathan, Raj, Parwal, Sherwood, Chabbi. USENIX ATC 2022, pp. 655-672; code `uber-research/CRISP`.*
**Mechanism.** Extracts the critical path (the chain of spans that determines end-to-end latency) from Jaeger traces at Uber scale. It aggregates paths for top-down analysis of an endpoint and bottom-up discovery of widely shared bottleneck APIs, with anomaly detection on the fly.
**Mapping.** The agent analogue of the critical path is the **information-critical path**: the chain of dependency edges from the task to the final answer in the DAG (MemTrace's backward slice; SymFail's ancestors of `final_result`). Restricting candidate steps to that path removes side branches that cannot have caused the answer. (b) L0. (c) a candidate mask. (d) a filter in front of any scorer; measure the hit rate of `k*` on the path and the candidate reduction. (f) Agent errors of *omission* (a missing verification) are not on any path. Steps whose effect is to *prevent* later search are invisible.
**Code.** `uber-research/CRISP`.

### 3.3 Log-based anomaly detection and localisation

#### 3.3.1 Drain (online template mining) = step-type vocabulary
*He, Zhu, Zheng, Lyu. ICWS 2017, DOI 10.1109/ICWS.2017.13.*
**Mechanism.** Online log parsing with a fixed-depth parse tree: route by length and leading tokens, then merge by token similarity into templates with wildcards.
**Mapping.** Builds the *activity alphabet* for every discrete method below. Apply it to tool-call strings (`tool(arg-types)`), observation first lines (error class), and orchestrator message heads (e.g. "Call Next Speaker: X"). Output = step-type token `c_t`. (f) Free-form LLM thoughts do not template. Restrict Drain to calls and observations, and use embedding clusters for text.
**Code.** logpai `logparser`, IBM Drain3 (not re-verified here).

#### 3.3.2 Xu et al. (PCA on console logs; session-level detection)
*Xu, Huang, Fox, Patterson, Jordan. SOSP 2009, DOI 10.1145/1629575.1629587.*
**Mechanism.** Groups log lines by identifier (session) into message-count vectors and flags sessions whose vector has a large residual outside the PCA normal subspace. The output is a *session* label, not an event label.
**Mapping.** The canonical example of the *session vs event granularity* gap: detection = trajectory-level `S`, localisation = step-level `k*`. The log community mostly solved the former; agent attribution needs the latter.

#### 3.3.3 DeepLog (next-log-key LSTM, top-g) = discrete OAT
*Du, Li, Zheng, Srikumar. CCS 2017, DOI 10.1145/3133956.3134015.*
**Mechanism (abstract plus secondary summaries).** An LSTM over log keys trained on normal executions predicts a distribution over the next key. An event is anomalous if the observed key is not among the top-g candidates. A second LSTM models parameter values (including inter-event time). Workflows (finite-state models) built from the logs support diagnosis after detection.
**Mapping.** (a) `c_t` = Drain step type (optionally with agent id). (b) L1: train only on successes, exactly OAT's selection. (c) per-step surprisal `-log p(c_t | c_<t)` or a top-g miss. (d) `k*`, `t_err`, `t_lock`; trajectory AUROC. (e) position prior *and* step-type prior. **Pilot (section 4):** on Failure-as-a-Process, the first step whose surprisal exceeds the 80th percentile of held-out PASS-step surprisals hits `t_err` exactly in 0.214 of failures, vs 0.102 for the position prior and 0.407 for "first W step". (f) The top-g criterion assumes rare = wrong. A wrong *content* at a common step type is invisible. The parameter-value model (an argument-value channel) is the part that could see content errors.
**Code.** Third-party PyTorch `deeplog` on PyPI; original code not verified.

#### 3.3.4 LogAnomaly (template2vec; sequential + quantitative anomalies)
*Meng, Liu, Zhu, Zhang, et al. IJCAI 2019, DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2019/658.*
**Mechanism.** template2vec embeds templates semantically, so templates that first appear between retrainings map to known ones. It detects *sequential* anomalies (next template) and *quantitative* anomalies (count relations between templates, e.g. every "open" matched by a "close").
**Mapping.** Quantitative relations = count invariants over step types: #tool_call = #tool_result, "every write followed by a test", "one final answer". (b) L1, mined from successes. (c) the first step at which an invariant becomes unsatisfiable. (d) `k*`/`t_lock`. Closely related to Declare (3.4.3) and to AgentRx's synthesised constraints. (f) Count invariants fire at the end, so localisation needs the activation step.
**Code.** Not verified.

#### 3.3.5 LogBERT and LogGPT (self-supervised transformers on normal logs)
*LogBERT: Guo, Yuan, Wu. IJCNN 2021, arXiv:2103.04475, DOI 10.1109/IJCNN52387.2021.9534113. LogGPT: Han, Yuan, Trabelsi, arXiv:2309.14482 (2023).*
**Mechanism.** LogBERT trains BERT on normal log-key sequences with two self-supervised tasks (masked log-key prediction and a hypersphere-compactness objective, per the paper body, not re-verified). An event is anomalous if masked keys are poorly predicted. LogGPT pretrains GPT-2 on next-entry prediction, then **fine-tunes with reinforcement learning** to align the language-model objective with anomaly detection.
**Mapping.** LogBERT's *masked* prediction is bidirectional, so it is non-causal like OAT's spline. LogGPT is causal. LogGPT's message, that the LM loss is misaligned with detection, is the log-community version of the brief's critique that OAT's signal comes from selection, not from the model. (b) L1. (c) per-step score. (d)/(e) as 3.3.3. (f) Same as DeepLog: syntax-level surprise.
**Code.** Not verified.

#### 3.3.6 LLM-based log analysis (LogPrompt)
*Liu, Tao, Meng, et al. "Interpretable online log analysis using LLMs with prompt strategies" (LogPrompt), ICPC 2024, arXiv:2308.07610.*
**Mechanism.** Zero-shot log parsing and anomaly detection by prompting, with reasons. It reports up to 380.7% gains over simple prompts and robustness to unseen domains, where trained detectors drop by up to 62.5%.
**Mapping.** The AIOps counterpart of LLM-judge attribution (All-at-once / Step-by-step). It belongs to the "LLM reads the log" family, not the time-series family. Cited for completeness.

#### 3.3.7 Le and Zhang, "Log-based anomaly detection with deep learning: how far are we?"
*ICSE 2022, arXiv:2202.04301, DOI 10.1145/3510003.3510155.*
**Findings.** Five deep detectors on four datasets. Training-data selection, data grouping (windowing vs sessions), class imbalance and label noise all change results significantly. F-measures above 0.9 on HDFS do not carry over, and "the problem … has not been solved yet".
**Mapping.** Directly relevant to pooling the 21 datasets. Step units (message vs LLM call vs span vs operation) play the role of "data grouping", and step-unit normalisation changes results. Report per-dataset, never pooled-only.

### 3.4 Process mining

#### 3.4.1 Conformance checking: token replay, alignments, prefix-alignments
- *van der Aalst. Process Mining: Data Science in Action, Springer 2016, DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-49851-4.*
- *Rozinat, van der Aalst. "Conformance checking of processes based on monitoring real behavior", Information Systems 33(1), 2008, DOI 10.1016/j.is.2007.07.001* (token-based replay: count missing and remaining tokens).
- *Adriansyah, van Dongen, van der Aalst. "Conformance checking using cost-based fitness analysis", EDOC 2011, DOI 10.1109/EDOC.2011.12* (robust replay with cost-based fitness; diagnostics = **skipped and inserted activities**).
- *Carmona, van Dongen, Solti, Weidlich. Conformance Checking: Relating Processes and Models, Springer 2018, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-99414-7* (textbook on alignments).
- *van Zelst, Bolt, Hassani, van Dongen, van der Aalst. "Online conformance checking: relating event streams to process models using prefix-alignments", Int. J. Data Sci. Anal. 2017/2019, DOI 10.1007/s41060-017-0078-6.*

**Mechanism.** An alignment pairs a trace with a run of the model through moves: *synchronous* (log and model agree), *log move* (an event the model cannot do here, an "insertion"), *model move* (an activity the model needed that the log skipped). An optimal alignment minimises total move cost (standard: 0 sync, 1 per log/model move, 0 for silent transitions), found by A* over the synchronous product net. Prefix-alignments compute this incrementally on a stream, so the deviation is reported *when it happens*.
**Mapping.** This is the "flow of success" of OAT in discrete form, specified as a baseline in section 4. (a) `c_t` = step-type token. (b) L1 (model discovered from successes) or L0 + specification (task reference or hand-written policy). (c) a per-step move type and cost; first deviation; who = agent of the deviating event, or owner of the skipped activity. (d) `k*` exact/±k; AgentRx `E` set-F1 (all deviations); `t_lock` for monitors (prefix variant, lead time). (e) position prior, step-type prior, Dummy. (f) Alternative valid plans produce false deviations. Content errors at the right step type produce none. A good model needs a vocabulary of tens of activities, not 8 codes and not free text.
**Code.** PM4Py (used by COMPASS); ProM.

#### 3.4.2 Process discovery from successful runs: Inductive Miner
*Leemans, Fahland, van der Aalst. "Discovering block-structured process models from event logs: a constructive approach", Petri Nets 2013, LNCS 7927, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-38697-8_17.*
**Mechanism.** Recursively finds cuts (sequence, exclusive choice, parallel, loop) in the directly-follows graph and builds a *process tree*. Polynomial time; returns a **sound** model that **fits** the log, with rediscoverability under log-completeness conditions (per the pm4py summary and the paper's framing). A later variant (IMf) filters infrequent behaviour. IM models are known to have *high fitness but low precision* (secondary statement in later van der Aalst-group work).
**Mapping.** Discover `N` from successes, then align failures (section 4). (f) With tens of successes and loops (retries, re-searches), the discovered tree tends to put most activities inside a loop, a near-"flower" model that accepts almost every failure. Check *precision* before using the model, or use frequency-filtered DFGs or n-grams instead, as in our pilot.

#### 3.4.3 Declare: declarative constraints as checkable step-level rules
*Pesic, Schonenberg, van der Aalst. "DECLARE: full support for loosely-structured processes", EDOC 2007, DOI 10.1109/EDOC.2007.14. de Leoni, Maggi, van der Aalst. "An alignment-based framework to check the conformance of declarative process models…", Information Systems 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.is.2013.12.005.*
**Mechanism.** Constraint templates with LTL-on-finite-traces semantics: existence, init, response(a,b) (every a eventually followed by b), precedence(a,b) (no b before an a), succession, not-coexistence, and so on. Declare models allow anything not forbidden, which suits flexible processes. de Leoni et al. define alignments against Declare models, giving the minimal-cost fix and its location.
**Mapping.** This is the process-mining form of **AgentRx's constraint synthesis** (arXiv:2602.02475: synthesised constraints, step-wise checking, validation log, LLM judge) and of **AgentLTL** (3.5.3). Two sources of rules: (i) *mined* from successes, i.e. constraints with support >= 0.9 in successes and lower in failures (L1); (ii) *written* from the task policy, e.g. tau-bench's airline/retail policy or BFCL's "login before send_message", the deviation in the AgentHallu example (L0 + spec). (c) the first violation step: precedence and chain constraints localise immediately; response constraints only at the end (localise at the activation). (d) AgentRx `E` and critical step (tau-bench rows); TrajErrBench tau2 critical step; MAST modes FM-1.3 (step repetition), FM-1.5 (unaware of termination), FM-3.2 (no/incomplete verification). (e) step-type prior. (f) Rule mining from few successes gives spurious rules (support over 29 runs is noisy). Semantic rules ("the refund amount must equal the order total") need data-aware Declare (MP-Declare) or code.

#### 3.4.4 Deviance mining
*Nguyen, Dumas, La Rosa, Maggi. "Mining business process deviance: a quest for accuracy", OTM 2014, DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-45563-0_25. Nguyen, Dumas, La Rosa, Maggi, Suriadi. "Business process deviance mining: review and evaluation", arXiv:1608.08252.*
**Mechanism.** Encode traces as features (activity frequencies, sequence patterns, discriminative patterns) and train a classifier that separates deviant from normal cases. The rules are read as causes of deviance. Finding: pattern-mining features "only slightly outperform" plain activity-frequency features.
**Mapping.** (a) bag or patterns of step types per trajectory. (b) L1 (outcome). (c) discriminative patterns plus their first occurrence in each failed trajectory. (d) system-level `a*` and mode; `k*` via first occurrence. (e) activity-frequency features, i.e. "which agent or tool appears more in failures". (f) Discriminative patterns pick up *consequences* (retries, R codes, verification theatre) as often as causes. In the Failure-as-a-Process pilot, "first R (recover) step" hits `t_err` in only 0.7% of failures.

#### 3.4.5 Predictive process monitoring (brief)
*Teinemaa, Dumas, La Rosa, Maggi. "Outcome-oriented predictive process monitoring: review and benchmark", ACM TKDD 2019, arXiv:1707.06766, DOI 10.1145/3301300. Tax, Verenich, La Rosa, Dumas. "Predictive business process monitoring with LSTM neural networks", CAiSE 2017, arXiv:1612.02130.*
**Mechanism.** Classify *ongoing* cases (prefixes) by eventual outcome (prefix bucketing and encoding, then a classifier), or predict next activity and time with an LSTM.
**Mapping.** This is exactly the brief's **U3 prefix monitor** and the outcome-supervised hazard model (P2) in process-mining dress. The first prefix length at which `P(fail | prefix)` crosses a threshold estimates `t_lock`; the increment `Δ P(fail)` at step t is a per-step hazard. (b) L1. (d) `t_lock`, lead time (Failure-as-a-Process, AgentRx). Covered in depth elsewhere in the survey; listed here only for the lineage.

### 3.5 2024-2026 work applying trace/log RCA or process mining to LLM agents

#### 3.5.1 FAMAS: spectrum-based failure attribution for multi-agent systems
*Ge, Xie, Li, Pei, Zhang. arXiv:2509.13782; published as "Spectrum-based failure attribution for multi-agent systems", Proc. ACM Softw. Eng. (FSE 2026), DOI 10.1145/3797113.*
**Mechanism.** Replays each failed task k = 20 times. An LLM (Qwen2.5-72B) abstracts each log into agent-action-state triples, which are clustered hierarchically (by agent, then by semantic similarity) into "program elements". Suspiciousness = `α · Kulczynski2^λ · (1+β) · (1+γ)`: λ-decayed counts (λ = 0.9), β = the triple's share of its agent's actions, γ = the share of the agent's trajectories that contain it, and α = local-frequency boost. On Who&When (184): agent 57.61%, action 29.35% overall (hand-crafted 62.07 / 41.38; algorithm-generated 55.56 / 23.81). Best LLM baselines reach about 54.6% agent and 19.7% step; Ochiai variant 54.35 / 21.74.
**Mapping.** This is TraceRCA/MicroRank for agents. (b) L1+ by construction (replays with outcomes). (d) Who&When; it should be run on replayable sets with more runs per task (tau2-bench, ALFWorld). (e) Who&When first-3 hit 0.36, random-3 0.29. (f) Needs a live environment (Who&When's live web is not reproducible, so its replays are new runs, not the labelled one). On the algorithm-generated subset the best LLM step score (25.51%) beats FAMAS (23.81%). The spectrum element abstraction is itself an LLM step.
**Code.** "Publicly downloadable" per the paper; repository not located.

#### 3.5.2 CHIEF: from flat logs to hierarchical causal graphs
*Wang, Wu, Wang, Wang. arXiv:2602.23701 (Feb 2026).*
**Mechanism.** An LLM builds a hierarchical causal graph (subtask nodes, then agent nodes, then step nodes with data-flow edges) from Who&When logs. It synthesises "virtual oracles" per subtask (goal, preconditions, key evidence, acceptance criteria) and backtracks top-down (subtasks, then agents, then steps). Progressive counterfactual screening runs local, planning-control, data-flow and deviation-aware passes (the last removes self-corrected errors). Who&When with ground truth: hand-crafted agent/step 77.59 / 29.31; algorithm-generated 76.80 / 52.00.
**Mapping.** This is MicroHECL plus CIRCA's "anomalous given parents" logic with an LLM as the per-node test. (b) L0 (+ ground truth optional). (f) Evaluated only on Who&When. The graph and oracles are LLM outputs, so graph errors are untested. Code: anonymous repository.

#### 3.5.3 AgentTrace: causal graph tracing for deployed MAS
*Wang. arXiv:2603.14688, ICLR 2026 Workshop on Agents in the Wild.*
**Mechanism.** Graph with sequential, communication and data-dependency edges. BFS backward from the error node (depth up to 10). 17 hand-crafted features in five groups, combined linearly with grid-searched weights: **position 0.70**, structure 0.20, content 0.05, flow 0.03, confidence 0.02. On a *synthetic* benchmark of 550 scenarios (8-15 actions, single injected bug, **60% at steps 2-3**): Hit@1 94.9% vs GPT-4 68.5%.
**Mapping.** A ready-made example of a position-prior shortcut dressed as RCA. Its numbers say nothing about natural failures until it is run on Who&When, TRAIL, SymFail and LongRCA. Include it as a *baseline family* ("structure + position, no LLM"), not as evidence.

#### 3.5.4 GraphTracer (withdrawn)
*Zhang, Shi, Gu, et al. arXiv:2510.10581.* Information-dependency graphs over multi-agent deep-search traces. **v2 is withdrawn "due to a fundamental error in the methodology that affects the validity of the main results"**. Do not cite its numbers. CHIEF's comparison against GraphTracer (3.5.2) inherits this problem.

#### 3.5.5 Process mining on agent logs
- **COMPASS.** Dorsch, Henselmann, Harth. "COMPASS: a process-mining-based methodology for prompt optimization of LLM agents", HybridAIMS/CAI 2025 workshops at CAiSE 2025, CEUR-WS Vol-3996, pp. 30-38. Five phases: plan, extract trajectories, convert to event logs (case = run, activity = tool or phase), discover (PM4Py) and explore, then feed back via conformance checking or prompt guidelines. Case study: a SPARQL-generating agent with three tools. No `k*` evaluation.
- **Agent Behavior Mining.** Vu, Körner, Rebmann, Kevorkian, Perscheid, Berg, Kampik. BPM 2026, arXiv:2606.20669. An event data model that turns reasoning traces, tool use and token costs into process logs. In a multi-agent order-to-cash system, conformance checking flags a policy deviation (an agent omits a required step: a *model move*). Practitioner study, n = 18.
- **AgentLTL.** Elkoussy, Perez. arXiv:2607.02599. A language derived from first-order LTL for procedural rules over agent traces. It gives a deterministic, judge-free compliance score, checks every *prefix* online to gate tool calls, and serves as a dense RL reward (+38 pp accuracy, +17.5 pp compliance on held-out patterns). This is the Declare/prefix-alignment line in agent form. Formula and localisation details not read.
- **TraceCompiler.** El Yadouni, Li. arXiv:2608.02680. Mines clusters of agent traces into mostly deterministic workflows. An inter-tool edge is admitted only when a consumer argument contains a value uniquely attributable to an earlier producer. Precision 0.928 / recall 0.943 over 15,775 def-use edges, vs 0.711-0.712 F1 for adjacency and frequency-thresholded directly-follows. **This is the graph-construction step that CIRCA-, CRISP- and MicroHECL-style methods need on TraceElephant, AgentHallu, tau2-bench and TRAIL.**
- **Berti et al.** "Re-thinking process mining in the AI-based agents era", arXiv:2408.07720, and **PMAx** (arXiv:2603.15351). These go the *other* direction (LLM agents that perform process mining). Not relevant beyond terminology.
- **"From event logs to governed action"** (Yang et al., ICDM 2026 BlueSky, arXiv:2609.07984). Agenda paper; no attribution method.

#### 3.5.6 Agent observability infrastructure (what an "agent trace" looks like in 2026)
- **OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions** (repo `open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai`; status **Development**). They define agent spans `create_agent`, `invoke_agent` (client and internal), `invoke_workflow`, a plan span, and `execute_tool` operations, with attributes `gen_ai.operation.name`, `gen_ai.agent.id`, `gen_ai.agent.name`, `gen_ai.provider.name`. TRAIL uses the related OpenInference conventions (span kinds AGENT/CHAIN/LLM/TOOL). A standard exporter therefore gives the span tree and agent ids for free: "who" is a span attribute, and "when" is a span id.
- **AgentOps taxonomy.** Dong, Lu, Zhu. arXiv:2411.05285. What to trace across the agent lifecycle.
- **AgentSight.** Zheng, Hu, Yu, Quinn. arXiv:2508.02736. eBPF "boundary tracing": intercepts TLS-encrypted LLM traffic and kernel events and correlates them causally across processes. Under 3% overhead; detects prompt injection, reasoning loops and coordination bottlenecks. Code `agent-sight/agentsight`.
- **AgentSpy.** Bühler, Biagiola, Di Grazia, Salvaneschi. arXiv:2610.06001 (Oct 2026). Records system calls and network traffic of the agent *and its subprocesses* in an isolated environment, with *conformance* analyses (obligations) and safety analyses (prohibitions). Among runs that pass outcome tests, 18% do task-unrelated activities and 7% read grading files. This is a direct measurement of "recovered errors and suspicious passes in successes", which undermines one-class training on successes (cf. Failure-as-a-Process: 6.4% suspicious passes).
- **Watson.** Rombaut et al., arXiv:2411.03455. Infers implicit reasoning traces of fast-thinking agents by prompt attribution ("cognitive observability"). Evaluated on SWE-bench-lite with AutoCodeRover and OpenHands.

#### 3.5.7 LLM-based RCA agents (the reverse direction, and a data source)
- **RCACopilot.** Chen et al., EuroSys 2024, DOI 10.1145/3627703.3629553; arXiv:2305.15778. Incident-handler matching, diagnostic collection and LLM root-cause category prediction; accuracy up to 0.766 on a year of Microsoft incidents.
- **RCAgent.** Wang et al., CIKM 2024, arXiv:2310.16340. A tool-augmented autonomous agent with self-consistency over *action trajectories*; deployed for Alibaba Cloud's Flink platform.
- **AIOpsLab.** Chen, Shetty, Somashekar, Ma, et al., arXiv:2501.06706. Deploys microservices, injects faults, exports telemetry and evaluates agents.
- **OpenRCA.** Xu et al., ICLR 2025. 335 failures from three enterprise systems, over 68 GB of telemetry; agents must output (time, component, reason).
- **OpenRCA 2.0.** Fang et al., arXiv:2606.27154, work in progress. The PAVE step-wise labelling protocol uses the *known injected intervention* to reconstruct the causal propagation path by forward verification. Over 11 LLMs the exact root-cause set is recovered in 20.7% of cases. One correct root-cause service is found in 76.0%, but grounded in a verified propagation path in only 61.5% ("ungrounded diagnosis").
- **RCLAgent.** Zhang et al., arXiv:2508.20370. Multi-agent recursion-of-thought for root-cause localisation.

**Mapping.** Two uses. (i) These agents' *own* trajectories are long, tool-heavy, replayable (AIOpsLab injects faults deterministically) and verifiable (the injected fault is the answer). So AIOpsLab/OpenRCA-style environments are a new **data engine** for collecting successful and failed agent runs with outcomes (L1). OpenRCA 2.0's propagation-path labels give *step-checkable* evidence: a claim at step t is right or wrong against the known path. That is a route to L1+/L2 step labels without human annotation. (ii) Their failure modes ("ungrounded diagnosis") are failure-attribution targets in their own right. **No paper found that runs failure attribution on RCA-agent trajectories.**

## Conformance checking as discrete OAT

**The baseline in one line.** Learn a model of how *successful* runs proceed, as a process model, constraint set or n-gram language model over step types. On a failed run, report the first step at which the run leaves the model, and the agent who took it. This is OAT with (i) a discrete alphabet instead of hidden states, (ii) an alignment or automaton instead of a neural CDE, and (iii) "first deviation" instead of top-k reconstruction error. Unlike OAT's cubic spline, the prefix-alignment version is causal and can run online.

**Recipe.**
1. **Step-type vocabulary `Σ`.** Token `c_t = (role(a_t), kind(u_t), tool(u_t), argsig(u_t), status(o_t))`. Here `kind` ∈ {message, plan, tool_call, final_answer, handoff}; `argsig` is a Drain template of the argument string (types and slot names, not values); `status(o_t)` ∈ {ok, error, empty, truncated}. Target |Σ| of 20-200: prune by frequency and merge rare tokens to "OTHER". Per dataset: TRAIL span kind + `tool.name`; SymFail node kind + `agent_id`; AgentHallu tool names (BFCL) and roles; tau2-bench tool names; ALFWorld action verb; HORIZON-OS command head; Failure-as-a-Process's 8 annotated codes.
2. **Model discovery from successes `L+`** (L1), most to least robust: (a) an n-gram LM with interpolation (as DeepLog), giving per-step surprisal; (b) a frequency-filtered directly-follows graph (DFG), where edges seen in under θ of `L+` transitions count as deviations; (c) Declare constraints with support >= 0.9 in `L+`; (d) Inductive Miner (IMf) process tree, used only if its precision on held-out successes is acceptable. **Alternative (L0 + spec):** a *task-specific reference*, i.e. the gold action sequence (tau2-bench `actions`, BFCL ground-truth calls, ALFWorld expert plan), as a sequential net.
3. **Alignment cost.** Sync 0; log move `cost = -log f_{L+}(c)` (rare inserted activities cost more, so harmless extra reads are cheap); model move 1, or higher for "check" activities (verification, login, confirmation). Use the optimal alignment (A*); for monitoring, the prefix-alignment of van Zelst et al.
4. **Decoders.** (i) `k_hat` = step of the first non-synchronous move with cost >= c; (ii) top-k steps by move cost (set prediction for `E`); (iii) a conformal threshold as in OAT: choose c so that at most α of *held-out successes* contain any move above c (trajectory-level false-positive rate <= α). For "who": the agent of the first deviating event. For a model move (skipped activity), report the agent that *should* have done it: in the BFCL example the agent skipped `message_login` before `send_message`.
5. **Mandatory baselines.** Position prior (median relative position of `k*` in training folds), first step, random step (Dummy), **step-type prior** (first occurrence of the step type most often labelled `k*`, e.g. "first edit"), trajectory length (for trajectory-level AUROC).

**Pilot 1: Failure-as-a-Process (Terminal-Bench, 21 scaffold x model systems), annotations only.** Step types = the 8 `action_segments` codes (X execute, W write/edit, V verify, E explore, C completion claim, R recover, P plan, O other). These are produced by the *same LLM annotator* as `t_err`, so expect some coupling. 610 PASS train the model (5-fold out-of-fold for PASS scores); 1,184 FAIL are scored. The model is a trigram with Jelinek-Mercer interpolation, plus a DFG.

Trajectory level (FAIL vs PASS, PASS out-of-fold): AUROC 0.578 for max surprisal, 0.450 for mean surprisal, **0.653 for length alone**. Readings:
- **Conformance beats the position prior, but loses badly to a step-type prior.** `t_err` falls on a W step in 58% of failures, while W makes up only 16% of PASS steps. The decisive error is usually the first edit that acts on a false premise. "False premise" is 30.7% of triggers, and it is a *content* error at a *normal* step type.
- The conformance decoder and the first-W rule are nearly disjoint (they coincide in 13% of failures). On the 702 failures where `t_err` is *not* the first W, the conformance decoder still hits 0.217 exact (position prior 0.084), so it carries information beyond step type. **Combining both** is the obvious next step.
- The first deviation falls *before* `t_err` in 615 of 1,184 failures, between `t_err` and `t_lock` in 357, and after `t_lock` in 176. A coarse control-flow model is noisy early and does not separate `t_err` from `t_lock`.

**What it can catch.** Control-flow errors: skipped prerequisites (login, confirmation, verification: MAST FM-3.2), wrong order, a forbidden or unfamiliar action, premature termination (model moves at the end: FM-3.1), loops and repetition (log moves: FM-1.3), role violations (the right activity by the wrong agent: FM-1.2), unawareness of termination conditions (FM-1.5).

**What it cannot catch.** Content errors at a normal step type: a wrong argument value, a false premise, a hallucinated fact, a misread tool output. That covers most of Failure-as-a-Process's epistemic triggers (57.9%) and most of AgentHallu's categories. Alternative valid plans produce false positives. For content errors the first *control-flow* deviation comes *after* `k*` (the agent starts retrying), so the method estimates `t_lock`/`t_obs`, not `t_err`. Like OAT, it misses "early latent reasoning errors". Unlike OAT, it does not fire on failure verbalisation, because that is not a step type.

## Open gaps

1. **No RCA method has been run unchanged on a natural failure-attribution benchmark.** The agent papers (FAMAS, CHIEF, AgentTrace) either use LLMs for every node test or evaluate on Who&When only or on synthetic data. The cheap, LLM-free RCA family (CIRCA, BARO/NSigma, TraceRCA, EventADL) has not been tried on SymFail, MemTrace or TRAIL, the three datasets that ship graphs.
2. **Successes are missing exactly where graphs exist.** SymFail, MemTrace, TraceElephant and TRAIL release (almost) no successful runs, yet every L1 method here needs them. Collecting successes with SymTrace's capture layer (WebArena-Verified, AssistantBench), MemTrace's MIT code, or AIOpsLab is a prerequisite.
3. **The graph is the bottleneck.** Span trees are control graphs; RCA needs data dependencies. TraceCompiler's def-use rule (P 0.93 / R 0.94) is the only quantified recovery method found. No dataset reports how often `k*` lies on the information-critical path of the final answer (MemTrace's 140/160 is the one data point).
4. **Content vs control flow.** Both pilots show that discrete conformance finds omissions and order errors but not false premises. No method combines an argument-value channel (DeepLog's parameter model, data-aware Declare) with step-type conformance on agent logs.
5. **Step-type priors are unreported.** The brief catalogues position priors; this survey finds a stronger *step-type* prior on Failure-as-a-Process (first edit = 0.41 exact). Whether similar priors exist elsewhere (TRAIL: 88% of errors on LLM spans; TraceElephant-SWE: 80% blame `str_replace_editor`) and how much of published accuracy they explain is unmeasured.
6. **Root-cause vs symptom evaluation.** RCA benchmarks moved to propagation-aware ground truth (FSE'26 benchmark; OpenRCA 2.0's PAVE paths). Agent datasets label `k*` (and sometimes `t_lock`/`t_obs`) but not the propagation chain between them, except Failure-as-a-Process's three timestamps. Methods that "fire on reactions" cannot be diagnosed without chain labels.
7. **RCA-agent trajectories as a data engine is unexploited.** AIOpsLab and OpenRCA provide replayable, verifiable, tool-heavy agent runs with known injected causes. No failure-attribution dataset has been built from them.
8. **Online conformance for agents.** Prefix-alignments and AgentLTL's prefix gating exist, but no paper reports lead time against `t_lock` for a conformance-based monitor.
