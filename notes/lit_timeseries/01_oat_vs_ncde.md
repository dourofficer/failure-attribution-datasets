# OAT against its source: what was borrowed from Neural CDEs, what was changed, and what follows

*Written 2026-10-30 from the OAT PDF (`sharonish/papers/2026_Tracing_Agentic_Failure_from_the_Flow_of_Success.pdf`, arXiv:2607.12747, NeurIPS 2026) and the NCDE paper (Kidger, Morrill, Foster, Lyons, NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2005.08926, read via arxiv.org/html). Equation numbers refer to the OAT PDF. Items marked **[derivation]** are my own reading of the equations, not statements in either paper.*

## 1. Side-by-side

| Component | Neural CDE (Kidger et al. 2020) | OAT (Yeh et al. 2026) | Same / changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observations | irregular series `((t_0,x_0),...,(t_n,x_n))`, `x_i ∈ R^v` | `(h_t, u_t)`, `h_t ∈ R^64` = PCA of mean-pooled last-layer hidden states of the generating (or proxy) LLM while producing step `t`; `u_t = t/T` | changed: the "time" is the normalised step index, not clock time |
| Control path `X` | natural cubic spline with knots at `t_i`, `X_{t_i} = (x_i, t_i)`, time as a channel | natural cubic spline, `X(u_t) = (h_t, u_t)`, not learnable (Eq. 6 text, Table 6) | same |
| Initial state | `z_{t_0} = ζ_θ(x_0, t_0)` (to avoid translation invariance) | `z(0) = g_2(h_Q)`, a linear map of the query representation (Eq. 4) | same role; `t_0 ≡ 0` so the time argument is dropped |
| Dynamics | `z_t = z_{t_0} + ∫ f_θ(z_s) dX_s`, `f_θ: R^w → R^{w×(v+1)}` | `z(u) = z(0) + ∫_0^u f(v, z(v); θ_f) dX(v)` (Eq. 6), `f` a 3-layer MLP, hidden 64 | same; OAT adds explicit `v` dependence, which NCDE gets through the time channel |
| Reduction to ODE | `dz/ds = f_θ(z_s) dX/ds`, any ODE solver (torchdiffeq) | `∫ f (dX/dv) dv` (Eq. 7), Euler solver (step size not reported) | same |
| Readout | linear map of `z_{t_n}` (or of the whole path) → class logits | `h_hat(u) = g_1(z(u))` at every knot → reconstruction of `h_t` (Eq. 4, 10) | changed: path readout into the observation space |
| Loss | cross-entropy on a sequence-level label | `E_{τ∼D_succ} Σ_t ‖h_t − h_hat(u_t)‖²` on successful trajectories only (Eq. 5) | changed: one-class reconstruction, no label |
| Gate | — | `dX̃/du = q(dX/du; θ_q) ⊙ dX/du`, `q: R^65 → [0,1]^65`, 4-layer MLP of width 12 (Eq. 8-9) | new |
| Decoding | argmax of logits | per-step score `e_t = ‖h'_t − h_hat(u_t)‖²` (Eq. 10); top-k (k=3) or conformal threshold at the (1−α)-quantile of step scores on held-out successes, α=0.2 (Eq. 11-13) | new (standard one-class decoding) |
| Domain shift | — | PCA fitted in-domain; CORAL to align second-order statistics of OOD embeddings (App. F) | new |
| Theory | universal approximation via signatures; strictly subsumes `dz/ds = h(z, X_s)` models; non-causal spline noted as a limitation for online use | none; cites NCDE for "irregularly structured trajectories" | — |

So the user's reading is right at the level of the model: Eq. (4)-(7) of OAT are Eq. (1)-(3) of Kidger et al. with the observation renamed, and the implementation (cubic spline control path, time channel, MLP vector field, ODE-solver reduction) is the standard `torchcde` recipe. OAT's own contributions are the one-class reconstruction objective, the gate on `dX/du`, the conformal decoder and the domain-alignment plumbing. The PDF's "Code & Dataset:" line is empty and no repository was found on 2026-10-30, so whether the implementation literally calls `torchcde` cannot be checked.

## 2. Where the transfer is weakest

**The irregular-sampling motivation does not survive the change of time axis.** NCDEs earn their place on series with missing or unevenly spaced observations (CharacterTrajectories with 30-70% of points dropped, PhysioNet sepsis). After `u_t = t/T`, every trajectory is a *regular* grid of spacing `1/T`; the only "irregularity" left is that `T` differs between trajectories, which is ordinary variable length and is handled by any RNN. The paper's phrase "irregularly sampled observations of an underlying continuous latent path" (Sec. 4.1) is therefore a re-description, not a property of the data.

**[derivation, corrected 2026-10-30 after `lit_A`] Even on the knot grid, OAT is non-causal, and the cubic spline is what makes it so.** An Euler step of size `1/T` on `dz/du = f(z) dX/du` uses the spline derivative at the left knot:
`z_t = z_{t−1} + (1/T) · f(u_{t−1}, z_{t−1}) · X'(u_{t−1})`.
For *linear* interpolation `X'(u_{t−1})·(1/T) = (Δh_t, 1/T)` exactly and the model collapses to a multiplicative-residual RNN driven by the embedding increment (Neural Rough DEs, Morrill et al. ICML 2021, §3.2, make the same observation). For the *natural cubic spline* OAT uses, `X'(u_{t−1})` is a weighted combination of neighbouring knots; on a uniform grid the weights are approximately −0.80, 0, +0.80, −0.22, +0.06 on `h_{t−2}, h_{t−1}, h_t, h_{t+1}, h_{t+2}` (numerically checked by `lit_A` with scipy's natural `CubicSpline`): a centred difference with no weight on the current knot and look-ahead of two steps. Consequences: (i) `h_hat(u_t)` depends on `h_{t+1}, h_{t+2}`, so an error at step `k` leaks into the scores of steps `k−1` and `k−2`, and OAT is an offline scorer that cannot serve as the prefix monitor of Proposal 2 (U3); Kidger et al. name the non-causality of natural cubic splines as a limitation, and Morrill, Kidger, Yang & Lyons 2021 (arXiv:2106.11028, TMLR 2022 "On the choice of interpolation scheme for Neural CDEs") fix it with rectilinear / Hermite-with-backward-differences interpolation, implemented in `torchcde`; (ii) the time channel `u = t/T` is itself non-causal, since `T` is unknown until the run ends; (iii) because `dX/du ≈ T·Δh`, the gate's input scales with trajectory length, so a Who&When Hand-Crafted run (median 32.5 steps) presents the gate with derivatives about 4.6× larger than the 7-step MCP-Atlas training runs for the same embedding change. The solver step size is not reported (Table 6); with sub-steps between knots the look-ahead only grows.

**[derivation] The reconstruction target is inside the control path.** `X(u_t)` contains `h_t`, and `z(u_t)` integrates `dX` up to `u_t`, so in the continuous limit the map `f ≡ [I_{64} 0]` (pass the embedding increment through, ignore the time channel) yields `z(u) = z(0) + X(u) − X(0)` and, with `g_1 = g_2 = I`, reconstructs every `h_t` exactly for *any* trajectory, failed or not. Kidger et al. list "a CDE can represent the identity between paths" as the reason NCDEs strictly subsume the `h(z, X_s)` family (their Theorem C.1); in a one-class reconstruction objective the same property is a degenerate global optimum with zero anomaly score everywhere. On the knot grid with the cubic-spline derivative the identity is only approximate (Euler error and the centred-difference weights), but the point stands: OAT's scores are informative only because the optimiser (300 epochs, lr 4e-5, weight decay, a 64-wide MLP, early stopping on a validation split) stays away from that solution, and nothing in the paper controls how far. The RNN ablation (Fig. 5) predicts `h_t` from `h_Q, h_1, ..., h_{t−1}` and has no such leak, which makes the CDE-vs-RNN comparison a comparison of two different tasks (reconstruct-with-access vs predict-without-access), not of two dynamics models.

**[derivation] The gate is the detector, the CDE is the carrier.** Writing `d_t = X'(u_{t−1})/T` for the (centred-difference) increment the solver sees, the gated model is `z_t = z_{t−1} + f(z_{t−1}) · (q(T·d_t) ⊙ d_t)`. When `q` suppresses an increment that is "atypically large or directionally unfamiliar" (Sec. 4.1), `z` cannot follow the jump in `h`, `h_hat(u_t)` lags, and `e_t` is large. The gate is thus a learned typicality function of the embedding increment, and the reconstruction error is one way of reading it out. This matches the ablations: the gate accounts for the whole OOD gain (+0.172 AUROC on Who&When, −0.028 in-domain), and ungated OAT's Who&When AUROC (≈0.59) equals the position-only baseline (0.60, `notes/fa_ideas_review.md`). Two further consequences: once the gate has suppressed a jump the latent state cannot catch up, so scores should stay high *after* the first gated step rather than spike (consistent with App. H, where OAT behaves like a detector of the first visible symptom, `t_obs`); and the gate receives no training signal on derivatives it never saw, so the +0.172 OOD gain has no stated mechanism. A direct test is cheap: score steps by `‖(1 − q(T·d_t)) ⊙ d_t‖` alone, no CDE.

**The one-class premise is the real design decision.** Successful trajectories are selected by the outcome label, after which the failures are discarded (brief, Sec. 4). The time-series fields that OAT borrows from have three decades of results on exactly this trade-off (semi-supervised anomaly detection with a few labelled anomalies beats one-class; range-based metrics; random-position baselines), and none of it is used. The companion files `lit_B_tsad.md` and `lit_D_survival_weak_supervision.md` collect it.

## 3. What OAT's own future-work list points to, and where it already exists

OAT's Appendix A names three extensions: exact likelihood via continuous normalising flows (score = NLL instead of reconstruction error), attention-based control paths, and Neural SDEs for stochasticity. All three exist in the NCDE lineage (FFJORD / CNFs; ANCDE / EXIT / learnable-path NCDEs; latent SDEs and Neural SDEs as GANs), and TE-CDE (Seedat, Imrie, Bellot, Qian & van der Schaar, ICML 2022, arXiv:2206.08311) already uses the same NCDE machinery to estimate *counterfactual* outcomes under time-varying treatments, which is the surrogate `f(τ_{<k}, s_k, δ) ≈ p_δ` of Proposal 1 (T1). See `lit_A_nde_signatures.md` and `lit_F_temporal_attribution_credit.md`.

## 4. The recipe, abstracted

OAT = (step representation) + (a sequence model of *normal* behaviour) + (a per-step deviation score) + (a decoder with a false-alarm guarantee). Every slot has a time-series literature:

| Slot | OAT's choice | The literature that owns the slot |
|---|---|---|
| representation | LLM hidden state, PCA 64, `u = t/T` | step-type / mark vocabularies (event sequences, process mining), proxy-LM surprisal, observation features |
| normal model | NCDE reconstruction on successes | one-class TSAD (Deep SVDD, USAD, Anomaly Transformer), next-event / TPP intensity, HMM / switching LDS, process-model conformance, signature conformance |
| deviation score | `‖h_t − h_hat(u_t)‖²` | reconstruction / forecast residual, NLL, CUSUM statistic, alignment cost, intensity surprisal, hazard |
| decoder | top-k, conformal quantile on successes | range-based / affiliation metrics, quickest-detection thresholds with ARL, early-classification stopping rules |
| supervision | successes only (uses `S`, then drops it) | semi-supervised AD, PU / MIL localisation from sequence labels, discrete-time survival on all outcomes, RUDDER return decomposition |

The survey files `lit_A` to `lit_G` fill these slots and map each candidate to the datasets of the report.
