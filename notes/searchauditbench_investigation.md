# SearchAuditBench as an anchor: what is different, what is exploitable, what a model could describe

Date: 2026-10-30. Sources: the paper (arXiv 2608.05212v1, read in full incl. appendices; detailed notes in the session scratchpad `paper_notes.md`), the released code (`data/searchauditbench/repo_SearchAuditor/`), and the 1,243 trajectories (`data/searchauditbench/extracted/benchmark_data_1243.jsonl`). All numbers marked [DATA] were computed here; [PAPER] are the authors'. Analysis scripts: scratchpad `analysis/{extract_ts,aggregate,dynamics,localize,compare_ww_te}.py`.

## 1. What is different from Who&When / TraceElephant

| | Who&When | TraceElephant | SearchAuditBench |
|---|---|---|---|
| system | 2 MAS (Magentic-One, CaptainAgent), output-only logs | 3 systems, full LLM-call traces | **one** ReAct scaffold, 8 backbones, 2 tools (`search`, `visit`) |
| step unit | one message (incl. orchestrator bookkeeping) | one LLM call | one *decision* = reasoning + tool call (+ its return) |
| who | agent name (exact match) | agent name | no "who": single agent, blame is a step + mechanism |
| label | earliest decisive step | earliest decisive step | earliest step where a named **mechanism** is *established*, + tolerance span, + 6-way root cause, + repair directive + rubrics |
| median length | 10 msgs (32.5 HC) | 21 calls | 20 decisions / 43 msgs; p90 81 decisions; **log-normal** (log-sd 1.06) |
| text per trajectory | 28k chars | 15k output chars | 123k chars median; **~75% of tokens are tool returns** [PAPER 49.1k of 65.1k] |
| gold answer visible in trajectory [DATA] | HC 26%, AG 12% | 11-13% | **35.6%** (54% for Entity-Relation Misbinding, 47% Candidate Mismanagement, 24% Search Coverage Gap); 31.6% visible in an observation *before or at* the critical step |
| wrong answer named before the critical step [DATA] | n/a | n/a | 50% (74% for Constraint Neglect) |
| steps after label | HC 18.5, AG 5 (median) | 6-13 | median 6, mean 16 decisions; 45% of tool calls and 47% of tokens come after k* [PAPER] |
| label provenance | 3 annotators, consensus | 3 annotators, alpha 0.64 | **1 annotator per case**, Claude-Opus-4.6 pre-screen with the gold answer, no IAA |
| exclusions | none stated | none | D4 environment-caused and **D5 multi-cause** failures removed: single-k* is by construction |

Three things make this dataset structurally unlike the MAS benchmarks:

1. **Homogeneous, machine-readable action space.** Every decision is `search(list of queries)` or `visit(urls, goal)` or `answer`. Search returns are 10-snippet lists; visit returns are *goal-conditioned LLM summaries* ("Evidence in page: … conclusion: the entry does not mention …"). The agent's own reasoning enumerates candidates and ticks constraints. Who&When/TraceElephant have none of this regularity; their difficulty is heterogeneity (82 expert names, orchestrator bookkeeping). Here the difficulty is *length and evidence selection inside one regular grammar*.
2. **The label is a commitment, not a first appearance.** The guideline says to trace backward from the answer, then forward to "the first harmful commitment"; unhelpful exploration is explicitly not critical. [DATA] the wrong candidate is already named before k* in 50% of cases, and the gold answer has already been retrieved before k* in 32%. In Who&When the label is more often the slip itself (a WebSurfer search that returned nothing).
3. **Failure is mostly a selection failure, not an acquisition failure.** 77% of root causes are processing errors [PAPER]; coverage failures (SCG) are the minority and sit early (median position 0.40) while Constraint Neglect sits late (0.84) and Unsupported Answer at the end (1.0). This gives a *root cause -> position* coupling that none of the MAS datasets has.

What the taxonomy is *not*: it is not a MAST-style behaviour taxonomy and not an agent-module taxonomy (AgentErrorBench). It is a taxonomy over **the epistemic state of the decisive evidence** at k*: never sought / found-but-unverified / surfaced-but-mismanaged / contradicted-but-accepted / bound-to-wrong-entity / no evidence at all. That is a state variable, which is why it is amenable to modelling (section 4).

## 2. What the paper's method exploits, and what it leaves on the table

SearchAuditor (3 parallel audits -> LLM adjudicator -> repair writer) builds only three intermediates: a per-query **constraint table** (supported/violated/unverified with evidence indices), a **forward timeline scan** (healthy/suspicious/decisive per turn, last-healthy index), and a **vote/cluster summary**; plus an "assistant outline" that is just the action skeleton (queries, URLs, goals per step). Removing the forward timeline audit hurts most (-4.0 CS-Strict, -7.0 FPS, n=300). It never builds a candidate ledger, never links facts to sources, never uses the visit-summary verdicts, and never uses the agent/benchmark position prior. Best result: CS-Strict 44.9 / CS-Loose 58.7 / RC-Acc 54.8 / FPS 32.3 (GPT-5.5). RC-Acc gains over All-at-Once are only ~3 points; no method exceeds 55%. The repo prompts also contain calibration text absent from the paper ("Constraint Neglect is systematically over-used").

## 3. Exploitable structure [DATA]

- **Candidate ledger is extractable without an LLM.** Visit `goal` strings name the candidate and list the constraints being checked; reasoning uses "another possible X:", "strong candidate", checkmark lists. The predicted entity appears in reasoning/queries in 99% of cases; after first mention, P(mentioned at t+1 | at t) = 0.60 vs 0.09 otherwise (an absorbing-ish state), yet 46% of trajectories show a >=3-step gap, i.e. candidate switching happens and is visible.
- **Visit summaries are cheap verdict oracles.** They state found / not mentioned / inaccessible per goal. "Negative verdict followed by continued pursuit of the same candidate" is the literal definition of Constraint Neglect and Unverified Source Reliance (46% of labels together with CM). At the CN critical step the agent's own text contains 4.1 check/confirm markers per step vs 0.96 elsewhere and the highest hedge density (8.5 vs 6.4 per 1k chars): the agent *writes down the mismatch and then rationalises it*.
- **Gold-in-hand test.** String-matching the retrieved text against candidates separates coverage from selection failures: gold visible in an observation in 24% of SCG vs 47-54% of CM/ERM. For an auditor without the gold answer the same test applied to *every* candidate the agent named gives, for free, "which candidates were ever evidenced, by snippet or by visited page" (the code's declared/observed grade).
- **Span geometry.** Spans are 1-3 decisions (304/430/323) and bracket "step that issued the call -> return -> step that used it". Predicting the deliberation turn right after a decisive return is loose-correct in most cases.
- **Strong priors from length alone.** Per-model median decisions range from 6 (Qwen3.5, Tongyi) to 73 (OpenSeeker); critical relative position from 0.39 (OpenSeeker) to 0.72 (Tongyi); "last step" gets 59% CS-Loose on Tongyi and 7.5% on OpenResearcher. log(D) is the top feature of a learned per-step scorer.
- **Critical steps are long.** Non-final critical steps have median reasoning 1,179-1,203 chars vs ~320 elsewhere (within-trajectory percentile 0.66-0.74). But hedge *density* is barely different (5.9 vs 5.2 per 1k chars): they are long deliberation turns, not anomalous in style.

What surface features *cannot* do: a gradient-boosted per-step scorer on ~30 structural features (position, lengths, novelty, revisits, failure flags, pred/gold mentions), leave-one-model-out, reaches CS-Strict 18.0 / CS-Loose 30.1, which equals the "last decision" rule (16.9 / 31.1) and is far below GPT-5.5 All-at-Once (39.0 / 51.9). Heuristics: first mention of the predicted answer 19.4 / 29.8; argmax reasoning length 13.4 / 22.1; max-novelty-drop split 11.8 / 22.5; argmax hedging 12.6 / 21.1. The localisation signal is semantic (which constraint was relaxed), so any model needs the content, but the content is regular enough that a ledger, not a frontier LLM reading 100k tokens, is the natural representation.

## 4. Systemic dynamics a mathematical model could describe [DATA]

1. **Exploration decays as a power law, not a change point.** Mean query novelty (fraction of query tokens never used before) falls with decision rank t as 0.44 * t^-0.31 (R^2 0.80 in log-log; exponential fit worse, 0.69). Cumulative new information therefore grows ~ t^0.69: sublinear, so long runs spend most steps re-querying. Revisits of already-opened URLs triple from the first to the last decile; quoted-phrase (verification-style) queries rise from 19% to 40%. **But the true critical step does not split the series better than a random split** (after-minus-before novelty contrast -0.19 at k* vs -0.24 at a random point; same for revisits, new domains, #queries). The regime change is global drift (explore -> verify), not an event at k*.
2. **Commitment is an absorbing state.** Markov persistence of the predicted candidate (0.60 vs 0.09) and the fact that 45% of calls happen after k* without repair [PAPER] describe a two-state process: *open* (candidate set being revised) and *locked* (confirmation search around one candidate). k* is, for CM/CN/USR, the *entry into the locked state despite contrary evidence*; the fix window is short (spans of 1-3 decisions). This is exactly the lock-in `k_lock` of Proposal 2 / Failure-as-a-Process, observed here in a domain where the lock-in is lexically visible (the candidate name enters the queries: pred-in-query rate doubles after k*).
3. **Coverage vs selection decomposition.** P(fail) = P(decisive evidence never retrieved) + P(retrieved, not selected). The SCG share is anti-correlated with the search budget (r = -0.88 across agents [PAPER]) while CM+CN rise with it: scaling search converts coverage failures into selection failures but does not remove them. A model of answer-in-hand probability as a function of budget t (saturating, ~ coverage curve) times a selection-error rate that *does not* decay with t would reproduce this.
4. **Length is log-normal** (empirical vs fitted quantiles: p10 5/5.2, p50 20/20.3, p90 81/79.5), consistent with multiplicative continuation ("one more verification") rather than a fixed budget; the heavy tail is what makes long-context auditing expensive.
5. **Tool failures are bursty and model-specific, but not the cause.** 60% of trajectories contain a hard tool failure (empty results, unreachable page, "[Visit] No resolvable URL": 602 calls with an empty URL list, 8.8% of OpenResearcher's decisions); P(fail_{t+1} | fail_t) = 0.27 vs 0.10. Only 7.2% of critical decisions coincide with a failing observation, because D4 removed environment-caused failures. So failures are nuisance structure to model (bursts) but not a localisation signal.

## 5. Implications for algorithm design on this dataset

- Represent a trajectory as a **candidate-by-constraint ledger over time** (candidate c, constraint j, status in {unsought, snippet-only, visited-supported, visited-contradicted, agent-overruled}, step index). Root cause then becomes a deterministic function of the ledger state at commitment, which is where current methods are weakest (RC-Acc <= 55%).
- Localise k* as the **first overrule event**: the first step at which the eventually-committed candidate is retained after a contradicted or unverified status on a hard constraint (CN/USR/CM), or the first narrowing that drops the set containing the never-evidenced path (SCG). This matches the annotation guideline's "earliest establishment", and the span geometry means scoring either the issuing or the interpreting step is loose-correct.
- Use the per-agent length prior explicitly; the input does not hide the generating model's style.
- Treat the global explore->verify drift as a nuisance baseline (power-law novelty), and look for *deviations from the agent's own ledger*, not deviations from population dynamics. This is the opposite of the OAT-style "atypicality" detector of Proposal 2: here the failing steps are typical in form.

## 6. Caveats

Single annotator per case with an LLM pre-screen that saw the gold answer; multi-cause and environment-caused failures were filtered out; no train/test split; no license. The "gold in observation" and "predicted entity" measures above are string matches with a normalised predicted-entity heuristic (short/bold/labelled forms, 1,089 of 1,243 usable) and gold strings of 2-60 chars (1,094 usable); treat them as lower bounds.
