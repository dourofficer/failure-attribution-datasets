\begin{multicols}{2}
\paragraph{Description} MP-Bench argues that failure attribution is \emph{non-deterministic}: a failed MAS log usually admits several plausible root causes, so a benchmark should store a \emph{set} of expert perspectives rather than one gold (agent, step). It re-annotates 289 existing, naturally failed logs from 121 MAS configurations (no injection):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{manual} (169 logs, hand-crafted MAS): 57 Who\&When Hand-Crafted Magentic-One logs plus 112 Magentic-One GAIA-validation console logs (86 level-2, 26 level-3) from the MAST failure-taxonomy corpus.
\item \textbf{automatic} (120 logs, algorithm-generated MAS): the Who\&When Algorithm-Generated CaptainAgent logs.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Annotation}: three experts recruited on Upwork after a two-stage screening labelled \emph{every} log independently, with no guiding perspective: per step a binary failure-inducing flag, a free-text \code{fail\_reason}, a counterfactual \code{ideal\_action} and a \code{fail\_category} from a 9-class list (Planning, Verification, Reasoning, Contextual Understanding, Tool Usage, Tool/Agent Invocation, Reasoning--Action Mismatch, Resource Access, System Error---not defined in the paper). $\approx$31 min per manual and 14 min per automatic log, $\approx$346 expert-hours. \textbf{Agreement} is low by design: of all flagged steps 16.2\% are flagged by all three annotators, 27.8\% by two, 56.1\% by one (paper); pairwise disagreement reaches $\approx$60\%. Each step gets a \emph{consensus rate} (fraction of annotators flagging it), and GPT-5.1 consolidates the annotators' reasons and ideal actions per step.

\paragraph{Metrics and baselines} The attributor outputs a \emph{ranked list} of failure steps with reasons (three seeds, consolidated). \emph{Attribution}: nDCG@5 (and nDCG@all) of that ranking against the consensus ranking; \emph{reasoning}: GPT-5.1 judge scores (1--10) for fail reason, ideal action and overall agreement with the consolidated annotator rationale. \paragraph{Schema} \code{MP-Bench/<annotator 1--3>/<manual|automatic>/<n>.json} (867 files = 3$\times$289): \code{log\_source} (GitHub URL of the Who\&When JSON or the MAST \code{console\_log.txt}) and \code{annotation}, a possibly empty list of \{\code{step}, \code{fail\_annotation} (``1''), \code{fail\_category}, \code{fail\_reason}, \code{ideal\_action}\}; \code{step} is a 0-based index (stored as a string) into the step list built by the official \code{build\_annotated\_from\_log\_source.py}: Who\&When \code{history} entries, or MAST console turns split on \code{---------- <Agent> ----------} delimiters (question from \code{prompt.txt}, answer from \code{expected\_answer.txt}). The normalised form is \{\code{question}, \code{ground\_truth}, \code{is\_correct}, \code{system\_prompt}, \code{history}:[\{\code{content}, \code{role}, \code{step}, + the four label fields\}]\}. Our copy keeps the fetched logs under \code{data/mp\_bench/sources/<sha1(url)[:16]>/} with an \code{index.json} map.
\end{multicols}

\paragraph{Example trajectory} \code{manual/18.json} (MAST Magentic-One GAIA level-2 log; 9 steps, 3 agents, 13.7\,k characters). The first Bing search already shows ``beta-geometric'' in a snippet; the WebSurfer's click on a paper silently fails, it then summarises ``Markov models'' and the orchestrator answers \emph{Markov Model} (ground truth \emph{beta geometric}). Five steps are flagged in total: 5, 7 and 8 by all three annotators, 3 only by annotator 3, 6 only by annotator 2---the renderer marks all five; the annotation box lists the per-annotator (step, annotator, category) labels with their reasons (truncated) and the per-step votes, and its ``faulty agent'' row names only the agent of the first flagged step (steps 6 and 8 blame the orchestrator).

\paragraph{Notes} (i) No single gold label: each log has three independent annotation sets, annotators flag several steps each (mean 5 per annotator-log, union mean 9, max 78), and the task is ranking/set prediction, not ``find the decisive step''. The ``earliest majority-flagged step'' position (0.40) is our derived quantity for comparability, not an official label. (ii) Categories are per flag and per annotator---the same step often receives different categories (step 5 of the example: Tool Usage / Reasoning--Action Mismatch / Tool/Agent Invocation); \code{ideal\_action} is unique to this dataset. (iii) Pointer-only release: trajectories must be fetched from the Who\&When and MAST repos, and step indices depend on the official parser (we re-implemented it rather than executing it); MAST logs name the orchestrator \code{MagenticOneOrchestrator} and start with the raw \code{user} prompt, Who\&When HC logs use \code{Orchestrator (thought)} / \code{Orchestrator (-> X)}. (iv) Counts differ across sources (README 289 / 121 MAS; eval README 169 + 126; released files 169 + 120; leaderboard 157 + 120)---treat 289 (169 / 120) as canonical. (v) Overlap: 177 of the 289 logs are Who\&When logs re-annotated, a direct test of label reliability (the three annotators frequently disagree with each other and with Who\&When's single decisive step); the other 112 are output-only Magentic-One console logs from MAST. Automatic (5--10 step) logs are much easier (nDCG $\approx$0.7--0.8) than hand-crafted ones ($\approx$0.4). (vi) Non-commercial licence; the consolidation and judge steps of the evaluation need paid API models.
